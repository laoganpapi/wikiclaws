# Acts (c): Conditions of Life — Evidentiary Record

*Sub-Agent 4 output for* Application of the Genocide Convention to the Israel–Palestine Conflict: An Evidentiary Review (as of May 2026). This document records documented conditions of life in Gaza and the West Bank from October 2023 through May 2026. It does not assess intent ("calculated"); that is Agent 6's lane. It does not draw legal conclusions; that is Agent 9's lane.

## 1. Food security — IPC reports

The IPC published a series of acute food insecurity analyses for the Gaza Strip following October 2023. The sequence below is the formal classification record, with exact language retained.

### IPC and Famine Review Committee findings, December 2023 – May 2026

| 18 Mar 2024 | 15 Feb – 15 Jul 2024 | "Famine is imminent" in North Gaza and Gaza Governorate (Phase 5 classified with reasonable evidence, 70 percent in Catastrophe in northern governorates); 1.11 million projected in Catastrophe Strip-wide in most-likely scenario | (IPC, "Gaza Strip: Famine is imminent as 1.1 million people, half of Gaza, experience catastrophic food insecurity," 18 Mar 2024) |

The August 2025 confirmation was the first formal IPC Phase 5 (Famine) determination for any part of Gaza. The Famine Review Committee was convened twice during the conflict: in March 2024 (assessed imminent Famine in the north) and in June 2024 (assessed available evidence did not show Famine was occurring at that moment, while affirming high risk). FEWS NET, in parallel analyses from May 2024 onward, took a more aggressive view, concluding that Famine thresholds in mortality and malnutrition were more likely to have been crossed in the north than the FRC's June 2024 review allowed (FEWS NET, "Gaza Strip Targeted Analysis," May–Aug 2024). The disagreement turned on data quality: the FRC noted that the May 2024 malnutrition sample was biased toward children already receiving therapeutic feeding, and that the pilot mortality survey methods had undergone limited validation.

### Acute malnutrition

UNICEF MUAC screening identified rising acute malnutrition through mid-2025 followed by post-ceasefire decline. Children under 5 diagnosed with acute malnutrition: about 6,500 in June 2025, around 5,000 in the first two weeks of July, 14,363 in August 2025, 11,746 in September 2025, and 9,300 in October 2025 (UNICEF, "Malnutrition persists as winter sets in"). The IPC projected that through June 2026, at least 132,000 children under 5 would suffer acute malnutrition, roughly double the May 2025 estimate (IPC, 22 Aug 2025). UNICEF reported that 57,973 children aged 6–59 months had received Small Quantity Lipid-based Nutritional Supplements by late 2025 (UNICEF Press Release, 2025).

## 2. Aid access — quantitative record

Pre-October 2023 baseline: roughly 500 trucks of commercial and humanitarian goods per day entered Gaza (OCHA, "Gaza crossings" dashboard, pre-Oct 2023 series).

The COGAT and OCHA figures diverge throughout the conflict because they count different things at different points (COGAT typically counts trucks crossing into the Gaza side of Israeli-controlled inspection points; OCHA typically counts pallets or trucks actually collected and distributed by UN partners inside Gaza).

The UN benchmark for adequate supply was 600 trucks per day (OCHA repeated statements through 2024–25). Israeli officials have argued the UN undercounts on the collection side (the Ynetnews story citing COGAT claiming nearly 6,000 trucks not counted by the UN reflects the structural counting difference; both figures are in the record).

### Crossings

Rafah was closed by Israel after its takeover of the Rafah border on 7 May 2024 and remained largely closed for humanitarian use through May 2026 (OCHA, Crossings data). Kerem Shalom and Erez (Zikim, when used) operated intermittently subject to Israeli inspection regimes. The 2 March 2025 closure of all crossings produced an 11-week total blockade until the introduction of the Gaza Humanitarian Foundation (GHF) distribution mechanism in late May 2025.

### Gaza Humanitarian Foundation mechanism, May – October 2025

From 27 May 2025, distribution shifted from a UN-led model with roughly 400 distribution points to four militarized GHF sites in southern Gaza. OHCHR recorded at least 859 Palestinians killed and several thousand injured while attempting to reach or near GHF sites between 27 May and 31 July 2025 (UN OHCHR, cited in HRW, "Gaza: Israeli Killings of Palestinians Seeking Food Are War Crimes," 1 Aug 2025; Amnesty, "GAZA: Starvation or Gunfire," Jul 2025).

### Israeli humanitarian-access measures in the record

Through the period, Israel undertook (a) opening of the Zikim crossing in the north in mid-2024, (b) air drops and a US-built temporary pier operating intermittently in May–July 2024, (c) tactical humanitarian pauses for the polio vaccination rounds (Sep 2024, Oct–Nov 2024, Feb 2025), (d) coordinated repairs to the Mekorot water lines, and (e) Phase 1 ceasefire compliance from 19 Jan to 1 Mar 2025 (multiple OCHA situation reports; WHO press releases on polio cooperation, Sep 2024, Feb 2025).

WHO Health Cluster tracked hospital functionality across the period. The Strip had 36 hospitals operational pre-conflict.

### Attacks on healthcare

By January 2026, WHO's Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care had verified over 930 incidents since October 2023, with hundreds of health worker deaths (WHO, Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care; UN experts statement on Kamal Adwan, Dec 2024). WHO reported over 1,000 health workers killed by early Dec 2024 (WHO Sitreps).

### Named hospital timelines

- **Al-Shifa** (Gaza City): Subject to two large-scale Israeli operations (Nov 2023 and Mar 2024). After the second, 14-day operation in March 2024, WHO's mission of 6 April 2024 described it as an "empty shell," fully non-functional (WHO, "Six months of war leave Al-Shifa hospital in ruins," 6 Apr 2024). Partial outpatient services resumed late 2024.
- **Kamal Adwan** (North Gaza): Last major facility in North Gaza. Taken out of service following an Israeli raid on 27 December 2024; laboratory, surgical unit, operating theatre and medical store were burnt and severely damaged. Hospital director Dr Hussam Abu Safiya was detained. WHO verified at least 50 attacks on or near the hospital between October 2024 and the raid (WHO, "Kamal Adwan Hospital out of service," 28 Dec 2024).
- **Nasser Medical Complex** (Khan Younis): Raided in February 2024; partially restored in subsequent months; remained one of the few functioning trauma centres in southern Gaza through 2025.
- **Indonesian Hospital** (North Gaza): Repeatedly attacked Nov 2023; out of service through most of 2024–25.
- **European Gaza Hospital** (Khan Younis): Damaged in May 2025 strikes; ceasefire-era restoration phased ("phase one to restore minimum functionality – two weeks, phase two towards partial restoration," per WHO 60-Day Ceasefire Plan, Oct 2025).
- **Al-Aqsa Martyrs Hospital** (Deir al-Balah): Functioned intermittently throughout, with periods of overwhelming patient load.

### Medical evacuation

WHO reported over 10,700 patients medically evacuated since October 2023, with about 15,600 still needing evacuation as of September 2025 (WHO EMRO, "Medical evacuation of Gaza patients," 10 Sep 2025). Rafah closure in May 2024 forced the use of slower Kerem Shalom and Ramon airport routes.

## 4. Water and sanitation

### Volumes

Pre-conflict domestic water consumption: 80–85 litres per person per day (UNICEF, "Increasing Water Security in Gaza through Seawater Desalination"). The SPHERE minimum standard is 15 L per person per day for survival and 50–100 L for basic needs.

Documented post-October 2023 figures:
- Aug 2024: about half the population had less than 15 L per person per day (UNICEF).
- Jan 2025: one-third of households had less than 15 L per person per day; a quarter had less than 9 L per person per day (UNICEF, Jan 2025 reporting).
- About 1.4 million people barely met the 6 L drinking-water minimum at various points in 2024 (UNICEF; OCHA situation reports).
- Oxfam in July 2024 documented less than 7 percent of pre-conflict water levels reaching Rafah and North Gaza at points of the May–July 2024 Rafah offensive (Oxfam, "Less than seven percent of pre-conflict water levels available to Rafah and North Gaza").

### Infrastructure

The Gaza City desalination plant (10 million L/day capacity) was destroyed. The South Gaza desalination plant in Deir al-Balah was reconnected to power in November 2024 (producing about 18,000 m³/day); its production dropped more than 80 percent to about 3,000 m³/day after Israel cut the power supply on 8 March 2025 (UNICEF, "With a ceasefire in Gaza, UNICEF begins assessing the damage"). Mekorot water lines from Israel were repaired in coordinated operations at multiple points; full restoration was not achieved by May 2026.

### Disease

The Type 2 vaccine-derived poliovirus reappeared in Gaza wastewater in mid-2024 and was detected in a paralysed child in Deir al-Balah in August 2024. Three rounds of mass polio vaccination followed:
- Round 1: 1–12 Sep 2024, 559,161 children, about 95 percent of target (WHO, 4 Sep 2024).
- Round 2: Oct–Nov 2024, 556,774 children, about 94 percent overall; 88 percent in the north, with 7,000–10,000 children in Jabalia, Beit Lahiya and Beit Hanoun unvaccinated (WHO/UNICEF, 6 Nov 2024).
- Round 3: 5-day campaign concluded Feb 2025, nearly 603,000 children reached during Phase 1 ceasefire (WHO, 19 Feb 2025).

Hepatitis A, acute watery diarrhoea, and skin disease outbreaks were widespread; 80 percent of displacement sites had visible rodent and pest presence and 48 percent had widespread skin disease as of early 2026 (UNRWA Situation Report, 2026).

### Human Rights Watch finding on water

HRW's December 2024 report (179 pages) concluded that "Israeli authorities have deliberately inflicted conditions of life calculated to bring about the destruction of part of the population in Gaza by intentionally depriving Palestinian civilians there of adequate access to water" (HRW, "Extermination and Acts of Genocide: Israel Deliberately Depriving Palestinians in Gaza of Water," 19 Dec 2024). HRW documented cut piped water from Israel, restrictions on electricity and fuel that disabled pumps and treatment, destruction of water and sanitation infrastructure (including the razing of solar panels powering four of Gaza's six wastewater treatment plants), and blockage of repair materials. Israeli officials have disputed the report's characterisations. The HRW finding is a documented allegation by a Tier 4 source and is included here as part of the record; it does not control Agent 6's intent analysis or Agent 9's synthesis.

## 5. Fuel and electricity

Israel cut electricity supply to Gaza on 8 October 2023; the sole Gaza Power Plant exhausted fuel on 11 October 2023 (OCHA Sitreps). Pre-conflict, the plant produced about 60 of a possible 120 MW; Israel provided 120 MW via 10 feeder lines; combined supply met about 45 percent of estimated 470 MW peak demand.

Through most of the conflict, mains electricity from Israel was not restored. Hospitals, water utilities, bakeries and sewage treatment depended on generators and on fuel deliveries that COGAT controlled. WHO reported repeated periods of hospitals operating with fewer than five days of fuel reserve; Al-Shifa's pre-shutdown consumption was 615 L/hour against 191,000 L of storage (OCHA Sitreps, 2023–24). The 19 Jan – 1 Mar 2025 ceasefire enabled steady fuel flow that restored partial operations. The 2 March 2025 blockade resumed depletion; the 10 October 2025 ceasefire restored some flow, but a March 2026 WHO update noted fuel rationing across hospitals to prioritise emergency, maternal-neonatal and communicable-disease services (WHO, March 2026 reporting).

## 6. Shelter and displacement

OCHA estimates of displacement, repeatedly updated, recorded:
- January 2024: about 1.9 million displaced, about 85 percent of the pre-war population (OCHA).
- Mid-2024 onward: roughly 1.9 million, about 90 percent of the population, "many displaced 10 times or more" (OCHA situation reports).
- November 2025: about 1 million of 2.1 million people residing in 862 displacement sites (Site Management Cluster).
- March 2026: at least 1.7 million people in roughly 1,600 verified displacement sites (Site Management Cluster, cited in OCHA Sitrep, 27 Mar 2026).
- April 2026: 67,000 displaced people in 83 UNRWA collective emergency shelters (UNRWA Situation Report).

UNOSAT damage assessments, satellite-based, tracked structural destruction:
- October 2025 assessment: approximately 81 percent of all structures in the Gaza Strip damaged; 123,464 destroyed; 17,116 severely damaged; 33,857 moderately damaged; 23,836 possibly damaged; total 198,273 affected. About 320,622 housing units damaged. Compared with the 8 July 2025 assessment: a 4 percent increase in total affected structures and an 18 percent increase in destroyed structures, with the largest increases in Gaza and North Gaza governorates (UNOSAT, "Gaza Strip Comprehensive Damage Assessment," Oct 2025).

### Evacuation orders

The IDF issued repeated evacuation orders for defined areas, framing them as warnings to civilians ahead of operations (IDF Spokesperson statements). The UN Commission of Inquiry, OHCHR and humanitarian agencies characterised many of these orders as not meeting international humanitarian law requirements for safe passage, time, and destinations (OHCHR statements through 2024–25). The legal characterisation of the orders sits in Agent 7's counter-position analysis and Agent 1's IHL framework.

## 7. Reconstruction and continued destruction (May 2024 – May 2026)

Two ceasefires occurred in the period.

**Phase 1 ceasefire, 19 January 2025 – 1 March 2025.** A 42-day phase under a Qatar/Egypt/US-mediated framework, including hostage and prisoner releases. OCHA recorded a "rapid scale-up" of humanitarian operations: daily fuel entry, large humanitarian inflows, and improved access. Phase 1 expired without agreement on Phase 2; on 2 March 2025 Israel imposed a total blockade on aid and commercial goods entering Gaza. Hostilities resumed on 18 March 2025 with renewed displacement orders.

**Total blockade and renewed offensive, March – October 2025.** All crossings were closed to humanitarian entry from 2 March 2025. The GHF mechanism began operations on 27 May 2025. The IPC famine determination of 22 August 2025 (Gaza Governorate) was issued during this phase.

**October 2025 ceasefire and 60-Day Plan.** A ceasefire took effect on 10 October 2025 under a 20-point framework attributed to the US administration. The UN/WHO 60-Day Ceasefire Humanitarian Response Plan organised rapid scale-up. From 11 October to 10 November 2025, 3,185 UN-partner trucks offloaded carrying 71,510 pallets (OCHA). Reconstruction-needs estimates ranged: the World Bank–UN–EU Interim Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment of February 2025 estimated $53 billion in reconstruction and recovery needs; a later joint assessment estimated about $71.5 billion in total recovery and reconstruction needs. Damages to physical structures alone were about $30 billion, with housing at 53 percent of damages (World Bank, "New Report Assesses Damages, Losses and Needs in Gaza and the West Bank," 18 Feb 2025). Gaza's economy contracted by about 83 percent in 2024.

**Post-ceasefire trend, January – May 2026.** Aid inflows declined 37 percent between months 1–3 and months 4–6 of the ceasefire (OCHA, 1 May 2026). HRW's 19 May 2026 report "Gaza: Israel Curbs Aid, Kills Civilians During Ceasefire" documented continued restrictions and incidents. WHO's March 2026 update found 19 of 36 hospitals partially functional with no fully functional facility, and "no functioning hospitals" in the north. OCHA's 15 May 2026 Situation Report noted "only one in every two aid trucks from Egypt could offload at Israeli-controlled crossings" during the first 11 days of May. The IPC's December 2025 Special Brief recorded that "Famine conditions offset, but situation remains critical," with over 500,000 in Phase 4 and over 100,000 in Phase 5.

## 8. West Bank conditions

OCHA documented sustained settler violence and displacement of Palestinian communities in 2025–26.

- October 2025 was the highest single month for settler attacks since OCHA began tracking in 2006, with over 260 attacks producing casualties or property damage (an average of eight per day) (OCHA, West Bank Humanitarian Situation Updates).
- Through 2025, OCHA documented over 1,700 settler attacks producing casualties or property damage, in more than 270 communities, concentrated in Ramallah, Nablus and Hebron governorates.
- Masafer Yatta (South Hebron Hills): incidents rose from about 1.5 per month in 2021–22 to nearly 5 per month over 2023–25; nearly 6 per month in Q1 2025 (OCHA).
- Jordan Valley: communities accounted for about one-third of settler-displacement events in 2025; B'Tselem documented the construction of a separation fence designed to cut off the northern Jordan Valley (B'Tselem, "Destruction and displacement in the West Bank," 2025).
- Demolitions: B'Tselem recorded 677 administrative demolitions in the West Bank and East Jerusalem from January through early July 2025.
- Forced displacement: B'Tselem reported that as of 16 March 2026, 59 Palestinian communities (more than 4,003 people) in Areas C and B had been forcibly displaced since 7 October 2023, with 507 additional residents partially displaced from 16 other communities.
- Movement restrictions: OCHA documented over 900 movement obstacles across the West Bank by late 2024, with road closures, gates and checkpoints expanded through 2025–26.
- Specific infrastructure incidents: on 11 February 2026, settlers cut the water network supplying the Palestinian community of Jinba in Masafer Yatta (OCHA West Bank Sitrep).

Economic conditions deteriorated alongside. The World Bank reported sharp contraction in the West Bank economy in 2024–25 driven by withheld Palestinian Authority clearance revenues, revoked work permits for Palestinian workers in Israel, and movement restrictions (World Bank, "Impacts of the Conflict in the Middle East on the Palestinian Economy," 2025).
