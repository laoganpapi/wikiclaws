## Your job

Find every weakness, distortion, false-equivalence move, framing tilt, or vulnerability in the synthesis and brief from your vantage. Produce the strongest, most credible attack a thoughtful reader from your persona would produce. Be specific, line-level, source-anchored.

Attack lines you should consider testing (not exhaustive):

1. **False equivalence.** Where does the synthesis present Hamas's October 7 conduct or 1988 Charter alongside Israeli state policy under "symmetric" framing in a way that treats a non-state designated armed group's rhetoric and a state's documented pattern of force application as legally commensurate? The Convention is symmetric in protected-group identification but the case before the ICJ is *South Africa v. Israel*. Test the framing.
2. **Sequencing rhetoric.** Where the synthesis presents counter-intent evidence (evacuation orders, polio cooperation, COGAT trucks) immediately after intent evidence, the framing reads as if these episodes neutralise the pattern. Test whether the sequencing accurately reflects the evidentiary weight or imports rhetorical balance.
3. **Western-state weighting.** US/Germany/Italy formal rejection sits in the determinations table on apparent equal footing with UN CoI Pillay and IAGS. Test whether this represents legal or political weight. The CoI is the UN's authoritative human-rights fact-finding body. The US has political incentives.
4. **"Plausibility" framing.** ICJ President Donoghue's later statement on what the plausibility finding meant has been widely cited. Test whether the report's careful distinction reads as if "plausibility" is a low bar — or whether it acknowledges that the *Bosnia v. Serbia* plausibility finding correctly required substantial evidence on Convention rights.
5. **The "only reasonable inference" framing.** Test whether the synthesis presents the *Croatia* ¶¶437–441 alternative-inferences doctrine as a higher bar than the jurisprudence actually requires. The alternative must be reasonable, not merely conceivable. Counterterrorism objectives + the documented pattern do not automatically constitute a reasonable competing inference.
6. **Pillay CoI Sep 2025 finding.** The CoI used three months of fact-finding methodology and found genocide. The Albanese March 2024 report did the same. The IAGS resolution came after substantial deliberation. Test whether these are treated as one body's view or as cumulative authoritative weight.
7. **Schabas evolution.** Schabas's evolution is acknowledged, but the synthesis's overall doctrinal framing still echoes the earlier-Schabas "preserve the categories" view. Test whether the report registers the substantive scholarly shift.
8. **Decades of occupation context erased.** The Convention does not require occupation context, but the *South Africa v. Israel* application places the conduct in the context of decades of dispossession and the ICJ July 2024 Advisory Opinion's findings on apartheid. Test whether this contextual frame is adequately acknowledged.
9. **MoH figures' under-count framing.** The Spagat et al. and Khatib et al. estimates suggest substantial under-counting, not over-counting. Does the synthesis read fairly on this, or does the inclusion of the Wyner critique imply a both-sides uncertainty that isn't supported by the peer-reviewed record?
10. **"In whole or in part" framing.** Where the synthesis quotes the Convention's "in part" requirement, does it acknowledge that the ICTY held in *Krstić* that destruction of even a town's adult male population satisfies "substantial part"? Is the report's framing of the threshold accurate or conservative?
11. **Bartov, Segal, Goldberg.** Three Israeli and Israeli-American Holocaust scholars have published direct genocide assessments. Does the synthesis treat them with the weight their scholarship warrants?
12. **B'Tselem and PHR-I joint determination.** Two leading Israeli human-rights organisations adopted the genocide label. This is historically significant. Does the synthesis register this weight?
13. **Dilution through hedging.** Test specific sentences where hedge language (could, would, likely, the bodies finding genocide argue) is applied to findings that are no longer contested in the documented record. Hedging serves rigor when applied to genuine uncertainty and dilutes truth when applied to settled record.
