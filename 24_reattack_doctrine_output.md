## 3. Inadequate Doctrinal Corrections

### 3.1. New §2 tracks paragraph: complicity track is correctly stated, with one phrasing nit

The new tri-track paragraph at synthesis §2 (line 41) reads:

> "The third is the obligation not to be complicit under Article III(e), which the Court applied under a knowledge standard rather than a specific-intent standard (¶¶419–424); 'at the least, the organs of the accomplice State knew' of the principal's genocidal intent (¶422)."

This is doctrinally accurate. The verbatim quote at *Bosnia* ¶422 is reproduced. The knowledge-vs-specific-intent contrast is the doctrinally salient point and is the correct characterisation of the ¶421 holding.

**Nit (not severity-rising):** the synthesis does not register that the *Bosnia* Court was careful to characterise Article III(e) Convention complicity as "similar to" but not identical with the ARSIWA Article 16 standard of aid or assistance (*Bosnia* ¶420). The paragraph leaves the impression that Article III(e) and ARSIWA 16 are interchangeable. A two-clause addition would close this, but the omission does not generate a doctrinal error visible to the reader because the operative point (knowledge, not specific intent) is correctly stated. I do not require remediation; for a third edition I would suggest the clarification.

The brief's §2 single-sentence mirror reads:

> "complicity under Article III(e), applied on a knowledge standard rather than specific intent (*Bosnia* ¶¶419–424)."

This captures the distinction adequately within the brief's space discipline. Verified.

### 3.2. M10 purpose-vs-knowledge: characterisation of counterterrorism framing as "motive-style"

The new §2 paragraph on intent at synthesis line 31 closes with:

> "The counterterrorism framing in Israel's pleadings is a motive-style argument on the Schabas reading; whether it is therefore irrelevant under the Convention or directly responsive is the live doctrinal question."

This is a doctrinally defensible characterisation. The intent-motive distinction is established at Schabas 2009 pp. 244–256 and at *Akayesu* ¶¶517–522 (treating motive as legally distinct from intent). The synthesis is correct that motive-as-irrelevant-to-intent is a Convention orthodoxy that Schabas himself has articulated. The claim that the counterterrorism framing is a motive-style argument on the Schabas reading is, however, a step of legal characterisation that goes beyond pure doctrine into application; the synthesis presents it as such and notes the question is "live," which is fair. The phrasing "on the Schabas reading" rather than "in the framework Schabas articulates" leaves the impression that there is a specific Schabas-authored Gaza-applied position, when what is actually in print is Schabas's general motive-vs-intent doctrine plus separate Schabas commentary calling *South Africa v. Israel* the strongest genocide case before the Court. This is not a doctrinal error; it is a citation-anchoring nit. The substance is sound.

### 3.3. M9 Krstić substantial-part: applied four criteria are not fully matched to Krstić's text

The new §2 paragraph at line 35 applies four criteria to Gaza:

> "Applied to Gaza: numeric (approximately 2.3 million Palestinians in Gaza of roughly 14 million worldwide); geographic (defined territorial concentration); prominence (the densest concentration of the Palestinian national group); emblematic (Gaza's destruction received across the Arab world and within the Palestinian community as a strike at national identity). *Bosnia* ¶¶198–201 on the Bosnian Muslims of Eastern Bosnia is the closest analog."

The *Krstić* Appeals ¶¶8–14 framework names: numeric (¶12), prominence within the group (¶12), the area of the perpetrators' activity / geographic (¶13), and the emblematic significance of the targeted part (¶12). The synthesis's labels match. The application is doctrinally sound at the level of identifying the four heads. The "prominence" category in *Krstić* refers to leadership / military or social significance ("prominent within the group, such as its leadership") rather than density of the group's population, so the synthesis's gloss of "prominence (the densest concentration)" is partly idiosyncratic: density is closer to a geographic point than to the *Krstić* "leadership / prominent members" point. The "emblematic" criterion is correctly identified.

This is a defensible but imprecise application of *Krstić* ¶12. The doctrinal claim that Palestinians in Gaza form a "substantial part" survives whether prominence is read as leadership or as density; the brief's mirror is correctly minimalist. **Recommended micro-edit (not severity-rising):** in §2 distinguish density (a geographic-heading point) from prominence (the *Krstić* leadership-or-significance point); the present synthesis runs them together. The merits adjudication will not be embarrassed by the present language but the *Krstić* labelling is sharper than the synthesis renders it.

## 4. New Doctrinal Claims Introduced

### 4.1. ICC contextual element doctrinal split (synthesis §2, line 29)

**Claim:** Article 6 of the ICC Elements attaches a contextual element requiring "conduct ... in the context of a manifest pattern of similar conduct directed against that group or ... conduct that could itself effect such destruction." Schabas reads it as a procedural threshold; Ambos as substantive; Mettraux as an objective dimension in tension with subjective *dolus specialis*.

**Authority:** ICC Elements of Crimes, Article 6 common chapeau element 4; Schabas, *International Criminal Court Commentary*, 2nd ed. 2016, pp. 130–135; Ambos, *Treatise*, vol. II, 2014.

**Assessment:** Accurate. The verbatim Elements text is reproduced. The three-camp doctrinal split is the right map of the literature.

### 4.2. Purpose-vs-knowledge intent debate (synthesis §2, line 31)

**Claim:** The ICJ formulation tracks a purpose-based conception. A knowledge-based reading (Greenawalt 1999) and a two-level reading (Ambos 2009, intent at leadership / knowledge at lower levels) remain in the conversation; the Court has applied the purpose-based formulation.

**Authority:** Greenawalt, *Columbia L. Rev.* 99 (1999); Ambos, *IRRC* 91:876 (2009).

**Assessment:** Accurate. The Ambos two-level position is correctly stated.

### 4.3. Subjective-vs-objective protected-group (synthesis §2, line 35)

**Claim:** The *Akayesu* objective approach (¶¶510–516, "stable and permanent") and the *Krstić*/*Rutaganda* trend toward subjective or mixed identification remain contested; ¶45 of the 26 January 2024 Order does not adjudicate.

**Authority:** *Akayesu* ¶¶510–516; *Krstić* Trial ¶¶555–557; *Rutaganda* ¶¶55–58.

**Assessment:** Accurate.

### 4.4. Article III(c) incitement as inchoate offence (synthesis §2, line 43)

**Claim:** Article III(c) makes "direct and public incitement to commit genocide" a separately punishable inchoate offence, requiring no underlying commission; *Akayesu* ¶¶551–562 and *Nahimana et al.* Trial ¶¶1010–1029 / Appeals ¶¶692–715 are cited.

**Authority:** Convention Article III(c); *Akayesu*; *Nahimana et al.*

**Assessment:** Accurate. The doctrinal point that incitement is independent of commission is the controlling holding of *Nahimana* Appeals ¶¶677–679 and ¶¶715–722; the synthesis's framing is sound. The CoI's Article III(c) identification of named officials is correctly characterised as a finding by a UN expert body, not a court.

### 4.5. "Calculated to bring about" doctrinal split (synthesis §4(c), line 108)

**Claim:** Whether "calculated to bring about" is a separate objective requirement (Schabas 2009 pp. 184–197; *Krstić* Trial ¶¶517–518) or absorbs into the chapeau *dolus specialis* (the calculation goes to *mens rea*) is a doctrinal disagreement (*Karadžić* Trial ¶¶544–548).

**Authority:** as cited.

**Assessment:** Accurate. The two-position map is fair. *Stakić* Trial ¶517 could be added as a parallel ICTY anchor but its omission is not a defect.

### 4.6. Article II(e) as narrow exception (synthesis §4(e), line 146)

**Claim:** Article II(e) is the only enumerated act that targets group identity directly rather than physical or biological existence; cultural destruction otherwise figures only as evidentiary of intent under Article II's chapeau (*Krstić*, Appeals Judgment, ¶25).

**Authority:** *Krstić* Appeals ¶25.

**Assessment:** Accurate. *Krstić* Appeals ¶25 is the correct anchor for the "cultural destruction as evidence not as act" doctrine.

### 4.7. ICC PTC charging discretion as "affirmative" determination (synthesis §4(c), line 126; brief §4(c))

**Claim:** "The Chamber's decision not to charge under Article 6 is an affirmative charging-discretion determination by the body authorised to bring criminal charges (ICC PTC I, 21 November 2024)."

**Authority:** Rome Statute Articles 7, 8, 58; ICC Press Release 21 November 2024.

**Assessment:** This is the new framing introduced to address my H4 / triage H14. It is doctrinally defensible. The Prosecutor under Article 58(1) applies for warrants on those charges the Prosecutor selects; the Chamber confirms or denies "reasonable grounds to believe" on the charges before it; the Chamber may, under Article 58(7) and Regulation 55 procedures, decline to issue a warrant on a charge if the standard is not met, but does not generally re-write the Prosecutor's charge sheet sua sponte at the warrant stage. The synthesis's "affirmative charging-discretion determination" therefore correctly describes the institutional posture as one of authorised non-charging by the body authorised to bring charges, rather than as a judicial finding of insufficiency. The brief's mirror at §4(c) is correctly minimalist: "It is not a partial Article II(c) finding." Verified.

### 4.8. Albanese Article III(e) framing on knowledge standard (synthesis §6, line 236)

**Claim:** "The 63-complicit-states framing engages the Article III(e) complicity track distinguished in §2. Row (a) institutionally; the complicity finding rests on the *Bosnia* ¶¶419–424 knowledge standard rather than *dolus specialis*."

**Authority:** *Bosnia* ¶¶419–424.

**Assessment:** Accurate. The doctrinal architecture of complicity-under-knowledge-standard is correctly applied to the Albanese framing, which was the operative remediation of my Round 1 H2.

### 4.9. Brief §2 mirror: tri-track sentence

**Claim:** "The Convention engages three analytically distinct State-responsibility tracks: commission under Article I read with Article II; the duty to prevent under Article I, a 'distinct obligation' arising at 'serious risk' on a 'due diligence' standard (*Bosnia v. Serbia*, 26 February 2007, ¶¶427, 430, 431); and complicity under Article III(e), applied on a knowledge standard rather than specific intent (*Bosnia* ¶¶419–424)."

**Assessment:** This single sentence captures the distinction adequately. The ¶427 "distinct obligation" quote, the ¶431 "serious risk" trigger, the ¶430 "due diligence" standard, and the ¶¶419–424 complicity locus are all named. For a brief operating under space discipline, this is the doctrinally minimal version that does the work. Verified.
