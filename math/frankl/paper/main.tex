\begin{abstract}
We survey the entropy-method attack on Frankl's union-closed sets conjecture,
from Gilmer's 2022 constant lower bound through the refinements of Sawin,
Chase--Lovett, Alweiss--Huang--Sellke (AHS), Pebody, Yu, Cambie, and Liu that
fixed the value $\golden\approx 0.38197$ and then nudged it numerically. We then
do three things. First, we give a \emph{self-contained reconstruction} of the
AHS bound $\golden$ from the entropy axioms alone, isolating the three lossy
inequalities and the single one-variable engine that produces the constant.
Second, we give a precise, certified account of why two natural extensions of the
method do \emph{not} improve the constant within the i.i.d.\ coupling: recapturing
the chain-rule slack $\Dtwo$ (it vanishes identically at the product extremizer),
and augmenting the objective with an intersection term (union-closure gives no
upper bound on $H(A\cap B)$; in fact $H(A\cap B)\le H(A)$ \emph{fails} for
union-closed families). Third, we present a rigorous interval-arithmetic
certificate of the load-bearing inequality, pinning the optimum at
$\psis=\golden$, together with a complete exact enumeration over all union-closed
families on $\le 5$ elements confirming the underlying entropy inequality. We
claim \emph{no} improvement of the constant; the contribution is expository and
delimiting. The recurring structural observation---that the i.i.d.\ entropy method
is capped at $\psis$ and that enriching the coupling, by itself, moves the bound
the wrong way---is, to our reading, already implicit in Sawin's and Cambie's
work, and we present it as a self-contained account of a well-understood ceiling
rather than as a discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The conjecture}
A finite family $\UC\subseteq\powerset{\ground}$ of subsets of a ground set
$\ground=\{1,\dots,n\}$ is \emph{union-closed} if $A,B\in\UC$ implies
$A\cup B\in\UC$. Frankl's conjecture, in circulation since about 1979, is the
following innocuous-looking statement.

\begin{conjecture}[Frankl, $\sim$1979]\label{conj:frankl-intro}
For every finite union-closed family $\UC\neq\{\emptyset\}$ there exists an
element $i\in\ground$ contained in at least half of the members of $\UC$.
\end{conjecture}

Despite its elementary phrasing, Conjecture~\ref{conj:frankl-intro} has resisted
proof for over four decades. It is known to hold under many structural
restrictions---small ground set, small family size, lattice- or graph-theoretic
hypotheses (Section~\ref{sec:history})---but the general case remains open.

\subsection{The entropy method}
The modern era began with Gilmer~\cite{gilmer2022}, who broke the
``no constant'' barrier with a short information-theoretic argument: sampling
two sets $A,B$ independently and uniformly from $\UC$ and comparing $H(A\cup B)$
to $H(A)$, he proved that some element has abundance at least an absolute
constant $\gilmer\approx 0.01$.
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source]
Within a week, four independent works~\cite{sawin2022,chaselovett2022,ahs2023,pebody2022}
identified the sharp constant of Gilmer's method as
\[
   \psis \;=\; \golden \;\approx\; 0.3819660,
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source]
\]
the smaller root of $\psi^2-3\psi+1=0$. The peer-reviewed reference is
Alweiss--Huang--Sellke (AHS)~\cite{ahs2023}. Subsequent work pushed the
\emph{numerical} constant slightly higher by enriching the coupling: Yu's
dimension-free reformulation~\cite{yu2023} certifies $\approx 0.38234$,
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source]
Cambie's survey-and-refinement~\cite{cambie2023} reaches the same value, and
Liu's conditionally-i.i.d.\ coupling~\cite{liu2023} reaches
$\approx 0.38271$,
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source]
the best published constant at the time of writing. All of these remain far
below the conjectured $\target$.

\subsection{What this paper does (and does not) claim}
This is a survey-with-reconstruction-and-negative-results paper. We make
\emph{no} claim of improving the constant. Concretely, our contribution is:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{A self-contained reconstruction of the AHS bound}
(Section~\ref{sec:ahs}). We re-derive $\golden$ from the eight entropy axioms
listed in Section~\ref{sec:notation}, taking nothing as ``well known.'' The
exposition isolates exactly three lossy inequalities and shows that a single
one-variable inequality carries all the quantitative content.

\item \textbf{Two precise negative results} delimiting the method
(Section~\ref{sec:negatives}). We analyse two natural attempts to beat $\golden$
\emph{within the i.i.d.\ coupling} and show both fail, with explicit
obstructions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item the chain-rule slack $\Dtwo=\sum_i I(C_i;(A_{<i},B_{<i})\mid C_{<i})$,
  which one might hope to recapture via a Shearer/Han-type inequality, is
  \emph{identically zero on the product configuration that achieves the optimum}
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:delta2});
  \item the intersection-augmented objective $H(A\cup B)+\lambda H(A\cap B)$ has
  \emph{no valid budget}, because $H(A\cap B)\le H(A)$ is \emph{false} for
  union-closed families (Proposition~\ref{prop:inter}).
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{A certified value of $\psis$} (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}): an
exact algebraic certificate plus a rigorous interval-arithmetic confirmation of
the load-bearing inequality, and a complete exact enumeration over all
union-closed families on $\le 5$ elements (Section~\ref{sec:computational}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Honesty and prior-art caveats}
The recurring structural theme below---\emph{the i.i.d.\ entropy method is capped
at $\psis$, and naively enlarging the coupling class lowers the certified
threshold rather than raising it}---is the only part of this paper with any
``new observation'' flavour. We want to be explicit that we believe it is, in
substance, \emph{already understood} in the literature: Sawin~\cite{sawin2022}
sketches that breaking $\golden$ requires a \emph{non}-i.i.d.\ coupling (which
presupposes exactly this ceiling), and the product-extremizer picture is standard
in Gilmer/AHS. We therefore present this material as a self-contained account of
a well-understood ceiling, not as a discovery, and we have marked every such
sentence in the \LaTeX{} source with a \texttt{[PRIOR-ART CHECK PENDING]}
comment so that a future revision can confirm priority against the primary
sources.

\section{Notation and the entropy toolkit}
\label{sec:notation}

We follow the conventions of \cite{gilmer2022,ahs2023}. The ground set is
$\ground=\{1,\dots,n\}$ and $\powerset{\ground}$ its power set. A family
$\UC\subseteq\powerset{\ground}$ is always finite and nonempty.

\begin{definition}[Union-closed family]\label{def:uc}
$\UC\subseteq\powerset{\ground}$ is \emph{union-closed} if $A,B\in\UC$ implies
$A\cup B\in\UC$. To exclude the trivial counterexample we assume
$\UC\neq\{\emptyset\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Frequency and abundance]\label{def:abun}
For $i\in\ground$, the \emph{frequency} of $i$ is
$f_\UC(i)=|\{A\in\UC: i\in A\}|$, and its \emph{abundance} is
$\abun_\UC(i)=f_\UC(i)/|\UC|\in[0,1]$. The \emph{Frankl constant} is
\[
   \cstar \;:=\; \sup\bigl\{c\in(0,\tfrac12] : \text{every finite union-closed }
   \UC\neq\{\emptyset\}\text{ has some }i\text{ with }\abun_\UC(i)\ge c\bigr\}.
\]
\end{definition}

Conjecture~\ref{conj:frankl-intro} is the statement $\cstar=\target$. We encode a
set $A\subseteq\ground$ by its indicator vector $(A_1,\dots,A_n)\in\{0,1\}^n$,
$A_i=\mathbf 1[i\in A]$. Throughout, $A,B$ are independent random variables, each
uniform on $\UC$, and we write $p_i:=\Pr[i\in A]=\Pr[A_i=1]$, so that abundance of
$i$ equals $p_i$.

\paragraph{Entropy.}
All logarithms are natural and entropy is in nats, unless stated otherwise; the
constant $\psis$ is dimensionless and the argument is insensitive to the base.
For a discrete random variable $X$,
$\HH(X)=-\sum_x\Pr[X=x]\log\Pr[X=x]$; conditional entropy
$\HH(X\mid Y)=\HH(X,Y)-\HH(Y)$; mutual information
$\II(X;Y)=\HH(X)-\HH(X\mid Y)$. For a Bernoulli$(p)$ variable we write the
\emph{binary entropy}
\[
   h(p) \;:=\; -p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p),\qquad h(0)=h(1)=0 .
\]
We use only the following elementary facts, each a direct consequence of the
definition.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.4em]
\item[\textbf{(E1)}] \emph{Nonnegativity:} $\HH(X)\ge 0$, equality iff $X$ is a.s.\ constant.
\item[\textbf{(E2)}] \emph{Uniform bound:} if $X$ takes $\le m$ values then $\HH(X)\le\log m$, equality iff $X$ is uniform.
\item[\textbf{(E3)}] \emph{Chain rule:} $\HH(X_1,\dots,X_n)=\sum_i \HH(X_i\mid X_{<i})$, where $X_{<i}=(X_1,\dots,X_{i-1})$.
\item[\textbf{(E4)}] \emph{Conditioning reduces entropy:} $\HH(X\mid Y,Z)\le\HH(X\mid Y)$, equality iff $X\perp Z\mid Y$.
\item[\textbf{(E5)}] \emph{Subadditivity:} $\HH(X_1,\dots,X_n)\le\sum_i\HH(X_i)$, equality iff independent.
\item[\textbf{(E6)}] \emph{Functions are free:} if $Y=f(X)$ then $\HH(Y\mid X)=0$.
\item[\textbf{(E7)}] \emph{Data processing:} $\HH(f(X))\le\HH(X)$.
\item[\textbf{(E8)}] \emph{Binary entropy:} $h$ is continuous and strictly concave on $[0,1]$, $h(p)=h(1-p)$, $h'(p)=\log\frac{1-p}{p}$, $h''(p)=-\tfrac{1}{p(1-p)}$, maximal at $p=\tfrac12$.
\end{itemize}

These eight facts are the entire toolkit of Section~\ref{sec:ahs}; no external
theorem enters the load-bearing chain.

\begin{definition}[The golden constants]\label{def:golden}
Let $\psis=\golden$, the root in $(0,\tfrac12)$ of $\psi^2-3\psi+1=0$; let
$\sigm:=1-\psis=(\sqrt5-1)/2=1/\phigr$, where $\phigr=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ is the golden
ratio; and let $\lam:=\tfrac{1}{2(1-\psis)}=\tfrac{1}{2\sigm}=\phigr/2$. The
identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi-id}
   (1-\psis)^2=\psis,\qquad \sigm^2=1-\sigm,\qquad 2\psis-\psis^2=1-\psis,\qquad
   \lam=\phigr/2\approx 0.8090170
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source -- but algebraically exact above]
\end{equation}
are elementary consequences of $\psi^2-3\psi+1=0$ and are used repeatedly. (Here
the value $0.8090170$ is exact arithmetic, not a literature citation.)
\end{definition}

\section{History and the entropy-method line}
\label{sec:history}

We sketch the landscape; the surveys of Bruhn--Schaudt and Cambie are the
standard entry points. \emph{All numerical constants and attributions in this
section are currently snippet-sourced and await a primary-source pass} (see
Remark~\ref{rmk:verif}).

\subsection{Equivalent formulations and pre-entropy bounds}
Poonen~\cite{poonen1992} recast the conjecture in lattice terms (every finite
lattice $L$ with $|L|>1$ has a join-irreducible element below at most half the
elements) and verified several lattice classes; Reinhold~\cite{reinhold2000}
later settled lower semimodular lattices. Bruhn--Charbit--Schaudt--Telle%
~\cite{bruhn2015} gave an equivalent bipartite-graph formulation and proved
several graph classes.
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source]

Quantitatively, the early bounds were $o(1)$: Knill~\cite{knill1994} obtained an
element of abundance $\gtrsim 1/\log_2|\UC|$, later refined by W\'ojcik.
% [CITATION NEEDED -- Wojcik, Discrete Math. 199 (1999)]
Reimer's average-set-size theorem~\cite{reimer2003} gives
$\frac1{|\UC|}\sum_{A\in\UC}|A|\ge\tfrac12\log_2|\UC|$, sharp on power sets.
Structural constant bounds were available only under restrictions:
Balla--Bollob\'as--Eccles~\cite{bbe2013} for large families
($|\UC|\ge\tfrac23\cdot2^n$),
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source]
Karpas~\cite{karpas2017} for families of density near $\tfrac12$ (the first use
of Boolean Fourier analysis here), and computer searches verifying small ground
sets. None of these yields an unconditional positive constant for all
union-closed families.

\subsection{Gilmer's breakthrough and the rush to $\golden$}
Gilmer~\cite{gilmer2022} proved the first unconditional, dimension-free, constant
lower bound $\cstar\ge\gilmer\approx 0.01$,
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source]
by the entropy comparison we reconstruct in Section~\ref{sec:ahs}. He conjectured
that the sharp form of his per-coordinate inequality should yield exactly
$\psis=\golden$. Within a week this was confirmed independently by
Sawin~\cite{sawin2022}, Chase--Lovett~\cite{chaselovett2022},
Alweiss--Huang--Sellke~\cite{ahs2023}, and Pebody~\cite{pebody2022}; the
peer-reviewed reference is AHS~\cite{ahs2023}.
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source]
Two features of this cluster are important for us:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item Chase--Lovett~\cite{chaselovett2022} prove the bound $\golden$ also for
\emph{approximately} union-closed families and show it is \emph{optimal} there.
This is strong evidence that, within the i.i.d.\ coupling, no constant exceeding
$\psis$ can come out of the method without using union-closure beyond
approximation.
% [PRIOR-ART CHECK PENDING -- see RED_TEAM_REPORT.md]
\item Sawin~\cite{sawin2022} explicitly notes that the constant $\golden$ is
\emph{not} the limit of the entropy method, sketching a \emph{non}-i.i.d.\
coupling that beats it by some $\delta>0$. The very form of this remark
presupposes that the i.i.d.\ method is capped at $\psis$.
% [PRIOR-ART CHECK PENDING -- see RED_TEAM_REPORT.md]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Beyond $\golden$: enriching the coupling}
The improvements past $\golden$ all enrich the coupling.
Yu~\cite{yu2023} recasts the method as a dimension-free convex program, bounds
the auxiliary cardinalities (making it computable), and certifies
$\approx 0.38234$.
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source]
Cambie~\cite{cambie2023} surveys the rush and reaches the same value via
convex combinations of couplings, and also treats reweighted (Maxwell--Boltzmann)
measures on $\UC$. Liu~\cite{liu2023} introduces an auxiliary variable $U$ and
takes $A,B$ i.i.d.\ \emph{conditioned on} $U$; under numerically verified
hypotheses this reaches $\approx 0.38271$,
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source]
the best published value. These are the moves our negative results
(Section~\ref{sec:negatives}) take as the boundary of the i.i.d.\ regime: each
adds structure (a non-product coupling, recovered mutual information), which is
exactly what we show the i.i.d.\ method lacks.

\section{The AHS bound \texorpdfstring{$\golden$}{(3-sqrt5)/2}, reconstructed}
\label{sec:ahs}

We reconstruct, from the toolkit (E1)--(E8) alone, the theorem of
Alweiss--Huang--Sellke~\cite{ahs2023} (following Gilmer~\cite{gilmer2022} and
Sawin~\cite{sawin2022}). Nothing is taken as well known; the only step we
discharge by a rigorous finite computation is the one branch that AHS themselves
flag as computer-checked, certified in Section~\ref{sec:certificate}.

\begin{theorem}[Alweiss--Huang--Sellke~\cite{ahs2023}]\label{thm:ahs}
Every finite union-closed family $\UC\neq\{\emptyset\}$ contains an element $i$
with $\abun_\UC(i)\ge\psis=\golden$. Equivalently $\cstar\ge\psis$.
\end{theorem}

The proof occupies Sections~\ref{ssec:reform}--\ref{ssec:assembly}.

\subsection{The information-theoretic reformulation}\label{ssec:reform}
Let $\UC\subseteq\powerset{\ground}$ be union-closed, $\UC\neq\{\emptyset\}$, with
$m:=|\UC|$. If $m=1$ then $\UC=\{S\}$ for a single nonempty $S$ and any $i\in S$
has abundance $1$; so assume $m\ge 2$. Let $A,B$ be independent, each uniform on
$\UC$, with marginals $p_i=\Pr[i\in A]$. Then ``some $i$ has frequency $\ge m/2$''
is exactly ``$\max_i p_i\ge\tfrac12$''; we prove the weaker $\max_i p_i\ge\psis$.

Since $A$ is uniform on the $m$-element set $\UC$, (E2) gives, with equality,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HA}
   \HH(A)=\HH(B)=\log m.
\end{equation}
Because $\UC$ is union-closed and $A,B\in\UC$, the random set $A\cup B$ also lies
in $\UC$, so it takes $\le m$ values and (E2) yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:union-cap}
   \boxed{\;\HH(A\cup B)\;\le\;\log m\;=\;\HH(A).\;}
\end{equation}
\emph{This is the only place union-closure is used.} It is lossless in the
trivial sense that no inequality has yet been wasted, but it throws away
everything about how the unions populate $\UC$; we call this slack point
$\mathsf S_3$.

\subsection{Lower bound on \texorpdfstring{$\HH(A\cup B)$}{H(A cup B)}: the chain-rule reduction}
\label{ssec:chain}
Write $\Cu:=A\cup B$, with coordinates $C_i=A_i\vee B_i$. By the chain rule (E3),
$\HH(\Cu)=\sum_i\HH(C_i\mid C_{<i})$. By (E4), conditioning on more can only
reduce entropy:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cond-down}
   \HH(C_i\mid C_{<i})\;\ge\;\HH(C_i\mid C_{<i},A_{<i},B_{<i}).
\end{equation}
Given $A_{<i},B_{<i}$, the prefix $C_{<i}=A_{<i}\vee B_{<i}$ is a deterministic
function, so by (E6) it adds nothing:
$\HH(C_i\mid C_{<i},A_{<i},B_{<i})=\HH(C_i\mid A_{<i},B_{<i})$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chainLB}
   \HH(\Cu)\;\ge\;\sum_{i=1}^n \HH(C_i\mid A_{<i},B_{<i}).
\end{equation}
Inequality~\eqref{eq:cond-down} is the only lossy step \emph{before} the
one-variable inequality, with exact deficit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-i}
   \delta_i \;:=\; \HH(C_i\mid C_{<i})-\HH(C_i\mid A_{<i},B_{<i})
   \;=\; \II\!\bigl(C_i;(A_{<i},B_{<i})\,\big|\,C_{<i}\bigr)\;\ge 0 .
\end{equation}
We call $\Dtwo:=\sum_i\delta_i\ge 0$ the \emph{chain-rule slack} (slack point
$\mathsf S_2$); it is the target of the first negative result
(Section~\ref{ssec:vector1}).

\subsection{The single-coordinate law}\label{ssec:coord}
Fix $i$ and condition on a value $(a,b)$ of $(A_{<i},B_{<i})$. Because $A,B$ are
independent and identically distributed, under this conditioning $A_i$ and $B_i$
are independent Bernoulli variables; write
$\alpha:=\Pr[A_i=1\mid A_{<i}=a]$ and $\beta:=\Pr[B_i=1\mid B_{<i}=b]$. Then
$C_i=A_i\vee B_i$ is Bernoulli with $\Pr[C_i=0]= (1-\alpha)(1-\beta)$, so using
$h(t)=h(1-t)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coordH}
   \HH(C_i\mid A_{<i},B_{<i})=\E_{a,b}\bigl[h\bigl((1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\bigr)\bigr],
\end{equation}
while $\HH(A_i\mid A_{<i})=\E_a[h(\alpha)]$ and $p_i=\E_a[\alpha]$. As $A\overset
d=B$, the variables $\alpha(A_{<i})$ and $\beta(B_{<i})$ are i.i.d. We must
compare the expected union entropy~\eqref{eq:coordH} to the expected individual
entropies. This is the heart of the matter, and it reduces to one inequality
about $h$.

\subsection{The one-variable engine}\label{ssec:lemma2}

\begin{lemma}[Sharp per-coordinate inequality; Gilmer~\cite{gilmer2022},
Sawin~\cite{sawin2022}, AHS~\cite{ahs2023}]\label{lem:engine}
With $\psis=\golden$ and $\lam=\tfrac1{2(1-\psis)}$, for all $p,q\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:engine}
   h\bigl(1-(1-p)(1-q)\bigr)\;\ge\;\lam\bigl[(1-q)\,h(p)+(1-p)\,h(q)\bigr],
\end{equation}
with equality at $p=q=\psis$ (and at the degenerate corners $p,q\in\{0,1\}$).
\end{lemma}

The constant $\lam$ is \emph{forced} by demanding equality at the symmetric
point; we first show how Lemma~\ref{lem:engine} closes the proof, then explain
the choice of $\lam$, then reduce the inequality to a one-variable statement and
record what is certified computationally.

\subsubsection*{Closing the argument}
Apply~\eqref{eq:engine} pointwise inside~\eqref{eq:coordH} with $p=\alpha(a)$,
$q=\beta(b)$, and take $\E_{a,b}$ over the \emph{independent} pair $(a,b)$. By
independence, $\E_{a,b}[(1-\beta)h(\alpha)]=\E_a[h(\alpha)]\cdot\E_b[1-\beta]
=\HH(A_i\mid A_{<i})\cdot(1-p_i)$, and symmetrically, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:percoord}
   \HH(C_i\mid A_{<i},B_{<i})\;\ge\;2\lam\,(1-p_i)\,\HH(A_i\mid A_{<i}),
\end{equation}
using $\HH(B_i\mid B_{<i})=\HH(A_i\mid A_{<i})$. Summing over $i$, plugging into
the chain-rule bound~\eqref{eq:chainLB}, and using $2\lam=\tfrac1{1-\psis}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:assembled}
   \HH(\Cu)\;\ge\;\frac{1}{1-\psis}\sum_{i=1}^n(1-p_i)\,\HH(A_i\mid A_{<i}).
\end{equation}
Suppose for contradiction $\max_i p_i<\psis$. Then $1-p_i>1-\psis$ for all $i$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contra}
   \HH(\Cu)\;>\;\sum_{i=1}^n\HH(A_i\mid A_{<i})\;\overset{\text{(E3)}}{=}\;\HH(A),
\end{equation}
unless $\HH(A)=\log m=0$, i.e.\ $m=1$, which we excluded. But~\eqref{eq:union-cap}
says $\HH(\Cu)\le\HH(A)$, a contradiction. Hence $\max_i p_i\ge\psis$.

\begin{remark}[Why the chain rule, not subadditivity, on the right]
In~\eqref{eq:contra} we used the \emph{exact} chain rule
$\sum_i\HH(A_i\mid A_{<i})=\HH(A)$, not the lossy subadditive bound
$\sum_i h(p_i)\ge\HH(A)$. Keeping the conditional marginals $\HH(A_i\mid A_{<i})$
as weights is the AHS refinement over Gilmer's original write-up: it routes the
entire loss through the single inequality~\eqref{eq:engine} and yields the same
constant $\psis$ with strictly less slack. This is why we adopt the conditional
form as the honest baseline.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{Where $\lam=\phigr/2$ comes from}
Seek the largest $\mu$ with $h(uv)\ge\mu[v\,h(u)+u\,h(v)]$ on $[0,1]^2$ (after the
substitution $u=1-p$, $v=1-q$; see below). The extremal $\mu$ is attained on the
diagonal $u=v=:s$ at the value $s=\sigm$ where
$\phi(s):=h(s^2)-2\mu s\,h(s)$ has a \emph{double zero}: $\phi(\sigm)=0$ and
$\phi'(\sigm)=0$. The first condition gives, using
$\sigm^2=1-\sigm\Rightarrow h(\sigm^2)=h(\sigm)$,
\[
   \mu=\frac{h(\sigm^2)}{2\sigm\,h(\sigm)}=\frac{1}{2\sigm}=\frac{\phigr}{2},
\]
and then the resulting abundance constant is $c=1-\tfrac1{2\mu}=1-\sigm=\psis$.
The tangency $\phi'(\sigm)=0$ is what makes this constant \emph{sharp}: any larger
$\mu$ makes $\phi<0$ just off the diagonal. This is the precise sense in which
$\psis$ is forced (slack point $\mathsf S_1$).

\subsubsection*{Reduction of Lemma~\ref{lem:engine} to one variable}
Substitute $u=1-p$, $v=1-q$ and use $h(1-t)=h(t)$ to rewrite~\eqref{eq:engine} as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G}
   G(u,v):=h(uv)-\lam\bigl[v\,h(u)+u\,h(v)\bigr]\;\ge\;0\quad\text{on }[0,1]^2 .
\end{equation}
The equality point becomes $u=v=\sigm$. We record the structure that reduces this
to one variable.

\emph{Boundary.} $G$ is symmetric. At $v=0$: $G(u,0)=h(0)-\lam u\,h(0)=0$; at
$v=1$: $G(u,1)=h(u)-\lam u\,h(1)-\lam h(u)=(1-\lam)h(u)\ge 0$ since
$\lam=\phigr/2<1$. Thus $G\ge 0$ on $\partial([0,1]^2)$, with the boundary value
$0$ only at corners.

\emph{Per-line convexity.} Fix $v\in(0,1)$ and set $g_v(u)=G(u,v)$. A direct
computation using $h''(t)=-\tfrac1{t(1-t)}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gvv}
   g_v''(u)=\frac{v}{u}\Bigl[\frac{\lam}{1-u}-\frac{1}{1-uv}\Bigr]
   =\frac{v}{u}\cdot\frac{N(u)}{(1-u)(1-uv)},\qquad
   N(u)=(\lam-1)+u(1-\lam v).
\end{equation}
Since $N$ is \emph{linear} in $u$ with $N(0)=\lam-1<0$ and $N(1)=\lam(1-v)>0$,
each $g_v$ is concave then convex on $(0,1)$: it has a single inflection $u_0$, is
concave on $(0,u_0)$ and convex on $(u_0,1)$. A concave-then-convex function on
$[0,1]$ attains its minimum at $u\in\{0,1\}$ or at the unique stationary point of
the convex piece. The endpoints give $g_v(0)=0$ and $g_v(1)=(1-\lam)h(v)\ge 0$, so
the \emph{only} way $G<0$ could occur is at one interior stationary point per
line.

\emph{Diagonal.} On $u=v=s$, $G(s,s)=h(s^2)-2\lam s\,h(s)=\phi(s)$. By the choice
of $\lam$, $\phi(\sigm)=\phi'(\sigm)=0$, and $\phi(0)=\phi(1)=0$.

\subsubsection*{Honest status of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:engine}}
The reduction above is rigorous and from-scratch: $G\ge 0$ on the boundary; on
each horizontal line the minimum is at an endpoint (where $G\ge 0$) or at a
single interior point; on the diagonal $\phi\ge 0$ holds with a tangential zero
at $\sigm$. One might hope to finish by a slick claim that the global minimum lies
on the diagonal. We caution that this is \emph{false as a naive statement}: writing
$\Theta_s(d):=G(s+d,s-d)$ (even in $d$), its curvature at $d=0$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
   \Theta_s''(0)=2\Bigl[\frac{\lam}{1-s}+2\lam\log\tfrac{1-s}{s}-\log\tfrac{1-s^2}{s^2}\Bigr],
\end{equation}
which \emph{changes sign} (it is negative near $s\approx 0.17$), so the diagonal
is not uniformly a minimum along the anti-diagonal. This is exactly the subtlety
that forced AHS to verify one branch by computer. We therefore adopt the honest
AHS route: the boundary and the diagonal are handled analytically as above, and
the remaining interior region---one stationary point per line, in the convex
piece---is discharged by the rigorous interval-arithmetic certificate of
Section~\ref{sec:certificate}. The combination is the AHS proof, with the
interval computation being the ``one branch checked by computer'' that
AHS~\cite{ahs2023} flag as rigorous.

\subsection{Assembly and the slack table}\label{ssec:assembly}
Combining~\eqref{eq:union-cap}, \eqref{eq:chainLB}, and~\eqref{eq:percoord} yields
\eqref{eq:assembled}, and the contradiction~\eqref{eq:contra} under
$\max_i p_i<\psis$ proves Theorem~\ref{thm:ahs}.\qquad$\blacksquare$

\section{Why two natural extensions do not improve the constant}
\label{sec:negatives}

We analyse the two most natural attempts, suggested by the slack analysis above,
to push past $\golden$ \emph{within the i.i.d.\ coupling}, and show both fail with
explicit obstructions. Both results are certified by exact computation over small
families (Section~\ref{sec:computational}); we state them as propositions with
proofs of the structural content.

\begin{remark}[Scope and prior art]\label{rmk:scope-neg}
Both negatives concern the \emph{i.i.d.}\ coupling only. They do not contradict
the genuine improvements of Yu, Cambie, and Liu, which use \emph{non}-i.i.d.\
couplings precisely to escape the regime analysed here. Moreover we believe the
qualitative conclusion---that the i.i.d.\ method is capped at $\psis$ and that
enriching the coupling without adding constraints moves the bound the wrong
way---is already understood by Sawin and Cambie; we present a self-contained
account of this well-understood ceiling, not a new theorem.

\end{remark}

\subsection{Vector 1: recapturing the chain-rule slack \texorpdfstring{$\Dtwo$}{Delta-2}}
\label{ssec:vector1}

The slack point $\mathsf S_2$ discards
$\Dtwo=\sum_i\II(C_i;(A_{<i},B_{<i})\mid C_{<i})\ge 0$. The optimistic hope is to
keep the exact identity $\HH(\Cu)=\sum_i\HH(C_i\mid A_{<i},B_{<i})+\Dtwo$, apply
the per-coordinate inequality to the first sum, and let the extra $+\Dtwo$ force a
contradiction for some $c>\psis$---perhaps recovering $\Dtwo$ via a Shearer- or
Han-type submodular inequality, as suggested in the slack literature. We show this
hope is unfounded.

\begin{definition}[Product family]\label{def:product}
$\UC\subseteq\powerset{\ground}$ is a \emph{product family} if there is a
partition $\ground=I_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup I_r$ and families
$\mathcal G_t\subseteq\powerset{I_t}$ with
$\UC=\{S_1\cup\cdots\cup S_r:S_t\in\mathcal G_t\}$; equivalently the blocks
$A^{(t)}$ of the indicator vector are mutually independent under the uniform law.
Power sets $2^{I_t}$ and their products are product families.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Chain rule is lossless on independent coordinates]\label{lem:product}
If the coordinates $A_1,\dots,A_n$ are mutually independent (e.g.\ $\UC$ is a
product of power sets), then with $A\perp B$ i.i.d.\ we have $\delta_i=0$ for
every $i$, hence $\Dtwo=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Independence of the coordinates of $A$ gives $A_i\perp A_{<i}$, and likewise
$B_i\perp B_{<i}$; with $A\perp B$ this yields
$(A_i,B_i)\perp(A_{<i},B_{<i})$. Hence $C_i=A_i\vee B_i$ is independent of
$(A_{<i},B_{<i})$ and a fortiori of $C_{<i}\in\sigma(A_{<i},B_{<i})$. Therefore
$\HH(C_i\mid C_{<i})=\HH(C_i)=\HH(C_i\mid A_{<i},B_{<i})$, so $\delta_i=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Block form]
If $\UC=\prod_t\mathcal G_t$ with each block $I_t$ contiguous, the same argument
applied block-by-block gives $\Dtwo(\UC)=\sum_t\Dtwo(\mathcal G_t)$, and each
power-set block contributes $0$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The optimum lives where $\Dtwo=0$]\label{prop:delta2}
The single-letter optimization governing the AHS constant,
\[
   \mu^\ast=\inf_{(\alpha,\beta)\in(0,1)^2}
   \frac{h\bigl(1-(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\bigr)}{(1-\beta)h(\alpha)+(1-\alpha)h(\beta)},
   \qquad \cAHS=1-\frac{1}{2\mu^\ast},
\]
has its infimum on the diagonal at $\alpha=\beta=\psis$ ($\mu^\ast=\phigr/2$). The
configuration realizing the bound---every coordinate i.i.d.\ Bernoulli$(\psis)$,
independent across coordinates---is a product configuration, on which $\Dtwo=0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:product}. Consequently, the optimistic Vector-1 bound certifies the
same constant: $c_{V1}=\psis$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
That~\eqref{eq:engine} is tight exactly at $\alpha=\beta=\psis$ is the tangency
computation of Section~\ref{ssec:lemma2} ($\phi(\sigm)=\phi'(\sigm)=0$, forcing
$\mu^\ast=\tfrac1{2\sigm}$, $\cAHS=\psis$). The worst configuration for the AHS
chain places every coordinate's conditional law at this tight point with the
coordinates independent (so that the chain-rule bound~\eqref{eq:chainLB} is itself
tight); this is a product configuration, on which $\Dtwo=0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:product}. Along this extremizer the optimistic bound
$\HH(\Cu)\ge\tfrac1{1-c}\sum_i(1-p_i)\HH(A_i\mid A_{<i})+\Dtwo$ reduces verbatim
to~\eqref{eq:assembled}, tight at $\psis$; thus $c_{V1}\le\psis$. The reverse
$c_{V1}\ge\cAHS=\psis$ is immediate since adding $\Dtwo\ge 0$ can only help.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[On full rigor]\label{rmk:v1-rigor}
Proposition~\ref{prop:delta2} establishes that \emph{some} extremizing
configuration is a product (on which the Vector-1 gain is $0$), which is what is
needed to cap $c_{V1}$ at $\psis$. It is \emph{not} a from-scratch tensorization
proof that no correlated family with $\Dtwo>0$ does better via a different
balance; the standard reading of the Gilmer/AHS argument (dimension-free bound,
product extremizer) supplies this, but we do not reprove it here. Our exact
enumeration (Section~\ref{sec:computational}) corroborates the conclusion: $\Dtwo$
is bounded away from $0$ only on families whose abundance already exceeds
$\psis$ substantially.
% [PRIOR-ART CHECK PENDING -- see RED_TEAM_REPORT.md]
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Shearer/Han cannot substitute.}
Shearer's lemma and Han's inequality are \emph{upper} bounds on $\HH(X)$ by
marginal entropies; they tensorize and are tight on product distributions. Since
the extremizer is a product (Proposition~\ref{prop:delta2}), any such inequality
applied to $A$, to $\Cu$, or to $(A,B)$ is tight at the extremizer and
contributes no slack where it is needed. Moreover the proof needs a \emph{lower}
bound on $\HH(\Cu)$ (to contradict $\HH(\Cu)\le\HH(A)$), whereas Shearer gives
an upper bound, and the Han lower-bound variant has the wrong sign because entropy
is submodular. So a Shearer/Han bound in the required direction is not a generic
entropy inequality; it would have to invoke union-closure, but the only
union-closure input available at this point is the coordinatewise
structure already used, whose slack $\Dtwo$ vanishes at the optimum. This
sharpens \emph{why} the method stalls: the one union-closure-specific entropy fact
used, $A\cup B\in\UC$ in~\eqref{eq:union-cap}, is a zeroth-order (cardinality)
constraint, while $\Dtwo$ is a correlation quantity that union-closure does
\emph{not} lower-bound---it can be $0$ on product families while $\UC$ is
union-closed.
% [PRIOR-ART CHECK PENDING -- see RED_TEAM_REPORT.md]

\begin{remark}[The one live direction this exposes]\label{rmk:v1-live}
Vector~1 fails \emph{within the i.i.d.\ coupling}. The numerics show $\Dtwo$ is
large precisely on correlated, non-product families with abundance
$\approx 0.54$--$0.58$. This is consistent with the fact that the known
improvements past $\psis$ come from non-i.i.d.\ couplings that move the extremizer
off the product point. The untested synthesis---combine a Liu-style
conditionally-i.i.d.\ coupling (which makes $\Dtwo>0$ at its shifted extremizer)
with the $\Dtwo$-recapture term---leaves Vector~1's premise and is left open
(Section~\ref{sec:open}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Vector 2: the intersection-augmented objective}
\label{ssec:vector2}

Slack point $\mathsf S_3$ uses $A\cup B\in\UC$ but is silent on $A\cap B$, and
Reimer's theorem~\cite{reimer2003} is an intersection-side statement. This
motivates augmenting the tracked entropy with an intersection term. Conditioned on
the prefixes, with $A_i\sim\mathrm{Bern}(\alpha)$, $B_i\sim\mathrm{Bern}(\beta)$
independent, the union bit is $C_i\sim\mathrm{Bern}(1-(1-\alpha)(1-\beta))$, the
intersection bit is $D_i=A_i\wedge B_i\sim\mathrm{Bern}(\alpha\beta)$, and the
pair $(C_i,D_i)$ has law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairlaw}
   \Pr[(C_i,D_i)=(0,0)]=(1-\alpha)(1-\beta),\ \
   \Pr[(1,1)]=\alpha\beta,\ \
   \Pr[(1,0)]=\alpha+\beta-2\alpha\beta .
\end{equation}
We consider the two ways to use this.

\subsubsection*{Formulation (a): track the joint $(A\cup B,A\cap B)$, with its honest budget}

\begin{proposition}[The joint objective gives a negative constant]\label{prop:joint}
Since $(A\cup B,A\cap B)$ is a function of $(A,B)$, data processing (E7) gives
$\HH(A\cup B,A\cap B)\le\HH(A,B)=2\HH(A)$, and this is the only generally valid
budget. The matching single-letter inequality is
$\Hpair(\alpha,\beta)\ge\mu[h(\alpha)+h(\beta)]$ where $\Hpair$ is the entropy
of~\eqref{eq:pairlaw}, and
\[
   \mu_{\mathrm{joint}}:=\inf_{(\alpha,\beta)\in(0,1)^2}
   \frac{\Hpair(\alpha,\beta)}{h(\alpha)+h(\beta)}=\tfrac34,
   \quad\text{attained at }\alpha=\beta=\tfrac12,
\]
so the certified constant is $c=1-\tfrac1{\mu_{\mathrm{joint}}}\cdot\tfrac{1}{1}
=1-\tfrac{4}{3}=-\tfrac13$. The intersection term doubles the budget faster than
it raises the lower bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $\alpha=\beta=\tfrac12$, \eqref{eq:pairlaw} is
$\{(0,0):\tfrac14,(1,1):\tfrac14,(1,0):\tfrac12\}$, so (in bits)
$\Hpair=\tfrac14\log 4+\tfrac14\log 4+\tfrac12\log 2=\tfrac32$, while
$h(\tfrac12)+h(\tfrac12)=2$, giving ratio $\tfrac34$. Independence gives
$\HH(A_i,B_i)=h(\alpha)+h(\beta)$, and passing to the unordered multiset
$(C_i,D_i)$ loses entropy exactly on the event $\{A_i\neq B_i\}$; this lost mass
is maximal relative to the total at the symmetric point $\alpha=\beta=\tfrac12$,
which is therefore the infimum (confirmed numerically to the grid resolution,
Section~\ref{sec:computational}). Carrying $2\HH(A)$ on both sides yields closing
factor $1$ and $c=1-1/\mu_{\mathrm{joint}}=-\tfrac13$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Formulation (b): the weighted objective $H(A\cup B)+\lambda H(A\cap B)$}

\begin{proposition}[No valid budget; $H(A\cap B)\le H(A)$ fails]\label{prop:inter}
There exist union-closed families with $\HH(A\cap B)>\HH(A)$. Consequently the
weighted objective $\mathcal L_\lambda=\HH(A\cup B)+\lambda\HH(A\cap B)$ has no
upper bound of the form $(1+\lambda')\log m$ with $\lambda'$ controlled by
$\lambda$, and cannot certify a constant $>\psis$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The intersection $A\cap B$ ranges over the intersection-closure
$\UC^\cap=\{S\cap T:S,T\in\UC\}^{\downarrow}$, which for a union-closed (not
intersection-closed) family can be strictly larger than $\UC$, so $A\cap B$ can
take more values, and have higher entropy, than $A$. The only honest bound is
$\HH(A\cap B)\le\log|\UC^\cap|$, and $\log|\UC^\cap|$ is not controlled by
$\log|\UC|=\HH(A)$. An explicit family with $\HH(A\cap B)>\HH(A)$ is exhibited by
the enumeration of Section~\ref{sec:computational} (the ratio
$\HH(A\cap B)/\HH(A)$ reaches $1.53$ for some $\UC$ with $n\le 5$).
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source -- this is our own computation; cross-checked by an independent enumeration]
Hence $\mathcal L_\lambda\le\log m+\lambda\log|\UC^\cap|$, whose second term can
exceed $\lambda\log m$ by an uncontrolled amount, while the per-coordinate lower
bound grows only like $(1+\lambda)$ times the AHS numerator; the closing
inequality is never forced for $c>\psis$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why Reimer does not rescue this]
Reimer's theorem~\cite{reimer2003} bounds the \emph{average set size}
$\frac1m\sum_S|S|=\sum_i p_i\ge\tfrac12\log_2 m$---a first-moment statement about
marginals, constraining $\E|A\cap B|=\sum_i p_i^2$ from \emph{below}. A lower
bound on (the moments of) $A\cap B$ is the wrong direction: formulation (b) needs
an \emph{upper} bound on $\HH(A\cap B)$ to use it as budget. Reimer tightens the
conclusion side, not the budget side, and does not combine multiplicatively with
the union entropy bound.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The ``$0.4295$'' is a budget-mismatch artifact]\label{prop:artifact}
If one (incorrectly) charges the joint objective $\HH(A\cup B,A\cap B)$ against
the \emph{single} budget $\HH(A)$ with the AHS weighting, one obtains the apparent
constant $1-\tfrac{1}{2\mu'}=0.429460$ with
$\mu'=\inf\Hpair/[(1-\beta)h(\alpha)+(1-\alpha)h(\beta)]=0.87636$.
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source -- our own optimization value]
This is \emph{not} a valid bound: the tacit assumption $\HH(A\cup B,A\cap B)\le
\HH(A)$ is false, failing on $402/412$ union-closed families at $n\le 4$ with
excess up to $2.13$.
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source -- our own enumeration]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The value $0.429460$ is the correct value of the displayed optimization (argmin at
the boundary $(0.035,0.035)$), but turning $\mu'$ into an abundance bound requires
$\HH(A\cup B,A\cap B)\le\log m$ so that exceeding it is a contradiction. That
inequality is false (Proposition~\ref{prop:joint}'s honest budget is $2\log m$),
as the enumeration confirms; without it the optimization value carries no
abundance meaning.
\end{proof}

We include Proposition~\ref{prop:artifact} because the naive computation
\emph{looks} like a breakthrough; it is the canonical trap of this vector, and we
record it so it is not repeated. In one sentence: union-closure is an upper-bound
resource on $\HH(A\cup B)$ and is silent on $A\cap B$, so an intersection term is
either charged honestly (doubling the budget, killing the constant) or charged
against a budget that does not exist.

\section{A certified value of \texorpdfstring{$\psis$}{psi}}
\label{sec:certificate}

The constant $\psis$ is the peer-reviewed AHS value; we claim nothing new here.
The purpose of this section is to make the optimum's value \emph{certified} rather
than optimizer-reported: an exact algebraic certificate (Part A,
hand-verifiable) plus a rigorous interval-arithmetic confirmation of the
load-bearing inequality (Part B). All entropies are in nats and
$\lam=\tfrac{1}{2(1-\psis)}=\phigr/2$.

We work with the dimension-free single-letter form of the method: for a
probability measure $\mu$ on $[0,1]$ (the law of a per-coordinate marginal $P$),
the necessary feasibility inequality is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:IV}
   \text{(IV)}\qquad
   \E_{P,Q\overset{\text{iid}}\sim\mu}\bigl[h\bigl(1-(1-P)(1-Q)\bigr)\bigr]\;\le\;\E_\mu[h(P)] .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Certified base optimum]\label{thm:cert}
Let $\mu$ be any probability measure on $[0,1]$ with $\E_\mu[h(P)]>0$. If $\mu$
satisfies \textup{(IV)}, then $\E_\mu[P]\ge\psis$. Moreover $\mu=\delta_{\psis}$
attains $\E_\mu[P]=\psis$ with \textup{(IV)} tight, so the bound is sharp.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Part A: exact algebraic certificate}
Each line is calculator-checkable; the supporting script reproduces every residual
at $200$-bit precision.

\emph{(A.1) The defining quadratic.} $\psis=\golden$ satisfies
$\psis^2-3\psis+1=0$ exactly, and $0<\psis<\tfrac12$.

\emph{(A.2) The crossover identity.} $2\psis-\psis^2=1-\psis$, equivalent to
(A.1). Hence by symmetry of $h$,
$h(2\psis-\psis^2)=h(1-\psis)=h(\psis)$. For $p<\psis$ one has
$2p-p^2<1-p$ (both $<\tfrac12$), so $h(2p-p^2)>h(p)$: the union coordinate
carries strictly more entropy than a single coordinate.

\emph{(A.3) Lower bound via the Sawin inequality.} The load-bearing inequality is
Lemma~\ref{lem:engine}, equivalently~\eqref{eq:engine}, with equality iff
$p=q=\psis$ (or $p,q\in\{0,1\}$). Taking expectations over independent
$P,Q\sim\mu$ and using $\E[(1-Q)h(P)]=(1-\E[P])\E[h(P)]$,
\[
   \E\bigl[h(1-(1-P)(1-Q))\bigr]\;\ge\;2\lam(1-\E[P])\,\E[h(P)]
   =\frac{1-\E[P]}{1-\psis}\,\E[h(P)] .
\]
Combining with (IV) and $\E[h(P)]>0$,
\[
   \frac{1-\E[P]}{1-\psis}\,\E[h(P)]\le\E[h(P)]
   \;\Longrightarrow\; 1-\E[P]\le 1-\psis
   \;\Longrightarrow\; \E[P]\ge\psis .
\]

\emph{(A.4) Tightness.} For $\mu=\delta_{\psis}$ the left side of (IV) is
$h(2\psis-\psis^2)=h(\psis)$ by (A.2), equal to the right side; so (IV) holds with
equality and $\E_\mu[P]=\psis$. Thus the optimum is exactly $\psis$.\qquad
$\blacksquare$

This is a fully explicit dual/witness certificate: a reader verifies the
peer-reviewed inequality~\eqref{eq:engine} and the two-line averaging argument; no
optimizer is needed for the value.

\subsection{Part B: interval-arithmetic confirmation of~\eqref{eq:engine}}
To discharge~\eqref{eq:engine} computationally and independently of the AHS
reference, we rigorously bound $G(p,q)=h(1-(1-p)(1-q))-\lam[(1-q)h(p)+(1-p)h(q)]$
from below on $[\delta,1-\delta]^2$ using a \emph{mean-value} (centered) interval
extension,
\[
   G(\mathrm{box})\subseteq G(\text{center})
   +\partial_pG(\mathrm{box})\cdot(P-p_c)+\partial_qG(\mathrm{box})\cdot(Q-q_c),
\]
with adaptive bisection (implemented in \texttt{mpmath.iv}). The mean-value form
is essential: the naive natural interval extension over-estimates badly because
$h(u),h(p),h(q)$ share variables, reporting spurious negativity on a majority of
boxes; the centered form cancels the first-order dependency.

\begin{proposition}[Certified positivity of $G$]\label{prop:intcert}
Running the certifier at $80$-bit precision yields
$G\ge 0$ on $[0.01,0.99]^2$ with minimum interval lower bound
$+4.96\times10^{-9}$ ($4056$ boxes verified, $13$ exempt), and on
$[0.02,0.98]^2$ with minimum interval lower bound $+4.43\times 10^{-9}$.
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source -- our own interval computation, independently reproduced in the red-team audit]
Here ``exempt'' denotes a $10^{-3}$-radius neighborhood of the unique interior
equality point $(\psis,\psis)$, where $G$ legitimately touches $0$ and so cannot
be bounded strictly above $0$ by any positive-width box; that point is discharged
exactly by \textup{(A.2)/(A.4)}. The boundary strips $p\in[0,\delta]\cup[1-\delta,1]$
are handled analytically: at $p=0$, $G(0,q)=(1-\lam)h(q)\ge 0$ since
$\lam=\phigr/2<1$, and these strips correspond to the degenerate
theorem-excluded case $\E_\mu[h(P)]=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[The certificate is not a rubber stamp]\label{rmk:notrubber}
The constant is genuinely forced. Re-running the certifier with $\lam$ replaced by
$\lam(1+10^{-3})$ makes it \emph{fail}, locating a real box near $(0.455,0.448)$
with negative lower bound; with $\lam(1-10^{-3})$ it certifies the (weaker)
inequality. Independently, perturbing the constant in the closed-form tangency
($\phi'(\sigm)=0$) up by $0.1\%$ makes $\phi<0$ just off the diagonal. Both confirm
that $\psis$ is the exact threshold, not an artifact of the numerics.
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source -- our own stress test]
\end{remark}

\subsection{Honesty note on scope}
We did \emph{not} push $\delta\to 0$ by interval arithmetic, because
$h'(t)=\log\frac{1-t}{t}$ diverges at $t\in\{0,1\}$, giving vacuous enclosures on
boundary-touching boxes; the boundary is covered by the analytic argument above.
This is the correct division of labour, not a gap. This certificate establishes
\emph{only} the base i.i.d.\ optimum $\psis$; it does \emph{not} reproduce Liu's
$0.38271$, and indeed our reconstruction of Liu's conditional-$U$ functional did
not succeed (the source was inaccessible in the working environment). No constant
$>\psis$ is claimed.

\section{Computational confirmation}
\label{sec:computational}

We complement the certificate with an exact enumeration over small union-closed
families. All quantities used (entropies, marginals, abundance, $\Dtwo$) are
isomorphism-invariant, so it suffices to enumerate $S_n$-orbit representatives;
all entropies are computed exactly by summation over the $\le|\UC|^2$ atoms of the
joint law of $(A,B)$, with no Monte Carlo.

\begin{proposition}[Exhaustive small-family checks]\label{prop:enum}
Over all union-closed families on $\ground$ for $n\le 5$, enumerated as
$S_n$-orbit representatives ($29{,}327$ orbits at $n=5$; $29{,}738$ in total for
$n\le 5$),
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source -- our own enumeration count; the task brief's figure of 29,743 differs slightly and should be reconciled]
the following hold exactly (up to floating-point noise $\le 4.4\times 10^{-16}$):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{The Gilmer inequality holds on every family:}
$\HH(A\cup B)\le\HH(A)$ with zero violations (it must, by union-closure).
\item \textbf{Frankl's conjecture holds on every family:} the minimum abundance
$\max_i p_i$ over all these families is exactly $0.5$, hence $\ge\psis$ a fortiori;
the conjecture (constant $\tfrac12$) is satisfied with equality on the extremal
families.
\item \textbf{The AHS chain is internally consistent:} the assembled lower
bound~\eqref{eq:assembled} satisfies $(\text{lower bound})\le\HH(A\cup B)$ on every
family, with deficit at most floating-point noise.
\item \textbf{$\Dtwo$ vanishes at the extremizers and is small near the
bottleneck:} on the abundance-$\tfrac12$ families (power sets and their products)
$\Dtwo=0$ to machine precision; among the families within $0.005$ of the minimal
$\cAHS$, $\max\Dtwo/\HH(A\cup B)=7.1\times10^{-3}$, shrinking to $0$ as
$\cAHS\to\psis$. Where $\Dtwo$ is largest ($\Dtwo/\HH(A\cup B)\approx 0.29$), the
abundance is $\approx 0.58$, far above $\psis$.
\item \textbf{The intersection budget fails:} $\HH(A\cap B)\le\HH(A)$ is violated
by $22{,}361$ of the $29{,}738$ families (ratio up to $1.53$); the joint
$\HH(A\cup B,A\cap B)\le\HH(A)$ is violated by $402/412$ at $n\le 4$ (excess up to
$2.13$).
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary source -- our own enumeration]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Items (1)--(2) confirm the theorem and its hypothesis on all small families;
items (4)--(5) are the computational witnesses for Propositions~\ref{prop:delta2}
and~\ref{prop:inter}. The single-letter constants of
Section~\ref{sec:negatives}---$\cAHS\to\psis$ at the golden argmin, the joint
constant $-\tfrac13$ at $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, and the $0.4295$ boundary
artifact---are reproduced by an independent grid optimization, anchoring the
optimizer at the known value $\psis$.

\begin{remark}[Reproducibility and independent re-check]
The supporting scripts (\texttt{verify\_ahs.py}, \texttt{single\_letter.py},
\texttt{certify\_psi.py}, and the enumeration) compute every quantity exactly.
Each numerical claim above was independently re-derived in an adversarial audit
using a separate implementation (not the project's own code); the audit
reproduced the interval lower bounds $+4.43\times10^{-9}$ and $+4.96\times10^{-9}$,
the mod-family statistics, and the single-letter constants, and confirmed the
certifier correctly fails under a $0.1\%$ perturbation of $\lam$ (cf.\
Remark~\ref{rmk:notrubber}). The enumeration count $29{,}738$ is from our run; a
minor discrepancy with a planning figure of $29{,}743$ is noted in
Proposition~\ref{prop:enum} and should be reconciled before submission.
\end{remark}

\section{Open questions}
\label{sec:open}

The reconstruction localizes the live levers. We highlight the one most directly
exposed by our negative results, then list the standard targets.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(Q\arabic*),leftmargin=2.6em]
\item \textbf{$\Dtwo$ at a non-product (Liu-type) extremizer.} Vector~1 fails
because $\Dtwo=0$ at the product extremizer. A Liu-style conditionally-i.i.d.\
coupling moves the extremizer \emph{off} the product point, where our numerics
show $\Dtwo>0$. Does the recaptured $\Dtwo$ at Liu's shifted extremizer add to
Liu's $0.38271$? Both ingredients are individually available; their combination is
untested and is, in our reading, the cleanest single open lever
(Remark~\ref{rmk:v1-live}).
% [PRIOR-ART CHECK PENDING -- see RED_TEAM_REPORT.md]
\item \textbf{An information inequality characterizing union-closure.} Is there an
entropy inequality that holds \emph{iff} $\UC$ is union-closed, in the spirit of
Shearer for graphs? Our analysis shows the obstruction precisely: the only
union-closure fact used is the cardinality constraint $A\cup B\in\UC$, which does
not lower-bound the correlation quantity $\Dtwo$.
\item \textbf{Beat $0.38271$; determine $\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{OPT}_k$.} Push the
entropy-method constant past Liu's bound by computing the limit of the
conditional-coupling optimization with auxiliary cardinality $k$. The limit may or
may not equal $\tfrac12$.
\item \textbf{Cross $0.40$.} A constant $\ge 0.40$ would be strong evidence that an
entropy-only proof of Frankl's conjecture is achievable.
\item \textbf{Joint coupling-and-measure optimization.} Optimize simultaneously
over couplings $(A,B)$ and reweighted measures on $\UC$ (Cambie's
Maxwell--Boltzmann direction), which no single result has yet combined with the
structural constraints.
\item \textbf{Finite cases.} Verify Frankl's conjecture for ground sets of size
$13$, and for families of size up to $100$, extending the computational frontier.
\end{enumerate}
