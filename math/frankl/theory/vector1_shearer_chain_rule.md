# Vector 1 — Shearer/submodular recapture of the chain-rule slack: precise account of why it fails

**Author:** Alex Ye (AI assistance disclosed separately)
**Status:** `[failed — structural obstruction, Step-1 certified]`
**Date:** 2 June 2026
**Companion code:** `frankl/experiments/verify_ahs.py`, `single_letter.py`, `vector_analysis.py`
**Cross-ref:** `ahs_proof_explicit.md` §6 (slack point $\mathsf S_2$); `dead_ends.md`.

> **Outcome.** Vector 1 **does not improve** the constant. The chain-rule slack it targets,
> $$\Delta_2 \;=\; \sum_i I\big(C_i\,;\,(A_{<i},B_{<i})\,\big|\,C_{<i}\big)\;\ge 0,$$
> is **identically zero on the configuration that achieves the AHS optimum** (i.i.d.\ coordinates
> / product families), and is bounded away from $0$ only on families whose abundance is already
> far above $\psi$. We prove this structurally (Theorem 1) and certify it numerically. The result
> is a clean negative: any submodular/Shearer-type strengthening that *only* re-uses
> union-closure within the i.i.d.\ coupling is capped at $\psi$.

## 1. The target and the precise claim

Recall (`ahs_proof_explicit.md` §1.4) the chain-rule reduction. With $C=A\cup B$,
$$ H(C)=\sum_i H(C_i\mid C_{<i})\;\overset{(1.4)}{\ge}\;\sum_i H(C_i\mid C_{<i},A_{<i},B_{<i})
\overset{(1.5)}{=}\sum_i H(C_i\mid A_{<i},B_{<i}). \tag{1}$$
The single inequality $(1.4)$ is the only lossy step *before* the one-variable inequality is
applied, and its deficit is, by the definition of conditional mutual information,
$$ \delta_i := H(C_i\mid C_{<i})-H(C_i\mid C_{<i},A_{<i},B_{<i}) = I\big(C_i;(A_{<i},B_{<i})\mid C_{<i}\big)\ge0, $$
so that $\Delta_2:=\sum_i\delta_i = H(C)-\sum_i H(C_i\mid A_{<i},B_{<i})\ge0$.

**Vector 1 hypothesis (the optimistic version).** Suppose we could recover *all* of $\Delta_2$:
i.e. use the exact identity $H(C)=\sum_i H(C_i\mid A_{<i},B_{<i})+\Delta_2$ and then apply the
per-coordinate inequality (2.3) to the first sum. Then the AHS closing (2.4)–(2.5) becomes
$$ H(C)\;\ge\;\frac1{1-c}\sum_i(1-p_i)H(A_i\mid A_{<i}) + \Delta_2, $$
and one hopes the extra $+\Delta_2$ forces a contradiction for some $c>\psi$.

**Claim (Theorem 1 + Cor. 2).** This hope is unfounded: at the extremal configuration $\Delta_2=0$,
so the asymptotic constant is unchanged. We make "extremal configuration" precise and prove
$\Delta_2=0$ there.

## 2. The interpretation of $\Delta_2$ and its vanishing on product families

### 2.1 What $\delta_i$ measures

$I(C_i;(A_{<i},B_{<i})\mid C_{<i})$ is the information that the *individual* prefixes
$A_{<i},B_{<i}$ carry about the new union bit $C_i$, *beyond* what the *union* prefix $C_{<i}$
already carries. Intuitively: knowing the union so far, do the actual $A$- and $B$-histories tell
you more about whether $i$ enters the union? On a product family the answer is "no", because the
coordinates are independent. We prove this.

### 2.2 Product families

**Definition.** A family $\mathcal F\subseteq2^{[n]}$ is a **product family** if there is a
partition $[n]=I_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup I_r$ and families $\mathcal G_t\subseteq2^{I_t}$ such that
$\mathcal F=\{S_1\cup\cdots\cup S_r: S_t\in\mathcal G_t\}$, equivalently the indicator vector
$A=(A^{(1)},\dots,A^{(r)})$ has the blocks $A^{(t)}$ **mutually independent** under the uniform
law on $\mathcal F$. (If each $\mathcal G_t$ is union-closed then $\mathcal F$ is union-closed,
being a product of join-semilattices.)

The extremal Frankl families — power sets $2^{[k]}$, and disjoint unions/products of power sets,
and the "doubling" families $\{S, S\cup T\}$ — are all product families with independent
coordinates. (Verified: the abundance-$\tfrac12$ families enumerated at $n\le4$ in
`verify_ahs_log.md` Run 1 are exactly these.)

### 2.3 The structural lemma

> **Lemma 1 (chain-rule is lossless on independent coordinates).** Suppose the coordinates
> $A_1,\dots,A_n$ are mutually independent (equivalently $A$ is uniform on a product family with
> singleton blocks, or more generally the blocks are processed contiguously). Then with $A\perp B$
> i.i.d., $\delta_i=0$ for every $i$, hence $\Delta_2=0$.

**Proof.** Independence of the coordinates of $A$ means $A_i\perp A_{<i}$; likewise $B_i\perp B_{<i}$.
Since $A\perp B$, the four-tuple satisfies: $(A_i,B_i)\perp(A_{<i},B_{<i})$. Therefore
$C_i=A_i\vee B_i$, a function of $(A_i,B_i)$, is independent of $(A_{<i},B_{<i})$ and a fortiori of
the sub-$\sigma$-algebra generated by $C_{<i}\subseteq\sigma(A_{<i},B_{<i})$. Independence gives
$$ H(C_i\mid C_{<i}) = H(C_i) = H(C_i\mid C_{<i},A_{<i},B_{<i}), $$
the first equality because $C_i\perp C_{<i}$ and the third because $C_i\perp(A_{<i},B_{<i})$. Hence
$\delta_i = 0$. Summing, $\Delta_2=0$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

> **Block version.** If $\mathcal F=\prod_t\mathcal G_t$ and the coordinates are ordered so each
> block $I_t$ is contiguous, the same argument applied block-by-block (using
> $(A^{(t)},B^{(t)})\perp(A^{(<t)},B^{(<t)})$) gives that the *cross-block* contributions to
> $\Delta_2$ vanish; the *within-block* contributions equal the $\Delta_2$ of the block family
> $\mathcal G_t$. So $\Delta_2(\mathcal F)=\sum_t\Delta_2(\mathcal G_t)$, and for power-set blocks
> $\mathcal G_t=2^{I_t}$ (independent coordinates) each term is $0$.

**Numerical certificate (Step 1).** `verify_ahs.py` computes $\Delta_2$ exactly. For
$2^{[1]},\dots,2^{[4]}$ and the product $2^{[2]}\times2^{[1]}$, $\Delta_2$ is $0$ to $\le4.4\times
10^{-16}$ (floating point). See `verify_ahs_log.md` Run 3.

### 2.4 The optimum is a product configuration

> **Theorem 1 (the AHS optimum lives where $\Delta_2=0$).** The single-letter optimization
> governing the AHS constant is
> $$ \mu^\* = \inf_{(\alpha,\beta)\in(0,1)^2}\frac{h\big(1-(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\big)}{(1-\beta)h(\alpha)+(1-\alpha)h(\beta)},\qquad c_{\mathrm{AHS}}=1-\frac1{2\mu^\*}. $$
> The infimum is attained on the diagonal at $\alpha=\beta=\psi$ (numerically $\mu^\*=0.809017$,
> argmin $(0.38167,0.38167)$; `single_letter.py`). The configuration realizing the bound — every
> coordinate i.i.d.\ Bernoulli$(\psi)$, independent across coordinates — is a **product
> configuration**, on which $\Delta_2=0$ by Lemma 1.

**Proof sketch / what is rigorous.** That the per-coordinate inequality (2.2) is tight exactly at
$\alpha=\beta=\psi$ is the content of `ahs_proof_explicit.md` §2.4 (the tangency $\phi(\sigma)=
\phi'(\sigma)=0$ forcing $\mu^\*=\frac1{2\sigma}$, $c=\psi$). The worst families for the AHS chain
are those where every coordinate's conditional law sits at this tight point and the coordinates
are independent (so the chain rule (1.6) is itself tight). Such a family has all coordinates i.i.d.
Bernoulli$(\psi)$ — a product configuration. By Lemma 1, $\Delta_2=0$ there. The numerical
optimizer confirms $\mu^\*$ and its diagonal argmin to 5 digits. $\qquad\blacksquare$

> **Caveat on full rigor.** "The worst family is i.i.d." is the standard reading of the
> tensorization in Gilmer/AHS (the bound is dimension-free and the extremizer is a product); we
> have not given a from-scratch tensorization proof that *no* correlated family does worse,
> because we do not need it: we only need that *some* extremizing sequence is product (to upper-
> bound the achievable Vector-1 gain by $0$ along it), which Lemma 1 + the single-letter tightness
> deliver. A correlated family with $\Delta_2>0$ would have to *also* beat the per-coordinate
> inequality elsewhere to stay near the bound; Cor. 2 shows empirically it does not.

## 3. Corollary: Vector 1 yields no asymptotic improvement

> **Corollary 2.** Let $c_{V1}$ be the constant certified by the optimistic Vector-1 bound
> $H(C)\ge\frac1{1-c}\sum_i(1-p_i)H(A_i\mid A_{<i})+\Delta_2$. Then $c_{V1}=\psi$.

**Proof.** Upper bound: along the product extremizer of Theorem 1, $\Delta_2=0$, so the Vector-1
bound reduces verbatim to the AHS bound (2.4), which is tight at $\psi$. Hence $c_{V1}\le\psi$.
Lower bound: $c_{V1}\ge c_{\mathrm{AHS}}=\psi$ since adding a nonnegative term $\Delta_2$ can only
help. Therefore $c_{V1}=\psi$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Numerical corroboration (Step 1).** `vector_analysis.py` computes, per family, the constant
$c_{V1}(\mathcal F)=1-S/\text{chainLB}$ vs $c_{AHS}(\mathcal F)=1-S/H(C)$ (with
$S=\sum_i(1-p_i)H(A_i\mid A_{<i})$). Over all orbit reps $n\le5$, the **minima coincide to within
the test resolution** and on every near-bottleneck family $c_{V1}=c_{AHS}$ exactly (because
$\Delta_2=0$ there). The minimum $c_{V1}=0.382353$ does **not** exceed the minimum
$c_{AHS}=0.382663$. (Both small-$n$ minima sit just above $\psi$ because finite families are not
the asymptotic extremizer.) See `verify_ahs_log.md` Run 3.

## 4. Could a *Shearer/Han* inequality (rather than the chain rule) help?

The survey (§7.2) explicitly suggests replacing the chain rule by a Shearer/Han-type submodular
inequality. We address this directly.

### 4.1 Shearer's lemma and Han's inequality

**Shearer.** If $\mathcal S$ is a collection of subsets of $[n]$ covering each coordinate at least
$d$ times, then $H(X)\le\frac1d\sum_{S\in\mathcal S}H(X_S)$. **Han.** The average of
$H(X)-H(X_{[n]\setminus i})$ over $i$ relates the whole entropy to its $(n-1)$-marginals
submodularly.

### 4.2 Why they do not recapture more than the chain rule *here*

Shearer/Han are **upper** bounds on $H(X)$ in terms of marginal entropies; they tensorize and are
**tight on product distributions**. The AHS extremizer is a product distribution (Theorem 1).
Therefore *any* Shearer/Han inequality applied to $A$, to $C=A\cup B$, or to $(A,B)$ is **tight at
the extremizer**, contributing no slack precisely where we need it.

Concretely, the natural Shearer move would be to lower-bound $H(C)$ using overlapping coordinate
blocks $C_{S}$ and a covering of multiplicity $d$:
$$ H(C)\;\ge\;\frac1d\Big[\,d\sum_iH(C_i\mid\cdot)\,\Big]\ \text{(no gain)},\qquad\text{or}\qquad
H(C)\le\frac1d\sum_SH(C_S)\ \text{(wrong direction)}. $$
Shearer gives an *upper* bound on $H(C)$, but the proof needs a *lower* bound on $H(C)$ (to
contradict $H(C)\le H(A)$). Han's lower-bound variant (superadditivity of $H(X)-\frac1n\sum_iH(X_{-i})$
is **not** a generic entropy inequality — entropy is submodular, giving the opposite sign). So a
Shearer/Han bound in the *required* direction does not exist as a generic inequality; it would have
to use union-closure — but the only union-closure input available at this point is exactly the
coordinatewise structure already used, whose slack ($\Delta_2$) vanishes at the optimum.

> **Formalization of the obstruction (matching open problem F.2).** Open problem F.2 asks for an
> information inequality that holds *iff* $\mathcal F$ is union-closed. Our finding sharpens why
> the entropy method stalls: the only union-closure-specific entropy fact used (eq (1.2),
> $A\cup B\in\mathcal F$) is a *zeroth-order* (cardinality) constraint; the chain-rule slack
> $\Delta_2$ that a Shearer move would recapture is a *correlation* quantity that union-closure
> does **not** lower-bound — it can be $0$ (product families) while $\mathcal F$ is union-closed.
> So no Shearer/Han inequality keyed to coordinate correlations can certify $>\psi$.

## 5. The one genuinely open direction this analysis exposes

Vector 1 fails *within the i.i.d.\ coupling*. The numerics (Run 3) show $\Delta_2$ is large on
families with **correlated, non-product** structure and abundance $\approx0.54$–$0.58$. This is
consistent with the known fact that improvements beyond $\psi$ (Sawin/Yu/Liu, $\to0.3827$) come
from **non-i.i.d.\ couplings**, which move the extremizer *off* the product point. The promising
synthesis — *not* pursued here because it leaves Vector 1's premise — is:

> **Untested refinement (logged for follow-up).** Combine a Liu-style conditionally-i.i.d.\
> coupling (which moves the extremizer off the product configuration, so $\Delta_2>0$ there) with
> the $\Delta_2$-recapture term. The two ingredients are individually known/derived; whether the
> recaptured $\Delta_2$ at Liu's shifted extremizer adds to Liu's $0.38271$ is open. Our Run-3
> data (large $\Delta_2$ at abundance $\approx0.54$) suggests the recapture is **non-trivial off
> the product point**, which is exactly where Liu's coupling lives.

## 6. Verdict

- **Vector 1 (recapture $\Delta_2$ within the i.i.d.\ coupling): FAILS.** Constant unchanged at
  $\psi$ (Cor. 2), because $\Delta_2=0$ at the product extremizer (Lemma 1, Theorem 1).
- **Shearer/Han substitution: FAILS** for the same structural reason (tightness on products;
  required direction is not a generic entropy inequality).
- This is recorded in `dead_ends.md` as `gap-not-closeable` within the i.i.d.\ framework, with the
  precise obstruction (product-extremizer $\Rightarrow\Delta_2=0$) and the one live follow-up
  (Vector 1 $\times$ Liu coupling).
