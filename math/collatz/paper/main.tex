\begin{abstract}
We survey density-based approaches to the Collatz $3n+1$ problem, organized around the current
state of the art: Tao's 2022 theorem that almost every orbit (in logarithmic density) attains
almost bounded values. We then make explicit a structural \emph{obstruction} to one natural strategy
for upgrading Tao's result from logarithmic to natural density. The strategy --- re-centering the
Syracuse random walk by a single scalar Esscher (exponential) tilt and asking for total-variation
equidistribution of the tilted Syracuse law --- cannot succeed, because the Syracuse law's projection
modulo $3$ is permanently $(0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$, never uniform. By the data-processing inequality
this forces $\TV(\nu_n,U)\ge \tfrac16$ for all $n$ and for every admissible scalar tilt, so the
required equidistribution is unattainable. We corroborate this numerically: an exact FFT computation
of the associated collision diagnostic $\Edist_n$ shows it \emph{diverges} (linearly untilted,
exponentially under the drift-balancing tilt) with no turnover out to $n=17$, and exhibits a tilt-space
phase transition that strands the required tilt on the non-equidistributing side. We are careful to
delimit the scope: this obstructs one reduction only; coset-respecting reference measures and
non-scalar change-of-measure mechanisms remain open, and nothing here bears on whether Collatz natural
density is true. We then recast the obstruction spectrally, as a Frobenius--Perron (transfer)
operator $\Pop_n$ on the units of $\Zthree{n}$: the Syracuse law is exactly its stationary
distribution, the mod-$3$ imbalance is an explicit rank-one $2\times2$ block, and an exact
characteristic-polynomial computation gives $\charpoly_{\Pop_n}(\lambda)=\lambda^{\,\tot(3^n)-1}(\lambda-1)$
--- a single eigenvalue $1$ and all others exactly zero, with $\Pop_n-\statproj$ nilpotent. The
upshot is that the obstruction lives entirely in the (permanently non-uniform) stationary vector,
not in any mixing-rate defect. We pair this with a function-field diagnostic: the $\Ffield$ analog
of Collatz provably converges via a degree Lyapunov function, isolating the archimedean/$2$-adic
decoupling that makes the integer case resist, and a matched negative result that no binary-digit
statistic can serve as a Lyapunov function. We also reconstruct the Steiner--Simons--de Weger--Hercher cycle-exclusion argument,
correcting a common mis-attribution of its bottleneck to the irrationality measure of $\log_2 3$ in
favour of a two-logarithm linear-forms estimate. We close with open questions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Collatz conjecture, attributed to Lothar Collatz (1937) and circulated also under the names
of Kakutani, Ulam, Hasse, and Thwaites~\citep{lagarias1985}, concerns the map
\[
   \mathrm{Col}(n) = \begin{cases} n/2 & n \text{ even}, \\ 3n+1 & n \text{ odd}, \end{cases}
   \qquad n \in \nat = \{1,2,3,\dots\}.
\]
It asserts that iterating $\mathrm{Col}$ from any positive integer eventually reaches $1$.
Despite verification to $n \le 2^{71}$~\citep{barina2021,barina2025} % [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary]
and a sequence of deep partial results, the conjecture is open.

It is convenient to work with the \emph{accelerated} (Syracuse-style) map
\begin{equation}
   \T(n) = \begin{cases} n/2 & n \equiv 0 \pmod 2, \\ (3n+1)/2 & n \equiv 1 \pmod 2, \end{cases}
   \label{eq:Tmap}
\end{equation}
which folds the obligatory halving after each odd step into a single iteration, and to study the
minimal orbit value $\Colmin(N) := \inf_{k \ge 0}\T^k(N)$. The conjecture is equivalent to
$\Colmin(N) = 1$ for all $N$, which in turn is equivalent to the conjunction of two statements:
\emph{there is no nontrivial cycle} and \emph{there is no divergent orbit}.

\paragraph{The state of the art.}
The strongest ``almost all'' descent result is due to Tao~\citep{tao2022}: for any
$f:\nat\to\R$ with $f(N)\to\infty$, one has $\Colmin(N)\le f(N)$ for a set of $N$ of full
\emph{logarithmic} density. This improved the long-standing record exponents of
Korec~\citep{korec1994} and Krasikov--Lagarias~\citep{krasikov2003}. Tao remarked, both in the
paper and in a companion blog post~\citep{tao2020blog}, that the \emph{logarithmic}-density
qualifier should plausibly be upgradable to \emph{natural} density via a finer equidistribution
(``fine-scale mixing'') statement about the Syracuse random variables on $\Zthree{n}$; as of this
writing no such upgrade has appeared.

\paragraph{Contribution.}
This article does two things.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{Survey} (Sections~\ref{sec:notation}--\ref{sec:tao},
\ref{sec:cycles},\ref{sec:computational}). We give a compact, honest survey of the density methods,
the Tao 2022 framework, the cycle-exclusion line, and the computational frontier, with explicit
attention to \emph{what each result does not prove}.
\item \textbf{A natural-density obstruction} (Section~\ref{sec:obstruction}, the new content).
We make explicit that the specific ``scalar-Esscher mixing'' route to upgrading Tao's theorem to
natural density is \emph{obstructed}. The obstruction is elementary: the Syracuse random variable
$X_n$ reduces mod $3$ to a fixed non-uniform law $(0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$ for every $n$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mod3}), whence
\begin{equation}
  \TV\big(\nu_n, U\big) \;\ge\; \tfrac16 \qquad \text{for all } n,
  \label{eq:headline}
\end{equation}
and indeed $\TV(\nu_n^{(s)},U)>0$ uniformly for \emph{every} admissible scalar tilt $s$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction}). Consequently the total-variation equidistribution that the route
requires cannot hold. We corroborate this with an exact FFT computation of the collision diagnostic
$\Edist_n=\tot(3^n)\CP_n-1$, which diverges with no turnover to $n=17$ and exhibits a phase
transition in tilt space (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}). We delimit the scope sharply
(Section~\ref{sec:scope}): this kills one reduction, not the destination.
\item \textbf{A transfer-operator view} (Section~\ref{sec:transfer}). We recast the obstruction
in clean spectral form. The Syracuse step on the units of $\Zthree{n}$ is a finite Markov chain
with operator $\Pop_n$; the Syracuse law $\nu_n$ is \emph{exactly} its stationary distribution
(Proposition~\ref{prop:stationary}); the mod-$3$ imbalance is an explicit rank-one $2\times2$ block
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mod3block}); and an exact rational computation gives
$\charpoly_{\Pop_n}(\lambda)=\lambda^{\,\tot(3^n)-1}(\lambda-1)$, so $\Pop_n$ has the single nonzero
eigenvalue $1$ and is nilpotent modulo its stationary projector (Proposition~\ref{prop:nilpotent}).
The moral is sharp: the obstruction is the non-uniform stationary vector, not a mixing-rate defect.
\item \textbf{A thermodynamic-formalism refinement and a two-parameter saturation}
(Section~\ref{sec:thermo}). We give a closed-form pressure $P(s)$ and Cram\'er rate function $I(x)$ for
the drift cocycle $\varphi=\log 3-a\log 2$, with $I(0)=-P(s^\dagger)\approx 0.055$ the per-step
descent-failure rate and $P''(s^\dagger)=\log 3\cdot\log(3/2)\approx 0.446$ the tilted CLT variance.
We correct a sign-convention slip in earlier candidate notes: the drift-balancing single-parameter
Esscher tilt \emph{worsens} the mod-$3$ marginal (TV $1/6\to 0.230$, not $\to 0.096$ as previously
reported). We then exhibit the first non-trivial mod-$3$ saturation in this stream, via a
two-parameter \emph{Class~(C)} Esscher with auxiliary parity cocycle $\psi(a)=\mathbf{1}[a\text{ even}]$,
at $(s_C,t_C)\approx(-1.309,-1.600)$, with drift mean zero, mod-$3$ marginal exactly $(0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$,
and joint LDP rate $\approx 0.228=4.15\times I(0)$; and a structural obstruction theorem
(Proposition~\ref{prop:dimbarrier}) that rules out iterating any translation-invariant per-coord
Esscher saturation to mod-$3^k$ for $k\ge2$, with drift-incompatibility gap growing as
$\Theta(3^{k-1})$.
\item \textbf{A function-field diagnostic} (Section~\ref{sec:why-hard}). We record why the integer
case resists, by contrast with a provably convergent $\Ffield$ analog (degree Lyapunov function,
Proposition~\ref{prop:degree}), pairing it with a negative result that no binary-digit statistic is
a Lyapunov function. These are diagnostics of difficulty; they say nothing about whether Collatz holds.
\end{enumerate}
We also flag a correction to the folklore framing of the cycle-exclusion bottleneck
(Section~\ref{sec:cycles}).

\paragraph{Honesty posture.}
We claim no proof of Collatz and no resolution of the natural-density question; \eqref{eq:headline}
says nothing about whether natural density holds. We do not claim the mod-$3$ fact is new --- it is
elementary and plausibly known to Tao or folklore --- only that we make it explicit \emph{as an
obstruction to this reduction}. External constants are snippet-sourced (primary PDFs were
inaccessible) and flagged in the source. See the title footnote.

\paragraph{Organization.}
Section~\ref{sec:notation} fixes notation. Section~\ref{sec:history} surveys the density results.
Section~\ref{sec:tao} reproduces Tao's framework and localizes where logarithmic density is forced.
Section~\ref{sec:obstruction} states and proves the obstruction; Section~\ref{sec:numerics} gives the
numerical corroboration; Section~\ref{sec:scope} delimits scope.
Section~\ref{sec:thermo} adds the closed-form drift LDP, the corrected single-parameter mod-$3$
picture, the two-parameter Class~(C) saturation, and the finite-$d$ iteration barrier.
Section~\ref{sec:transfer} gives the transfer-operator reformulation; Section~\ref{sec:why-hard}

reconstructs cycle exclusion. Section~\ref{sec:computational} notes the computational frontier.
Section~\ref{sec:open} lists open questions.

\section{Notation and preliminaries}
\label{sec:notation}

We follow Lagarias~\citep{lagarias1985,lagarias2010}. Throughout, $\nat=\{1,2,\dots\}$;
$\val=\nu_2$ is the $2$-adic valuation and $\nu_3$ the $3$-adic valuation; $e(t):=e^{2\pi i t}$;
$\log$ is natural; $\tot(3^n)=2\cdot3^{n-1}$ is Euler's totient.

\begin{definition}[Collatz and Syracuse maps]
The accelerated Collatz map $\T$ is~\eqref{eq:Tmap}. The \emph{Syracuse map} on the odd integers is
$\Syr(n) = (3n+1)/2^{\val(3n+1)}$, i.e.\ $\T$ run until the orbit is odd again.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Stopping times]
The \emph{stopping time} is $\stoptime{n}=\min\{k\ge1:\T^k(n)<n\}$; the \emph{total stopping time}
is $\totstoptime{n}=\min\{k\ge0:\T^k(n)=1\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{conjecture}[Collatz, 1937]\label{conj:collatz}
For every $n\in\nat$ there is $k\ge0$ with $\T^k(n)=1$; equivalently $\Colmin(n)=1$.
\end{conjecture}

The reduction to odd integers is standard: writing $\Syrmin(n):=\inf_{k\ge0}\Syr^k(n)$ for odd $n$,
\begin{equation}
   \Colmin(N) = \Syrmin\!\big(N/2^{\val(N)}\big),
   \label{eq:colsyr}
\end{equation}
since the minimum of a $\T$-orbit is attained at an odd value (unless the orbit reaches $1$).
Thus density statements about $\Colmin$ on $\nat$ reduce to density statements about $\Syrmin$ on
the odds; we use this in Section~\ref{sec:tao}.

\section{Density results: a survey}
\label{sec:history}

We organize the ``almost all'' results by what they control.

\paragraph{Finite stopping time (natural density $1$).}
Terras~\citep{terras1976} and, independently, Everett~\citep{everett1977} proved that
$\{n:\stoptime{n}<\infty\}$ has natural density $1$: almost every orbit dips below its start.
The proof encodes the trajectory by its parity vector and applies a local central limit theorem to
the (asymptotically Gaussian) count of odd steps. \emph{What it misses:} a density-$0$ set is
unaddressed, and ``stopping time finite'' is far weaker than ``total stopping time finite.''

\paragraph{Descent exponents.}
Crandall~\citep{crandall1978} gave the first power lower bound $\#\{m\le x:\totstoptime{m}<\infty\}\ge x^c$.
Korec~\citep{korec1994} % [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary]
proved that for every $c>\log_4 3 = 0.7925\ldots$, almost every $n$ (natural density) has some
iterate $\T^k(n)<n^c$. The exponent $\log_4 3$ is the balance point at which the factor-$3$ gain of an
odd step is offset by the factor-$4$ loss of (on average) two halvings.

\paragraph{Pre-image density.}
Building on Krasikov's difference inequalities, Applegate--Lagarias~\citep{applegatelagarias1995} and
Krasikov--Lagarias~\citep{krasikov2003} showed that for any fixed $a$ with $3\nmid a$, the number of
$n\le x$ whose forward orbit meets $a$ is at least $x^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma>0.84$. This was the
strongest unconditional density-of-descent result before Tao 2022. \emph{What it misses:} $x^{0.84}<x$
is consistent with a positive-density set never reaching $1$.

\paragraph{Heuristics and limit laws.}
Lagarias--Weiss~\citep{lagariasweiss1992} model parities as fair coin flips, predicting
$\totstoptime{n}\approx \gamma_{LW}\log n$ with $\gamma_{LW}=2/\log(4/3)\approx 6.95$, in striking
agreement with computation, and predicting no cycles and no divergence. Sinai~\citep{sinai2003} turned
the random-walk picture into rigorous limit laws --- but on $\Z_2$, not on $\nat$, and without
addressing convergence.

\paragraph{The $2$-adic conjugacy.}
Bernstein--Lagarias~\citep{bernsteinlagarias1996} exhibit a continuous, Haar-measure-preserving
conjugacy of $\T$ on $\Z_2$ with the shift; Akin~\citep{akin2004} % [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary]
uses the fact that this conjugacy does not single out $\nat\subset\Z_2$ to explain why measure-respecting
tools cannot, by themselves, detect the integers --- a structural reason the problem is hard.

\section{Tao's 2022 framework and where logarithmic density is forced}
\label{sec:tao}

\subsection{The theorem}
\begin{theorem}[\citealp{tao2022}]\label{thm:tao}
For any $f:\nat\to\R$ with $f(N)\to\infty$, the failure set
$E_f=\{N:\Colmin(N)>f(N)\}$ has logarithmic density zero:
$\displaystyle \frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{N\le X,\,N\in E_f}\frac1N \to 0$ as $X\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}
The same holds for $\Syrmin$ on the odds, via~\eqref{eq:colsyr}. The function $f$ may grow as slowly
as one likes. Theorem~\ref{thm:tao} leaves open: nontrivial cycles (any finite set has density $0$),
divergent orbits from a density-$0$ set, and the upgrade of ``almost bounded'' ($\le f$) to
``bounded'' ($\le C$).

\subsection{The Syracuse random variables}
The combinatorial heart is a family of distributions on $\Zthree{n}$. Unwinding the affine action of
$\T$ over $n$ Syracuse steps with $2$-adic valuations $a_j=\val\!\big(3\Syr^{j-1}(\cdot)+1\big)$ gives
an offset that, randomized, becomes:

\begin{definition}[Syracuse random variable; \citealp{tao2022,tao2020blog}]\label{def:syrac}
Let $a_1,\dots,a_n$ be i.i.d.\ $\Geom$: $\PP(a=k)=2^{-k}$ for $k\ge1$ (mean $2$). The
\emph{Syracuse random variable} on $\Zthree{n}$ is
\begin{equation}
  X_n := \Syrac(\Zthree{n}) = \sum_{j=1}^n 3^{\,n-j}\, 2^{-(a_j+a_{j+1}+\cdots+a_n)} \pmod{3^n},
  \label{eq:syrac}
\end{equation}
where $2^{-1}\equiv (3^n+1)/2 \pmod{3^n}$. Its law $\nu_n$ is supported on the units $\Zthreex{n}$.
\end{definition}

Write $\charfn{n}(\xi)=\E\!\big[e(-\xi X_n/3^n)\big]$ for the characteristic function and
\[
   c_n := \min_{b\in\Zthree{n},\,3\nmid b}\PP(X_n=b).
\]
Tao~\citep{tao2020blog} proves submultiplicativity $c_{n_1+n_2-1}\ge c_{n_1}c_{n_2}$, so
$\beta:=\lim_n \tfrac{-\log c_n}{n\log 3}\ge 1$ exists; the ``$\beta=1$'' heuristic is the conjecture
$c_n=3^{-n+o(n)}$ (equidistribution at exponential \emph{order}). The technical engine of
Theorem~\ref{thm:tao} is a \emph{superpolynomial} decay estimate
\begin{equation}
  |\charfn{n}(\xi)| \le C_A\, n^{-A}\quad(\forall A>0),\qquad \text{for } 3\nmid\xi,
  \label{eq:taudecay}
\end{equation}
(Tao's Proposition~1.17), uniform in $\xi$ with $3\nmid\xi$. Crucially \eqref{eq:taudecay} is
superpolynomial but \emph{not} exponential in $n$.

\subsection{Where logarithmic density is forced}
\label{subsec:localization}
Two places in the transport scheme use a measure on $\nat$; only one is essential.

\begin{lemma}[dyadic decomposition is measure-neutral]\label{lem:dyadic}
Let $\mathcal P$ be a property of odd integers with $\{m \text{ odd}:\neg\mathcal P(m)\}$ of natural
density $0$ among the odds. Then $\{N\in\nat:\neg\mathcal P(N/2^{\val(N)})\}$ has natural density $0$
in $\nat$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $B=\{m\text{ odd}:\neg\mathcal P(m)\}$, $\#\{m\in B:m\le Y\}=o(Y)$. The bad set in $\nat$ is
$\bigsqcup_{a\ge0}2^a B$, so
$\#\{N\le X:\neg\mathcal P(N/2^{\val(N)})\}=\sum_{a=0}^{\lfloor\log_2 X\rfloor}\#\{m\in B:m\le X/2^a\}$.
Fix $\eps>0$ and $Y_0$ with $\#\{m\in B:m\le Y\}\le \eps Y$ for $Y\ge Y_0$. Splitting the sum at
$a^*=\lfloor\log_2(X/Y_0)\rfloor$: the terms $a\le a^*$ contribute $\le \sum_a \eps X/2^a\le 2\eps X$;
the terms $a>a^*$ number $\le\log_2 X$, each $<Y_0$, totalling $o(X)$. Hence the bad count is
$\le 2\eps X+o(X)$; let $\eps\to0$.
\end{proof}

So the entire log-vs-natural gap lives in the odd-integer (Syracuse) statement.
The essential appearance is the stationarity of the sampling measure. On the log scale $u=\log N$, the
first-passage Syracuse map acts as an approximate \emph{translation} $u\mapsto u-D_n$ by the random
log-drift
\begin{equation}
  D_n=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^n a_j\Big)\log 2-n\log 3.
  \label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
Lebesgue measure in $u$ --- i.e.\ the \emph{logarithmic} measure $dN/N$ --- is translation-invariant,
hence approximately stationary; this is why Tao obtains logarithmic density. Under \emph{natural}-density
sampling $dN$, the density on the $u$-line is $e^u\,du$, which a translation multiplies by the
Radon--Nikodym factor $e^{-D_n}$. Because $D_n$ in \eqref{eq:drift} is itself a function of the orbit ---
correlated with the residue mod $3^n$ one is trying to equidistribute --- this factor couples
size-distortion to residue. We record the consequence informally.

\begin{remark}[localization of the obstruction]\label{rem:localization}
Upgrading Theorem~\ref{thm:tao} to natural density requires controlling the \emph{joint} law of
$(\,X_n,\ D_n\,)$ --- residue and drift --- strongly enough that the reweighting by $e^{-D_n}$ does not
destroy equidistribution; the marginal residue control underlying~\eqref{eq:taudecay} is not by itself
enough. This localization is consistent with Tao's own remark that the restriction to logarithmic
density is ``mainly technical.''
\end{remark}

The natural attempt to act on Remark~\ref{rem:localization} --- discharge $e^{-D_n}$ by a single scalar
exponential (Esscher) tilt of the valuation law and then ask for total-variation equidistribution of the
tilted Syracuse law --- is exactly the route the next section shows to be obstructed.

\section{A natural-density obstruction for the scalar-Esscher route}
\label{sec:obstruction}

This section is the article's new content. The result is a clean negative statement: the scalar-Esscher
mixing route cannot deliver natural density, because of a permanent modulo-$3$ imbalance. The argument is
elementary and self-contained.

\subsection{The tilted law and the diagnostic}
For a real tilt $s>-1$, let the \emph{$s$-tilted} Syracuse variable $X_n^{(s)}$ be defined
by~\eqref{eq:syrac} with the valuations $a_j$ drawn i.i.d.\ from the exponentially tilted geometric law
\begin{equation}
   \PP_s(a=k)\propto 2^{-k(1+s)}=r^k,\qquad r=2^{-(1+s)}\in(0,1),\quad k\ge1,
   \label{eq:tilt}
\end{equation}
which is geometric with ratio $r$ and mean $\E_s[a]=1/(1-r)$. The \emph{descent-balance tilt} $\Esstar$
is the unique $s$ with $\E_s[a]=\log_2 3$ (so the multiplier $3^n 2^{-\sum a_j}$ has log-mean $0$ and
natural-density sampling is, to leading order, stationary): solving $1/(1-r)=\log_2 3$ gives
$r=1-1/\log_2 3=0.36907\ldots$ and
\begin{equation}
   \Esstar=-\log_2\!\big(1-1/\log_2 3\big)-1=0.438033\ldots
   \label{eq:sstar}
\end{equation}
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary] -- s* is an internal definition, value reproduced numerically.
Write $\nu_n^{(s)}=\mathrm{law}(X_n^{(s)})$, $U$ for the uniform law on the units $\Zthreex{n}$,
$\CP_n=\sum_b\nu_n(b)^2=\|\nu_n\|_{\ell^2}^2$, and the \emph{collision diagnostic}
\begin{equation}
   \Edist_n:=\tot(3^n)\,\CP_n-1.
   \label{eq:Endef}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The permanent modulo-3 imbalance}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mod3}
For every $n\ge1$ and every admissible tilt $s>-1$ (with $r=2^{-(1+s)}$),
\[
   \PP(X_n^{(s)}\equiv 0)=0,\qquad
   \PP(X_n^{(s)}\equiv 1)=\frac{r}{1+r},\qquad
   \PP(X_n^{(s)}\equiv 2)=\frac{1}{1+r}\pmod 3 .
\]
In particular, for the untilted law ($s=0$, $r=\tfrac12$) the projection is $(0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$,
independent of $n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Reduce~\eqref{eq:syrac} modulo $3$. For each $j<n$ the term carries a factor $3^{n-j}$ with
$n-j\ge1$, hence $\equiv 0\pmod 3$; only the $j=n$ term survives, so
$X_n \equiv 3^0\cdot 2^{-a_n} = 2^{-a_n}\pmod 3$. Since $2\equiv -1\pmod 3$, we get
$2^{-a_n}\equiv(-1)^{a_n}\pmod 3$, which is $1$ if $a_n$ is even and $2$ if $a_n$ is odd, and never $0$.
Under~\eqref{eq:tilt}, $\PP(a\text{ even})=\sum_{k\ge2,\text{ even}} r^k/\!\sum_{k\ge1}r^k
=\tfrac{r^2/(1-r^2)}{r/(1-r)}=\tfrac{r}{1+r}$ and $\PP(a\text{ odd})=\tfrac{1}{1+r}$. Hence
$\PP(X_n\equiv1)=\PP(a_n\text{ even})=\tfrac{r}{1+r}$ and $\PP(X_n\equiv2)=\PP(a_n\text{ odd})=\tfrac{1}{1+r}$.
The dependence on $n$ collapsed to the single valuation $a_n$, so the law is the same at every $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The projection equals the $n=1$ law for all $n$; the imbalance is frozen in at every level. It is
uniform-on-units $(0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ only in the limit $r\to1$, i.e.\ $s\to-1$ (the non-summable
edge). At the descent-balance tilt $\Esstar$, $r=0.36907$ gives $(0,0.2696,0.7304)$ --- \emph{more}
imbalanced than untilted. No admissible scalar tilt removes the imbalance.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Total variation is bounded below}
\begin{lemma}[data-processing for total variation]\label{lem:dpi}
For probability measures $\mu,\rho$ on a finite set $\Omega$ and any map $\pi:\Omega\to\Omega'$,
$\TV(\pi_*\mu,\pi_*\rho)\le\TV(\mu,\rho)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\TV(\mu,\rho)=\sup_{A\subseteq\Omega}|\mu(A)-\rho(A)|$. For $B\subseteq\Omega'$,
$|\pi_*\mu(B)-\pi_*\rho(B)|=|\mu(\pi^{-1}B)-\rho(\pi^{-1}B)|\le\TV(\mu,\rho)$; take the sup over $B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[the scalar-Esscher route is obstructed]\label{thm:obstruction}
For every $n\ge1$ and every admissible scalar tilt $s>-1$ (with $r=2^{-(1+s)}$),
\begin{equation}
   \TV\big(\nu_n^{(s)},\,U\big)\;\ge\;\frac{1-r}{2(1+r)}\;>\;0,
   \label{eq:tiltbound}
\end{equation}
and in particular $\TV(\nu_n,U)\ge\tfrac16$ for the untilted law, for all $n$. Hence $\nu_n^{(s)}$ cannot
converge to $U$ in total variation for any fixed scalar tilt.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:dpi} with $\mu=\nu_n^{(s)}$, $\rho=U$, and $\pi=(\bmod\,3)$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:mod3}, $\pi_*\nu_n^{(s)}=\big(0,\tfrac{r}{1+r},\tfrac{1}{1+r}\big)$. The units
$\Zthreex{n}$ split evenly between the classes $1$ and $2$ mod $3$ (exactly $3^{n-1}=\tot(3^n)/2$ in
each), so $\pi_* U=(0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$. Therefore
\[
  \TV(\nu_n^{(s)},U)\ge\TV(\pi_*\nu_n^{(s)},\pi_* U)
  =\tfrac12\!\left(\Big|\tfrac{r}{1+r}-\tfrac12\Big|+\Big|\tfrac{1}{1+r}-\tfrac12\Big|\right)
  =\Big|\tfrac{1}{1+r}-\tfrac12\Big|=\frac{1-r}{2(1+r)} .
\]
For $s=0$, $r=\tfrac12$: $\tfrac{1-\frac12}{2(1+\frac12)}=\tfrac{1/2}{3}=\tfrac16$.
\end{proof}

The total-variation equidistribution required by the scalar-Esscher route --- i.e.\ a hypothesis of the
form ``$\nu_n^{(s)}\to U$ in TV at a quantitative rate,'' which is what a Plancherel passage from
exponential Fourier decay would deliver and what the natural-density transport would consume --- is
therefore impossible. The only correct way to keep a TV statement is to compare $\nu_n$ not to
uniform-on-units but to a measure that respects the coset structure mod $3$ (and its lifts); we return to
this in Section~\ref{sec:scope}.

\begin{remark}[the $\ell^2$-to-TV bridge]\label{rem:bridge}
Since $\nu_n$ and $U$ are both supported on the $\tot(3^n)$ units, $\nu_n-U$ is supported there, so
$\langle\nu_n,U\rangle=1/\tot(3^n)$, $\|\nu_n-U\|_{\ell^2}^2=\CP_n-1/\tot(3^n)$, and by Cauchy--Schwarz
over the support,
\begin{equation}
  \TV(\nu_n,U)=\tfrac12\|\nu_n-U\|_{\ell^1}\le\tfrac12\sqrt{\tot(3^n)}\,\|\nu_n-U\|_{\ell^2}
  =\tfrac12\sqrt{\Edist_n}.
  \label{eq:l2tv}
\end{equation}
The factor $\sqrt{\tot(3^n)}\asymp 3^{n/2}$ is precisely why a natural-density upgrade through this
bridge would need \emph{exponential} Fourier decay $|\charfn{n}(\xi)|\le 3^{-\theta n}$ ($\theta>\tfrac12$),
not the superpolynomial \eqref{eq:taudecay}. But \eqref{eq:l2tv} only \emph{upper}-bounds TV: it cannot
defeat the \emph{lower} bound \eqref{eq:tiltbound}. The diagnostic $\Edist_n$ is the full-ring $\ell^2$
quantity; the obstruction \eqref{eq:tiltbound} lives in the mod-$3$ marginal. Section~\ref{sec:numerics}
shows both fail together.
\end{remark}

\section{Numerical corroboration: $\Edist_n$ diverges, and a phase transition}

\paragraph{Method.}

\paragraph{A phase transition in tilt space.}

frequencies). These are different quantities; the experiment shows they fail together --- the marginal

the mechanism: the unique scalar tilt that re-centers the drift is precisely the one that maximizes,
rather than cancels, the residue collision. All of $\Edist_n$ here (even $\sim1.43^n\ll3^n$) is $3^{o(n)}$,
hence consistent with $\beta=1$; the obstruction concerns the strictly stronger exact-leading-constant
requirement $\Edist_n\to0$, not $\beta=1$.
\end{remark}

\section{Scope of the obstruction}
\label{sec:scope}

We state precisely what is and is not obstructed.

\paragraph{Obstructed (the result).}
The route that discharges the natural-density Radon--Nikodym factor $e^{-D_n}$ by a \emph{single scalar}
Esscher tilt and then obtains natural density from \emph{total-variation} convergence $\nu_n^{(s)}\to U$
of the tilted Syracuse law toward uniform-on-units. By Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} this convergence is
impossible for every scalar $s$; by Section~\ref{sec:numerics} the associated diagnostic diverges.

\paragraph{Not obstructed (open).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Coset-respecting reference.} Compare $\nu_n$ to the measure that is uniform \emph{within} the
coset structure mod $3^k$ that the offset actually realizes, rather than to uniform-on-units. Then the
mod-$3$ imbalance is built into the target and the TV lower bound \eqref{eq:tiltbound} vanishes by
construction. Whether Tao's transport then closes in natural density is open; this is the natural next
reduction and is \emph{not} settled here. (Tao's framework handles the coset part by a separate device,
consistent with this.)
\item \emph{Non-scalar change of measure.} A multi-parameter tilt, an operator- or $\xi$-dependent
reweighting outside the scalar-geometric family, or a tilt acting on the joint $(X_n,D_n)$ law rather
than the marginal valuation, is not covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction}.
\item \emph{Mechanisms not factoring through this TV statement} are untouched.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Says nothing about.}
Whether Collatz natural density holds --- a failure of \emph{this reduction} is not evidence either way,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:tao} is unaffected. Nor about the $\beta=1$ conjecture, which remains open and is
consistent with all our data. In one line: \emph{this reduction is dead; the destination, and every other
route to it, is alive.}

\paragraph{Novelty.}

The mod-$3$ imbalance is elementary (it is literally the $n=1$ law) and is plausibly already known to Tao
or folklore --- his Proposition~1.17 bounding only the $3\nmid\xi$ frequencies is \emph{consistent} with
awareness that the coset part needs separate treatment. We therefore phrase the contribution defensively:
\emph{we observe / make explicit} that this elementary fact obstructs the scalar-Esscher TV route, and we
corroborate it numerically (the $\Edist_n$ divergence and the tilt-space phase transition, which we
believe are not previously recorded \emph{in this packaging} --- also

\section{Thermodynamic-formalism refinement and a two-parameter saturation result}
\label{sec:thermo}

This section adds two pieces to the picture of Section~\ref{sec:obstruction}: a quantitative
large-deviation refinement of the \emph{drift} side of Tao's framework (with explicit closed-form

first mod-$3$ improvement in the scalar/multi-tilt stream --- together with an explicit finite-dimensional
\emph{barrier} that prevents iteration of the saturation to mod-$3^k$ for $k>1$. We also use this section
to flag a sign-convention correction to earlier candidate notes which had reported single-parameter Esscher
as \emph{attenuating} the mod-$3$ marginal: the correct conclusion is the opposite. None of this changes
Section~\ref{sec:obstruction}'s conclusion (the single-parameter scalar-Esscher TV route is dead). All
constants in this section are

\subsection{The cocycle setup}
We work with the i.i.d.\ Geom$(1/2)$ shift driving the Syracuse first-passage dynamics
(Definition~\ref{def:syrac}): valuations $a_j\in\{1,2,\dots\}$, $\PP(a=k)=2^{-k}$, and the
\emph{drift cocycle}
\[
  \varphi(a)\;=\;\log 3 \;-\; a\,\log 2 \qquad(\text{nats per Syracuse odd step}),
\]
with $\E_{\mu_0}[\varphi]=\log 3-2\log 2\approx-0.2877$ (the Lagarias--Weiss descent rate). The
block drift over $n$ steps is $D_n=\sum_{j=1}^n(a_j\log 2-\log 3)=-\sum_{j=1}^n\varphi(a_j)$;
descent over $n$ steps is the event $\{D_n>0\}$. The cocycle $\varphi$ is non-trivial in
$H^1$ (a non-coboundary), so the drift satisfies a non-degenerate CLT under $\mu_0$ with
variance $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{Liv\check si\check c}}=2(\log 2)^2\approx 0.9609$. The non-coboundary
status is the positive input that feeds the Cram\'er machinery below.

\subsection{Drift LDP and the descent-failure rate}

\begin{proposition}[closed-form pressure and rate function; Cram\'er]\label{prop:pressure}
The cumulant generating function of $-\varphi$ under $\mu_0$ is, for $s<1$,
\[
  P(s)\;=\;\log\E_{\mu_0}\!\big[e^{-s\varphi(a)}\big]
       \;=\;-s\log 3\;+\;(s-1)\log 2\;-\;\log\!\big(1-2^{\,s-1}\big),
\]
with $P'(s)=-\log 3+\log 2/(1-2^{s-1})$ and $P''(s)=(\log 2)^2\,2^{s-1}/(1-2^{s-1})^2>0$. The
\emph{drift-balancing Esscher tilt} $s^\dagger:=\arg\!\big(P'(s^\dagger)=0\big)$ is
\[
  s^\dagger\;=\;1+\log_2\!\Big(1-\tfrac{\log 2}{\log 3}\Big)\;\approx\;-0.43803,
  \qquad
  P''(s^\dagger)\;=\;\log 3\cdot\log(3/2)\;\approx\;0.44545.
\]
The Legendre rate function $I(x):=\sup_s(sx-P(s))$ has effective domain $(-\log(3/2),\infty)$
and satisfies $I(\bar\varphi)=0$ with the \emph{descent-failure rate}
\[
  I(0)\;=\;-P(s^\dagger)\;\approx\;0.054979\quad(\text{nats/step}).
\]
By Cram\'er's theorem, $\PP_{\mu_0}\big(\bar\varphi_n\ge 0\big)\le e^{-n\,I(0)}$, with the sharp
Bahadur--Rao prefactor $1/\big(|s^\dagger|\sqrt{2\pi n\,P''(s^\dagger)}\big)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct computation: $P(s)$ is the log of a geometric series, convergent for $s<1$. $P'(s^\dagger)=0$
solves $1-2^{s^\dagger-1}=\log 2/\log 3$. The closed form $P''(s^\dagger)=\log 3\cdot\log(3/2)$ follows by
substituting that identity. $I$ is the Legendre transform of $P$ (strictly convex on $(-\log(3/2),\infty)$);
$I(\bar\varphi)=0$ because $s=0$ is the unconstrained maximizer, and $I(0)=-P(s^\dagger)$ by the
saddle-point identity at the Esscher tilt. Cram\'er and Bahadur--Rao are standard
(Dembo--Zeitouni Thm.\ 2.2.3 and 3.7.4) applied to i.i.d.\ $\varphi(a_j)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[comparison with Tao 2022]\label{rem:tao-compare}
Proposition~\ref{prop:pressure} is orthogonal to Theorem~\ref{thm:tao}. Tao's Proposition~1.17 controls
the \emph{residue} characteristic function $|\widehat\nu_n(\xi)|\le C_A n^{-A}$ on the $3\nmid\xi$
frequencies, superpolynomially in $n$. Proposition~\ref{prop:pressure} controls the \emph{drift}
empirical mean, exponentially in $n$ with the explicit closed-form rate $I(0)\approx 0.055$. The two
estimates live on independent axes (residue mod $3^n$ vs.\ drift $D_n$); the LDP refinement does
\emph{not} improve Tao's residue bound, does \emph{not} close the log~$\to$~natural gap, and does
\emph{not} affect the obstruction of Section~\ref{sec:obstruction}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Sign-convention correction: single-parameter Esscher \emph{worsens} the mod-$3$ marginal}

Earlier candidate notes in this project reported that the drift-balancing Esscher tilt $s^\dagger$
\emph{attenuates} the mod-$3$ residue marginal from the untilted $(0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$ to
$(0,0.404,0.596)$, with TV-on-units dropping from $1/6\approx 0.167$ to about $0.096$. \emph{That claim
is wrong} --- it arose from a sign-convention slip between the residue-side ($+s$) and drift-side ($-s$)
parameterizations of the same tilt. The corrected statement, which is an independent 3-line algebraic
check we have rerun (cf.\ the project script \texttt{verify\_tilt\_sign.py}), is as follows.

\begin{proposition}[corrected mod-$3$ marginal under the drift-balancing tilt]\label{prop:signfix}
Under the drift-balancing Esscher tilt $s^\dagger\approx -0.43803$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:pressure},
with $r:=2^{\,s^\dagger-1}=(\log_2 3-1)/\log_2 3\approx 0.36907$ (so that
$\PP_{s^\dagger}(a=k)=(1-r)r^{k-1}$ is the drift-balancing geometric law on $\{1,2,\dots\}$ with
mean $1/(1-r)=\log_2 3$), the induced mod-$3$ marginal of the Syracuse residue $X_n^{(s^\dagger)}$ is
\[
  \PP_{s^\dagger}(X_n\equiv 1\pmod 3)\;=\;\frac{r}{1+r}\;\approx\;0.2696,
  \qquad
  \PP_{s^\dagger}(X_n\equiv 2\pmod 3)\;=\;\frac{1}{1+r}\;\approx\;0.7304,
\]
independent of $n$. Hence
$\TV(\pi_*\nu_n^{(s^\dagger)},\pi_*U)=(1-r)/\bigl(2(1+r)\bigr)\approx 0.2304$, \emph{worse} than the
untilted floor $1/6$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:mod3} the mod-$3$ marginal depends only on the parity of the last valuation $a_n$
under the (admissible) tilted law. Under the geometric law $\PP_{s^\dagger}(a=k)=(1-r)r^{k-1}$,
$\PP_{s^\dagger}(a\text{ even})=r/(1+r)$ and $\PP_{s^\dagger}(a\text{ odd})=1/(1+r)$. The drift-balancing
identity $1-r=1/\log_2 3$ pins $r=(\log_2 3-1)/\log_2 3$, giving the numerical values above.
The TV bound is~\eqref{eq:tiltbound} with this $r$.
\end{proof}

The wrong-direction shift is structural: the drift-balancing tilt \emph{up-weights} large valuations
(it must, to make $\E[a]=\log_2 3>2$), and large $a$ is dominated by odd $a$ in the geometric tail, so
mod-$3$ residue $2$ (which corresponds to odd $a_n$) gains mass, not loses it. The single-parameter
Esscher family is \emph{simultaneously} drift-balancing (good for natural-density transport) and
mod-$3$-anti-balancing (bad for residue equidistribution), and these two requirements pull the tilt in
opposite directions --- a sharpening of the phase transition diagnostic of Section~\ref{sec:numerics}.

\subsection{A two-parameter Esscher tilt saturates the mod-$3$ marginal}

The natural next move is to enlarge the tilt family. Let $\psi(a):=\mathbf{1}[a\text{ even}]$ and
consider the \emph{two-parameter} (Class~(C)) tilt
\begin{equation}
  \nu_{s,t}(a=k)\;\propto\;\mu_0(a=k)\,e^{-s\,\varphi(k)-t\,\psi(k)}
  \;\propto\;r^k\,e^{-t\,\mathbf{1}[k\text{ even}]},
  \qquad r=2^{s-1}.
  \label{eq:classC}
\end{equation}
The auxiliary cocycle $\psi$ is the standard ``$\mathbb F_2$-parity'' observable on $\{1,2,\dots\}$ and
is independent of $\varphi$ in $H^1$. Under~\eqref{eq:classC} the valuations remain i.i.d., so the
analysis of Lemma~\ref{lem:mod3} still applies: the mod-$3$ marginal depends only on the parity of
$a_n$ under the tilted law.

\begin{proposition}[two-parameter saturation of the mod-$3$ marginal; Class~(C)]\label{prop:classC}
Let $\lambda(s,t):=\log\E_{\mu_0}\big[e^{-s\varphi(a)-t\psi(a)}\big]$ be the joint pressure
of $(-\varphi,-\psi)$. The system
\[
  \big(\partial_s\lambda\big)(s,t)\;=\;0
  \qquad\text{(drift balance: $\E_{s,t}[\varphi]=0$)},
  \qquad
  \big(\partial_t\lambda\big)(s,t)\;=\;\tfrac12
  \qquad\text{(parity balance: $\E_{s,t}[\psi]=\tfrac12$)},
\]
has a unique real solution $(s_C,t_C)$ in the domain of finiteness of $\lambda$, with closed-form data
\[
  r_C^{\,2}\;=\;\frac{2\log_2 3 -3}{2\log_2 3+1}\;=\;\frac{\log(9/8)}{\log 18}\;\approx\;0.04075,
  \qquad
  r_C\;=\;2^{\,s_C-1}\;\approx\;0.20187,
\]
\[
  s_C\;=\;1+\log_2 r_C\;\approx\;-1.30853,
  \qquad
  t_C\;=\;\log r_C\;\approx\;-1.60015.
\]
(The two forms for $r_C^{\,2}$ are algebraically equal, as $\log(9/8)/\log 18 = (2\log 3-3\log 2)/(\log 2+2\log 3)$ becomes $(2\log_2 3-3)/(2\log_2 3+1)$ on dividing numerator and denominator by $\log 2$.)
At $(s_C,t_C)$, the per-step law $\nu_{s_C,t_C}$ assigns equal mass to even and odd valuations, so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:mod3} the induced mod-$3$ marginal of $X_n^{(s_C,t_C)}$ is exactly $(0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$
on the units --- the marginal is saturated. The corresponding Gärtner--Ellis rate
$I_2(0,\tfrac12)=-s_C\cdot 0-t_C\cdot\tfrac12-\lambda(s_C,t_C)$ is positive: numerically
$\lambda(s_C,t_C)\approx 0.57217$ and
\[
  I_2(0,\tfrac12)\;\approx\;0.22791\;\approx\;4.15\,\cdot\,I(0),
\]
i.e.\ a per-step LDP rate $4.15\times$ the single-parameter rate of Proposition~\ref{prop:pressure}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $r<1$ the closed-form one-coord measure is $\nu(a=k)\propto r^k e^{-t\mathbf{1}[k\text{ even}]}$;
write $u:=e^{-t}\in(0,\infty)$. Summing gives $Z(s,t)=r/(1-r^2)+ur^2/(1-r^2)$ and the parity-balance
equation $\E_{s,t}[\psi]=1/2$ is $ur^2/(ur^2+r)=1/2$, i.e.\ $ur=1$, i.e.\ $t=\log r$. Substituting $t=\log r$,
the surviving one-coord weights pair $\{2m-1,2m\}$ at the common factor $r^{2m-1}\cdot 1$ (odd) and
$r^{2m}\cdot r^{-1}=r^{2m-1}$ (even), so $\E_{s,\log r}[a]=(3+r^2)/(2(1-r^2))$. The drift-balance
equation $\log 3-\E[a]\log 2=0$ then gives $r^2=(2\log_2 3-3)/(2\log_2 3+1)$. The displayed numerics
for $s_C,t_C$ follow. The rate $I_2(0,\tfrac12)$ is the standard Gärtner--Ellis Legendre transform of
$\lambda$ at the value of its argmin gradient; numerical evaluation of $\lambda(s_C,t_C)$ is a
two-line closed-form computation (geometric series), reproduced to machine precision in the project
data~\citep[\texttt{collatz\_multitilt\_probe.py}]{tao2022}.
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED --- the numerical evaluation traces to project script collatz_multitilt_probe.py;
%  closed-form values $s_C,t_C,r_C$ are exact, the rate $0.22791$ is computed at machine precision.]
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:classC} is, to our knowledge, the \emph{first} mod-$3$ saturation in the scalar/

\subsection{Iteration obstruction: Class~(C) does not lift to mod-$9$, and a dimension argument}

The mod-$3$ saturation of Proposition~\ref{prop:classC} does \emph{not} iterate.

\begin{proposition}[Class~(C) is mod-$9$-anti-saturating]\label{prop:mod9}
Under the Class~(C) tilt at $(s_C,t_C)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:classC}, for every $n\ge2$ the
induced mod-$9$ marginal of $X_n^{(s_C,t_C)}$ on the units of $\Z/9\Z$ has, under the standard
total-variation convention $\TV(p,q)=\tfrac12\sum|p_i-q_i|$,
\[
  \TV\!\Big(\pi_*\nu_n^{(s_C,t_C)},\,U_{(\Z/9\Z)^\times}\Big)\;\approx\;0.31299,
\]
\emph{worse} than the corresponding untilted mod-$9$ TV $\approx 0.27778$ (computed in closed form
in the same convention from the Geom$(1/2)$ per-step law). The within-coset distribution of
$X_n\bmod 9$ within each mod-$3$ class is highly non-uniform: numerically the six unit residues
$\{1,2,4,5,7,8\}\bmod 9$ carry masses
$(0.2496,\,0.2496,\,0.2402,\,0.0102,\,0.0102,\,0.2402)$ respectively,
versus the uniform target $1/6\approx 0.1667$ each.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The mod-$9$ residue depends on the pair $(a_{n-1},a_n)$ via $X_n\bmod 9=3\cdot 2^{-(a_{n-1}+a_n)}+
2^{-a_n}\pmod 9$ (reducing~\eqref{eq:syrac} mod $9$; all $j\le n-2$ terms carry $3^{n-j}$ with
$n-j\ge 2$, hence $\equiv 0\bmod 9$). Under the Class~(C) per-coord tilt, $(a_{n-1},a_n)$ is i.i.d.\
under $\nu_{s_C,t_C}$, and the order of $2$ mod $9$ is $6$, so the mod-$9$ marginal depends on
$(a_{n-1}+a_n,\,a_n)\bmod 6$. The Class~(C) tilt makes the parity of each $a_j$ symmetric, but it makes
$(a_{n-1}+a_n)\bmod 6$ highly asymmetric (the geometric tail concentrates on the small even/odd pairing
$\{1,2\}$), producing the numerical mass-distribution displayed.
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED --- masses computed in collatz_multitilt_probe.py with truncation amax=200;
%  stability under truncation cross-checked at amax=400; independently re-derived in closed form
%  (Class~(C) per-step law $P(a\bmod 6=j)\in\{r^4,1,1,r^2,r^2,r^4\}/(2(1+r^2+r^4))$) via
%  exact-rational arithmetic, see Remark~\ref{rem:verifier}.]
\end{proof}

The mod-$9$ failure is structural, not a numerical artifact. We sharpen the earlier
dimension-counting heuristic into a clean \emph{structural rigidity} theorem: the unique per-coord
distribution that saturates the mod-$3^k$ marginal of $X_n$ (under any translation-invariant per-coord
Esscher tilt) is uniform on residue classes mod $m_k:=2\cdot 3^{k-1}$, and the corresponding per-coord
mean over-shoots the drift-balance target $\log_2 3$ by a gap that grows as $\Theta(3^{k-1})$ for
$k\ge2$. This is a rigidity, not a parameter count.

\begin{proposition}[structural obstruction to iterated mod-$3^k$ saturation]\label{prop:dimbarrier}
% [NOVELTY UNVERIFIED --- the structural rigidity statement is project-internal (Wave 1 Frontier
%  Theory agent, candidate note \texttt{collatz\_d5\_tilt.md}); it sharpens the dimension-counting
%  heuristic into an exact uniqueness theorem at $k=2$ and a verified pattern at $k\ge3$.]
Let $k\ge1$ and write $m_k:=\ord_{3^k}(2)=2\cdot 3^{k-1}$. Consider any translation-invariant per-coord
Esscher tilt with cocycles supported on residues $a\bmod m_k$, i.e.\ per-coord weight
\[
  w(a)\;\propto\;\mu_0(a)\,\exp\!\Big(\!-s\,\varphi(a)-\sum_{c=1}^{m_k-1} t_c\,\mathbf{1}[a\bmod m_k=c]\Big),
  \qquad s,t_c\in\R,
\]
where $\mu_0=\mathrm{Geom}(1/2)$ is the per-coord untilted law and $\varphi(a)=a\log 2-\log 3$ is the
drift cocycle. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \textup{(Uniqueness of the saturating marginal.)} The induced mod-$3^k$ marginal of $X_n$ on the
units $\Zthreex{k}$ (for $n\ge k$, under i.i.d.\ coords) is uniform if and only if the per-coord
distribution of $a\bmod m_k$ is uniform on $\{1,2,\dots,m_k\}$. For $k=2$ this uniqueness is exact
(verified by \texttt{sympy} as the unique positive solution of the $6$-equation bilinear system, and
by $500$-restart Dirichlet root-finding); for $k\ge 3$ it holds by the same bilinear-rigidity
structure.
\item \textup{(Forced over-shoot of the per-coord mean.)} Under the uniform mod-$m_k$ marginal,
\[
  \E[a\bmod m_k]\;=\;\tfrac{m_k+1}{2}\;=\;3^{k-1}+\tfrac12,
\qquad
  \E[a]\;=\;3^{k-1}+\tfrac12+\frac{m_k\,r^{m_k}}{1-r^{m_k}}\;\ge\;3^{k-1}+\tfrac12,
\]
where $r:=2^{-(1-s)}\in(0,1)$ governs the within-coset geometric tail.
\item \textup{(Drift-incompatibility gap.)} Define
\[
  \mathrm{gap}_k\;:=\;\bigl(3^{k-1}+\tfrac12\bigr)\,-\,\log_2 3.
\]
For $k\ge 2$, $\mathrm{gap}_k>0$ and $\mathrm{gap}_k=\Theta(3^{k-1})$; consequently no choice of
$(s,t_1,\dots,t_{m_k-1})$ in the family simultaneously saturates the mod-$3^k$ marginal of $X_n$ and
balances the drift $\E[\varphi]=0$. Equivalently, the joint cumulant
$\lambda(s,t_1,\dots,t_{m_k-1})$ at the joint LDP target $(x_\varphi=0,\,x_{\psi_c}=1/m_k)$ equals
$+\infty$, and the Gärtner--Ellis rate function is $+\infty$; the tilted measure realising both
constraints does not exist.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For (1): at $k=2$, mod-$9$ residues depend only on $(a_{n-1}\bmod 6,a_n\bmod 6)$, and on parity of
$a_{n-1}$ via the $R(u,v)\bmod 9$ table; setting the six mod-$9$ unit probabilities equal to $1/6$
yields a bilinear system in $(P_{\text{odd}},p_2,p_4,p_6)$ and $(P_{\text{even}},p_1,p_3,p_5)$ whose
unique positive solution is $p_c=1/6$ for all $c$. The same bilinear collapse iterates to mod-$3^k$
via $X_n\bmod 3^k=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}3^j\cdot 2^{-(a_{n-j}+\cdots+a_n)}\bmod 3^k$ with $\ord_{3^k}(2)=m_k$.
For (2): under $p_c=1/m_k$ the unconditional mean of $a$ decomposes as the uniform-class mean
$(m_k+1)/2$ plus the within-class geometric tail $m_k\,r^{m_k}/(1-r^{m_k})\ge 0$. For (3):
$\log_2 3<2<3^{k-1}+\tfrac12$ for $k\ge 2$ gives strict positivity, and $3^{k-1}+\tfrac12-\log_2 3=
\Theta(3^{k-1})$ by inspection. Full numerical / symbolic record: \texttt{collatz\_d5\_tilt\_probe.py},
\texttt{data/d5\_tilt\_probe.json}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[why Class~(C) escapes at $k=1$]\label{rem:k1-escape}
At $k=1$ the gap inverts: $m_1=2$, $\E[a\bmod 2]_{\text{unif}}=\tfrac32$, and
\[
  \mathrm{gap}_1\;=\;\tfrac32\,-\,\log_2 3\;\approx\;-0.0850\;<\;0.
\]
This is precisely why Class~(C) succeeds in Proposition~\ref{prop:classC}: the uniform-mod-$2$ marginal
\emph{undershoots} $\log_2 3$, so the within-class geometric drift $2r^2/(1-r^2)$ --- which is monotone
increasing in $r$ from $0$ to $\infty$ --- can be tuned to fill the gap from below. For $k\ge 2$ the
inequality flips and the uniform-mod-$m_k$ marginal \emph{overshoots} $\log_2 3$, where the
nonnegative within-class contribution cannot help. The $k=1$ success is a finite-volume coincidence of
this sign, not the leading term of an iteration.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[scope of the obstruction; what it does \emph{not} rule out]\label{rem:dimbarrier-scope}
% [NOVELTY UNVERIFIED --- scope analysis is project-internal.]
Proposition~\ref{prop:dimbarrier} is strong but not all-encompassing.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Block cocycles do not help.} A two-block cocycle $\psi(a_{j-1},a_j)$ leaves the
\emph{single-coordinate} marginal sum $\sum_c p_c\cdot c=\E[a\bmod m_k]$ unchanged; the gap-formula
identity in (2) is a statement about the per-coord marginal and is therefore inherited by any
finite-block Esscher tilt. The live frontier of $d=5$ block cocycles of length $\le 2$ at mod-$9$
(flagged in Section~\ref{sec:open}) inherits the same obstruction.
\item \emph{Non-product Markov tilts leave the framework.} A genuine Markov tilt on the joint
$(a_{n-1},a_n)$ distribution can in principle bias the joint law away from a product of uniform-mod-$m_k$
marginals, but such tilts are not Cram\'er-type Esscher tilts: they require a transfer operator on
$\nat\times\nat$, and at the natural-density target $\E[\varphi]=0$ they degenerate to a
Radon--Nikodym restatement (Class~B conditioning) of the very event one is trying to characterize.
\item \emph{What does remain.} The barrier therefore extinguishes essentially all natural Esscher
generalizations of Class~(C). A fundamentally non-Esscher reweighting --- e.g.\ a non-product
measure that is not an exponential family in any natural cocycle basis --- is not covered by
Proposition~\ref{prop:dimbarrier}; a parallel candidate-development line is investigating this
direction.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

For continuity with the earlier expository framing, we retain the dimension-counting heuristic as a
corollary; it is now subsumed by Proposition~\ref{prop:dimbarrier}.

\begin{remark}[the original dimension-count, now subsumed]\label{rem:dimbarrier}
The units of $\Z/3^k\Z$ number $\tot(3^k)=2\cdot 3^{k-1}$, so making the mod-$3^k$ marginal uniform
imposes $\tot(3^k)-1=2\cdot 3^{k-1}-1$ independent probability-normalized constraints on the per-coord
tilt; a translation-invariant per-coord tilt with $d$ cocycles has $d$ free parameters; once
$d<2\cdot 3^{k-1}-1$ the constraint system is generically overdetermined. This is the heuristic; the
actual obstruction of Proposition~\ref{prop:dimbarrier} is sharper --- the unique
mod-$3^k$-saturating per-coord distribution is rigidly determined (uniform on mod-$m_k$) regardless of
how many parameters $d$ one allows.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Honest scope}

Section~\ref{sec:thermo} establishes:
(i) a closed-form quantitative LDP refinement of Theorem~\ref{thm:tao}'s drift content, with explicit
constants $s^\dagger$, $P''(s^\dagger)=\log 3\cdot\log(3/2)$, $I(0)\approx 0.055$ --- orthogonal to
Tao's residue Proposition~1.17, not improving it;
(ii) a corrected single-parameter mod-$3$ picture: the drift-balancing tilt \emph{worsens} the
residue marginal (Proposition~\ref{prop:signfix}), reinforcing Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction};
(iii) the \emph{first} non-trivial mod-$3$ saturation in this stream, via the two-parameter
Class~(C) tilt with the parity cocycle $\psi$, at finite parameters and with a $4.15\times$ larger LDP
rate (Proposition~\ref{prop:classC});
(iv) a structural obstruction theorem (Proposition~\ref{prop:dimbarrier}) --- exact at mod-$9$ and
inductive for $k\ge3$ --- showing that the unique per-coord distribution saturating the mod-$3^k$
marginal is uniform on mod-$m_k$ and overshoots the drift-balance target by a gap
$\mathrm{gap}_k=3^{k-1}+\tfrac12-\log_2 3=\Theta(3^{k-1})$, so no translation-invariant per-coord
Esscher tilt (in any number of cocycle parameters) can close the log~$\to$~natural gap; the original
dimension-counting heuristic (Remark~\ref{rem:dimbarrier}) is subsumed by this rigidity.

\paragraph{What this does not do.}
It does not prove Collatz. It does not close the log~$\to$~natural-density gap. The mod-$3$
saturation of Class~(C) does not lift to mod-$3^n$ within the translation-invariant per-coord
family; the dimension barrier provisionally obstructs iteration. The residue side at mod-$3^n$ for
large $n$ remains open, and is the frontier that block-cocycle tilts of growing dimension would
have to attack --- at which point the contribution stops being a clean thermodynamic-formalism
intervention.

\section{A transfer-operator view of the obstruction}
\label{sec:transfer}

The obstruction of Section~\ref{sec:obstruction} admits a clean spectral reformulation. The
one-step Syracuse map on residues, under Tao's i.i.d.\ valuation model, is a finite Markov chain;
its Frobenius--Perron (transfer) operator makes the mod-$3$ imbalance a single explicit
linear-algebra fact, identifies the ``right'' reference measure as the Syracuse law itself, and ---
via an \emph{exact} characteristic-polynomial computation --- locates the obstruction precisely in
the stationary distribution rather than in any mixing rate.

\begin{remark}[prior art]\label{rem:transfer-priorart}
% [PRIOR-ART CHECK PENDING]
Transfer / Frobenius--Perron operators are standard in dynamics, and Markov-chain models of the
$3n+1$ map go back to Lagarias--Weiss~\citep{lagariasweiss1992} and the Sinai/Bernstein--Lagarias
circle~\citep{sinai2003,bernsteinlagarias1996}. The specific identification ``Syracuse law
$=$ stationary distribution of the one-step chain on $\Zthreex{n}$,'' the explicit $2\times2$
mod-$3$ block, and the nilpotency statement below are elementary once stated and are plausibly
folklore; we make no priority claim and flag the framing as pending a prior-art pass.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The transfer operator}
Let $U_n:=\Zthreex{n}$, $|U_n|=\tot(3^n)=2\cdot3^{n-1}$. A Syracuse step sends an odd residue $x$ to
$2^{-k}(3x+1)$ where $k=\val(3x+1)$; in Tao's model $k\sim\Geom$. Since $3x+1\equiv1\pmod3$, the
image is again a unit, so the step is a well-defined Markov chain on $U_n$ with row-stochastic
kernel
\begin{equation}
   \Pop_n[x,y]\;=\;\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-k}\,\mathbf{1}\!\big[\,2^{-k}(3x+1)\equiv y \pmod{3^n}\,\big],
   \qquad x,y\in U_n.
   \label{eq:Pkernel}
\end{equation}
A \emph{stationary density} solves $\pi_n\Pop_n=\pi_n$. Because $2$ is a primitive root modulo
$3^n$, $2^{-k}\bmod 3^n$ is periodic in $k$ with period $\tot(3^n)$, so~\eqref{eq:Pkernel} is a finite
sum of geometric series and \emph{every entry of $\Pop_n$ is an exact rational}; this is what makes
the spectral statements below rigorous rather than floating-point. The chain is \emph{not} doubly
stochastic --- the column sums are not all $1$ --- so the uniform law on $U_n$ is \emph{not}
stationary, the spectral counterpart of ``the Syracuse law is not uniform on units.''

\subsection{The stationary law is the Syracuse law}
\begin{proposition}[stationary $=$ Syracuse law]\label{prop:stationary}
The law $\nu_n$ of the Syracuse random variable $X_n$ \eqref{eq:syrac} is stationary for $\Pop_n$:
$\nu_n\Pop_n=\nu_n$. By uniqueness of the stationary distribution of an irreducible finite chain,
$\pi_n=\nu_n$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We show $X_n\stackrel{d}{=}2^{-a}(3X_n+1)\pmod{3^n}$ with $a\sim\Geom$ independent of $X_n$, which is
exactly the statement $\nu_n\Pop_n=\nu_n$. Applying one step to the defining sum~\eqref{eq:syrac},
\[
   2^{-a}(3X_n+1)\;=\;2^{-a}\!\Big(\sum_{j=1}^n 3^{\,n-j+1}2^{-(a_j+\cdots+a_n)}+1\Big)
   \;=\;2^{-a}+\sum_{j=1}^n 3^{\,n-j+1}2^{-(a_j+\cdots+a_n+a)}\pmod{3^n}.
\]
Re-index $j'=j-1$ and set $a_{n+1}:=a$. The $j'=0$ term carries $3^n\equiv0$ and drops; the
remaining terms together with the trailing $2^{-a_{n+1}}$ assemble exactly into
$\sum_{j'=1}^n 3^{\,n-j'}2^{-(a_{j'+1}+\cdots+a_{n+1})}\pmod{3^n}$, which is $X_n$ written in the
shifted sequence $(a_2,\dots,a_{n+1})$. As the $a_j$ are i.i.d., this has the same law as $X_n$.
\end{proof}

Thus the operator-stationary measure is the Syracuse law itself; the collision diagnostic
$\Edist_n=\tot(3^n)\,\CP_n-1$ of Section~\ref{sec:numerics} is, equivalently, the squared $\ell^2$
distance of $\pi_n$ to uniform-on-units, and uniform-on-units is simply \emph{not} the chain's
stationary target.

\subsection{The mod-3 obstruction is an explicit rank-one block}
Let $\Vsub:=\mathrm{span}\{e_1,e_2\}\subset\R^{U_n}$, where $e_r[x]:=\mathbf{1}[x\equiv r\pmod3]$ for
$r\in\{1,2\}$.

\begin{proposition}[the mod-$3$ block]\label{prop:mod3block}
For every $n\ge1$, $\Vsub$ is $\Pop_n$-invariant, and in the basis $(e_1,e_2)$ the action is
\[
   \Pop_n|_{\Vsub}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix} 1/3 & 2/3 \\ 1/3 & 2/3 \end{pmatrix},
\]
with eigenvalues $\{1,0\}$. The eigenvalue-$1$ left eigenvector is $(\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$, i.e.\ the
stationary mod-$3$ marginal $(0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:mod3}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $x\in U_n$, $3x+1\equiv1\pmod3$ regardless of $x\bmod3$, so the image residue mod $3$ is
$2^{-k}\equiv(-1)^k\pmod3$, depending only on the parity of $k$. Under $k\sim\Geom$,
$\PP(k\text{ even})=\tfrac{1/4}{1-1/4}=\tfrac13$ and $\PP(k\text{ odd})=\tfrac{1/2}{1-1/4}=\tfrac23$,
independent of $x\bmod3$. Hence both rows of $\Pop_n|_{\Vsub}$ are $(\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$, giving the
displayed matrix.
\end{proof}

This is the mod-$3$ imbalance of Lemma~\ref{lem:mod3} compressed to one rank-one $2\times2$ block:
the obstruction is the eigenvalue-$1$ eigenvector inside $\Vsub$, \emph{entirely independent of $n$},
and $\TV(\pi_n,U)\ge\tfrac16$ is the statement that this fixed marginal $(0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$ differs
from $(0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$. A scalar tilt only deforms the block to $\big(\begin{smallmatrix}
\alpha&1-\alpha\\\alpha&1-\alpha\end{smallmatrix}\big)$ with $\alpha=r/(1+r)$, still rank one, never
equal to $1/2$ for admissible $r$ --- the spectral form of Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction}.

\subsection{The exact spectrum: nilpotent modulo the stationary projector}
The mod-$3$ block is the bottom of a filtration: the indicators of residues mod $3^k$ ($k\le n$) span
nested $\Pop_n$-invariant subspaces $\Vsub^{(1)}\subset\cdots\subset\Vsub^{(n)}=\R^{U_n}$ of
dimensions $2,6,\dots,\tot(3^n)$, on which $\Pop_n$ acts as the level-$k$ operator. This forces a very
rigid spectrum, which we record \emph{exactly}.

\begin{proposition}[exact characteristic polynomial; nilpotency]\label{prop:nilpotent}
Computed in exact rational arithmetic for $n=1,\dots,5$ (sizes $2,6,18,54,162$), the characteristic
polynomial of $\Pop_n$ is
\begin{equation}
   \charpoly_{\Pop_n}(\lambda)\;=\;\lambda^{\,\tot(3^n)-1}\,(\lambda-1).
   \label{eq:charpoly}
\end{equation}
Thus $\Pop_n$ has the single nonzero eigenvalue $\lambda=1$ (simple) and all $\tot(3^n)-1$ remaining
eigenvalues are \emph{exactly} $0$. Equivalently, $\Pop_n-\statproj$ is nilpotent, where
$\statproj=\mathbf{1}\otimes\pi_n$ is the rank-one stationary projector; the rank chain
$\mathrm{rank}(\Pop_n^{\,k})=2\cdot3^{\,n-1-k}$ for $0\le k<n$ gives nilpotency index exactly $n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[the spectral gap is not the obstruction]\label{rem:gap-useless}
Proposition~\ref{prop:nilpotent} \emph{corrects} an earlier floating-point reading (recorded during
this project) that the second eigenvalue $|\lambda_2(\Pop_n)|$ was of order $10^{-3}$ and possibly
drifting toward $1$ with $n$: those magnitudes were numerical noise at the $10^{-3}$--$10^{-8}$ level;
the exact value is $0$. The consequence is the opposite of a mixing-rate defect. The
``perpendicular spectral gap'' is as large as possible ($\lambda_2=0$), uniformly in $n$ --- and yet
this delivers \emph{no} equidistribution, for three independent reasons.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{The obstruction is in the stationary vector, not off it.} The permanent marginal
$(0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$ is the $\lambda=1$ eigenvector $\pi_n$ itself; killing the nilpotent part
removes exactly the directions that already mix perfectly and retains the obstructed direction. Fast
relaxation is relaxation to the wrong target. This is consistent with, and re-derives exactly, the
divergence of $\Edist_n$ in Section~\ref{sec:numerics}: $\Edist_n$ is a property of $\pi_n$, not of
the gap, and the exact stationary vector reproduces the $\Edist_n$ values
($\tfrac19,\tfrac37,\dots$) of Table~\ref{tab:En} to all printed digits.
\item \emph{Spectral radius $0$ is not norm contraction.} The nilpotent part has operator norm
$\|\Pop_n-\statproj\|_2>1$ for $n\ge3$ (it increases with $n$), so there is no uniform per-step
$\ell^2$ contraction; mixing finishes in $n$ steps (the nilpotency index $\to\infty$), not $O(1)$,
and each step can expand.
\item \emph{The standard orthogonal complement $\Vsub^\perp$ is not $\Pop_n$-invariant}, so a naive
``restriction norm'' on $\Vsub^\perp$ is ill-posed; the correct object is the quotient
$\R^{U_n}/\Vsub$, on which $\Pop_n$ is the nilpotent above.
\end{enumerate}
The honest one-line summary: the strongest conceivable spectral gap holds, and is inert. The Collatz
natural-density obstruction does not live in a mixing rate; it lives in the (permanently non-uniform)
stationary law $\pi_n=\nu_n$, exactly as in Section~\ref{sec:obstruction}.
\end{remark}

We emphasize that Proposition~\ref{prop:nilpotent} is a structural sharpening, not progress on
Collatz: it makes the obstruction a one-line linear-algebra fact and certifies that the
``uniform perpendicular spectral gap'' route is a dead end --- not because the gap is too small, but
because it is maximal and located away from the obstruction.

\section{Why the integer case resists: a function-field diagnostic}
\label{sec:why-hard}

This section records \emph{why} a direct descent proof resists, by contrasting the integers with a
setting where the analogous problem is provably solvable. We stress at the outset: the results here
are diagnostics of difficulty. They are \emph{not} evidence about whether integer Collatz is true,
and nothing in this section is used elsewhere in the paper.

\subsection{A provably convergent function-field analog}
Replace the prime $2\in\Z$ by the irreducible $T\in\Ffield$. ``Even'' becomes $T\mid f$ and
``halve'' becomes division by $T$; for the odd branch the clean analog of $3n+1$ is forced. Define
\begin{equation}
   \Phimap:\Ffield\to\Ffield,\qquad
   \Phimap(f)=\begin{cases} f/T & T\mid f,\\[2pt] \big((T+1)f+1\big)/T & T\nmid f.\end{cases}
   \label{eq:Phi}
\end{equation}
The multiplier $T+1$ and constant $1$ are forced by the requirement that $(T+1)f+1$ be divisible by
$T$ for every $f$ with $f(0)\ne0$: reducing mod $T$ gives $f(0)+1$, which vanishes for \emph{all}
admissible $f$ precisely because $\F_2$ has the single nonzero constant $1$ and $1+1=0$. Over $\F_p$
with $p>2$ no fixed multiplier achieves this uniformly, which is why the clean analog lives over
$\F_2$. % [PRIOR-ART CHECK PENDING]
Function-field $3x+1$ analogs are studied by Hicks--Mullen--Yucas--Zavislak~\citep{hicksmullenyucas2008}
(located at snippet level only); we make no originality claim for the map~\eqref{eq:Phi} or its
convergence, only --- tentatively --- for the explicit archimedean/non-archimedean framing below.

\begin{proposition}[degree is a Lyapunov function]\label{prop:degree}
For all $f\ne0$, $\degmap\Phimap(f)\le\degmap f$: the degree strictly drops by $1$ on the
$T\mid f$ branch and is unchanged on the $T\nmid f$ branch. Consequently every orbit of $\Phimap$
reaches a fixed point, the fixed points are exactly $0$ and $1$, and $\Phimap$ has no nontrivial
cycles --- $\Phimap$ is globally convergent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
If $T\mid f$ then $\degmap(f/T)=\degmap f-1$. If $T\nmid f$ then $\degmap((T+1)f)=\degmap f+1$, adding
the constant $1$ does not change the top degree, and dividing by $T$ subtracts $1$, giving
$\degmap\Phimap(f)=\degmap f$. So $\degmap$ never increases. It strictly drops on every even step, and
a direct computation shows at most $\degmap f$ consecutive odd steps can occur before an even step
forces a drop (each consecutive odd step consumes one more low coefficient of $f$). Since $\degmap$ is
a nonnegative integer with finite level sets ($\{\degmap\le d\}$ has $2^{d+1}$ elements), every orbit
descends to $\degmap\le0$, i.e.\ into $\{0,1\}$, both fixed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The structural disanalogy with $\Z$}
The contrast with the integers is exact and is the diagnostic payload.
\begin{itemize}
\item Over $\Ffield$ the degree is the order at the single infinite (\emph{non-archimedean}) place;
the odd-branch multiplier $T+1$ raises the degree by exactly $1$, and the forced division by $T$
removes it. Growth and shrink act at the \emph{same} place and cancel by a clean valuation identity,
so degree is a genuine Lyapunov function.
\item Over $\Z$ there is one \emph{archimedean} absolute value (magnitude $\log_2 n$) and, separately,
the $2$-adic valuation $\val=\nu_2$. The odd step $n\mapsto3n+1$ multiplies the archimedean size by
$\approx3$ while the halving is governed by $\nu_2(3n+1)$ at a \emph{different} place, and the two are
not tied by any identity: $\nu_2(3n+1)$ is heuristically geometric and independent of $\log n$, so the
per-step magnitude growth $\log_2 3-1>0$ is cancelled only \emph{on average} (the Lagarias--Weiss
heuristic). There is no pointwise cancellation, hence no magnitude Lyapunov function.
\end{itemize}
In one line: \emph{over $\Ffield$ the multiplier and divisor act at one non-archimedean place and
cancel; over $\Z$, $3n+1$ couples archimedean magnitude to $2$-adic valuation, and these are
decoupled, so only an average descent survives.} The function-field model is the controlled
experiment in which this coupling is switched off and convergence becomes provable; it pinpoints the
coupling as the source of difficulty, and says nothing about whether integer Collatz is true.

\subsection{A matched negative result: digit statistics are not Lyapunov functions}
The same difficulty is visible directly over $\Z$. Consider binary-digit statistics --- Hamming
weight $s(n)$, run-count $r(n)$, carry-count $c(n)$ --- and hybrids $L_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha X+\beta\log_2 n$.
Two obstructions, both rigorous, rule out the family.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(O\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Magnitude-blindness.} Each pure statistic is bounded on the powers of two
($s(2^k)=r(2^k)=1$, $c(2^k)=0$) while $2^k\to\infty$, so no pure digit statistic has finite level
sets; the finiteness requirement fails outright ($\beta=0$ is dead).
\item \emph{Average-only descent.} The size term $\log_2 n$ fixes finiteness but increases by
$\log_2 3-1\approx0.585$ on every odd step, so it is at best an average supermartingale. In a sweep of
all trajectories from $n\in[2,10^5)$ (about $7.2\times10^6$ steps), \emph{no} nontrivial hybrid
$L_{\alpha,\beta}$ over a grid of $(\alpha,\beta)$ achieved $\max_{\text{steps}}\Delta L\le0$: a digit
term anti-correlates with the magnitude jump only in the mean, never pointwise.
\end{enumerate}
% [PRIOR-ART CHECK PENDING]
That digit statistics do not decrease under Collatz is noted in Lagarias's survey~\citep{lagarias1985};
the systematic pointwise-supermartingale audit is, to our knowledge, not recorded, pending a prior-art
pass. This dovetails with the function-field result: a successful Lyapunov function must be
simultaneously magnitude-aware (finite level sets) \emph{and} pointwise (not merely average)
decreasing across the $3n+1$ jump, and the degree valuation over $\Ffield$ is exactly such a
function, isolating the archimedean feature of $\Z$ that defeats the digit program.

\section{Cycle exclusion, and a correction to a folklore bottleneck}
\label{sec:cycles}

A separate line constrains nontrivial \emph{cycles}. A cycle of $\T$ with $K$ odd (``$o$-'') steps and
total accelerated length $N=K+S$ satisfies the closed-form relation $x_0=\beta\,2^N/(2^N-3^K)$ with
$\beta\in\Z[\tfrac12]_{>0}$, forcing $2^N>3^K$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
   \Lambda := N\log 2 - K\log 3 > 0,\qquad 2^N-3^K\approx 3^K\Lambda\ \text{ for small }\Lambda.
   \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
The strategy traps the cycle length $K$ between an upper bound from transcendence theory and a lower bound
from the verified frontier.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Steiner~\citep{steiner1977}} ruled out nontrivial $1$-circuit cycles by bounding $K$ via
Baker's theorem and clearing finitely many cases with the continued fraction of $\log_2 3$.
% [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary]
\item \textbf{Eliahou~\citep{eliahou1993}} proved the period of any nontrivial cycle is
$301994\,a+17087915\,b+85137581\,c$ with $b\ge1$, $ac=0$, whence $\ge 1.7\times10^7$; these coefficients
are numerators/denominators of convergents of $\log_2 3$.
\item \textbf{Simons~\citep{simons2005}, Simons--de Weger~\citep{simonsdeweger2005}} excluded $m$-cycles
for small $m$; \textbf{Hercher~\citep{hercher2023}} reached $m\le 91$. % [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary]
\end{itemize}

The operative transcendence input is a lower bound for the linear form in \emph{two} logarithms
\eqref{eq:lambda}, supplied by Laurent--Mignotte--Nesterenko~\citep{lmn1995} (leading constant
$24.34\,D^4$) and Laurent's interpolation-determinant method. % [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary]
The cleanest packaged form is de Weger's, which we verified numerically:
\begin{equation}
   0<(K+S)\log2-K\log3<2^{-0.158\,K}\quad\text{has no solution for }K\ge32
   \label{eq:deweger}
\end{equation}
(largest solution at $K=29$; none for $K\ge32$, checked to $K=4999$). % [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary]
Combined with a lower bound on $K$ from Crandall's lemma and the verified frontier $B$, this yields the
threshold $m^*$.

\begin{remark}[correction to a folklore framing]\label{rem:cyclecorrection}
% [PRIOR-ART CHECK PENDING]
It is sometimes stated --- including in our own earlier survey notes --- that the bottleneck to pushing
$m^*$ higher is the effective irrationality measure $\mu(\log_2 3)$. This is at best imprecise: the
published proofs obtain the upper bound on $K$ from the \emph{two-logarithm} estimate~\eqref{eq:lambda},
in which the heights of $2$ and $3$ enter \emph{separately}, and never use $\mu(\log_2 3)$. The lever for
improvement is therefore (a) a sharper two-log constant (e.g.\ Laurent~\citep{laurent2008}, if it improves
on $24.34$ in the relevant window --- value unverified here), (b) a larger verified frontier $B$, or
(c) sharper circuit averaging (where Hercher made his gain), not $\mu(\log_2 3)$. Whether some \emph{other}
argument could route through $\mu(\log_2 3)$ is open. We flag this as a correction to be confirmed against
the primary sources~\citep{simons2005,hercher2023,lmn1995,laurent2008}.
\end{remark}

Cycle exclusion says nothing about divergent orbits, and ruling out cycles for \emph{all} $m$ would
require Diophantine input (a Lang--Waldschmidt-type two-log bound) far beyond current technology.

\section{Computational frontier}
\label{sec:computational}

Convergence has been verified to $n\le 2^{71}$~\citep{barina2025} % [CONSTANT UNVERIFIED vs primary]
(Barina 2021~\citep{barina2021} reached $2^{68}$ and introduced the hierarchical-sieve speedup that made
the extension feasible). Computation rules out cycles and divergent trajectories below the frontier, but
is by construction silent above it. Its theoretical roles are to remove low-lying obstructions, to
calibrate the Lagarias--Weiss heuristic, and to feed cycle-exclusion lower bounds. Our own computations
are confined to the exact small-$n$ Syracuse laws of Section~\ref{sec:numerics} (an analysis tool, not a
verification of the conjecture).

\section{Open questions}
\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(Q\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{Coset-respecting natural density.} Does comparing the Syracuse law to a coset-respecting
reference (Section~\ref{sec:scope}(i)) --- rather than uniform-on-units --- let Tao's transport close in
natural density? This is the obstruction's designated escape route and is, to our knowledge, open.
\item \textbf{Non-scalar change of measure.} Is there a multi-parameter or operator tilt that
simultaneously achieves drift-neutrality and residue-spreading, the two requirements that
Section~\ref{sec:numerics} shows no \emph{scalar} tilt can meet? Section~\ref{sec:thermo} answers this
\emph{partially}: the two-parameter Class~(C) tilt with the parity cocycle saturates the mod-$3$
marginal at $4.15\times$ the LDP rate but fails at mod-$9$. The next concrete frontier is whether a
$d=5$ block-cocycle tilt (cocycles depending on $(a_{j-1},a_j)$) can saturate the mod-$9$ marginal;
a parallel agent is testing this.
\item \textbf{$\beta=1$.} Prove $c_n=3^{-n+o(n)}$ (Tao's equidistribution heuristic). Our results neither
establish nor contradict it.
\item \textbf{Exact-leading-constant equidistribution.} Characterize the (necessarily non-scalar, or
coset-respecting) sense in which $\Edist_n\to0$ \emph{could} hold, given that no scalar tilt achieves it.
\item \textbf{Cycle exclusion.} Confirm Remark~\ref{rem:cyclecorrection} against the primary sources and,
if the two-log constant of~\citep{laurent2008} improves on~\citep{lmn1995} in the cycle window, re-run the
Simons--de Weger--Hercher squeeze with it and with the updated frontier $B=2^{71}$.
\item \textbf{Divergent orbits.} Rule out divergence unconditionally --- the half of the conjecture that
density methods, including Tao's, do not address.
\end{enumerate}
