# Chapter 3. Internal and External Observation

## The phenomenon

A person observes two realities: an internal world of their own states, thoughts, and reactions,
and an external world of everything outside them. This system measures how much of each a person
registers, how clearly, and which they trust when the two would lead to different decisions. It
governs what a person takes as evidence.

We separate two things that are easy to run together: how well a person *observes* a world, and
which world they *rely on* to decide. They come apart. A person can have a rich and accurate view
of their internal world and still prefer, when choosing, to lean on the external one. Observation
is a talent of registration; reliance is a preference. The theory measures both, and keeps them
distinct.

## The measures

For each world, internal and external, we measure two properties, and then a fifth measure spans
both.

**1. Scale (internal).** How much of the internal field a person registers: the breadth and
magnitude of inner states they notice at all.

**2. Fidelity (internal).** The resolution and accuracy of that registration: how finely and how
truly a person reads the inner states they do notice.

**3. Scale (external).** How much of the external field a person takes in: the breadth of the
outside world they register.

**4. Fidelity (external).** The resolution and accuracy of that external registration: how finely
and how truly they read what they take in.

Scale and fidelity are different. A person can register a vast field at low resolution, or a
narrow field at high resolution. Recording them separately is what lets the theory describe, for
instance, someone who notices a great deal about other people but reads little of it accurately,
against someone who notices few people but reads those few with precision.

**5. The reliance preference.** When the internal and external worlds would point to different
decisions, which one a person trusts. This is the preference that the talent measures do not
capture, and it is often where the surprises are: a person with high internal scale and fidelity
who nevertheless decides by the external world.

Measures one through four describe a talent of observation and so brush against capacity; we hold
the preference line by recording what a person *habitually attends to*, not the maximum they could
attend to under instruction. The fifth measure is preference outright.

## The decision rule

Faced with a stimulus, a person observes it through whichever world they are disposed to attend
to, at their scale and fidelity, and when the two worlds conflict, the reliance preference breaks
the tie. A person of high internal fidelity and external reliance will notice their own reaction
clearly and then set it aside in favor of outside evidence. Reading such a person only by their
reliance would miss the inner life; reading them only by their observation would mispredict the
decision.

## The strategy layer

This system has a lighter strategy layer than the social energy economy. The nearest thing to a
strategy is calibration: a person can learn to weight the world they under-trust, or to widen a
narrow scale deliberately for a decision that warrants it. We note the possibility and leave its
development to a later pass.

## Illustrations

- High internal scale and fidelity, modest external scale, internal reliance. A person who lives
  by a clear and detailed inner read and decides from it.
- Modest internal scale, high external scale and fidelity, external reliance. A person tuned
  outward who decides from sharp observation of the world and rarely consults their own state.
- High internal fidelity, high external fidelity, external reliance. A person who reads both
  worlds well and, on conflict, defers to the outside one anyway.

## Open questions

- Are scale and fidelity truly independent, or does very high scale cost fidelity?
- Is reliance a single preference, or does it shift by domain (trusting the inner world for
  values, the outer for facts)?
- Does fidelity here entangle with the formal-thinking preference of the next chapter?
