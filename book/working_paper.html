A New Theory of Personality

Working paper · with Steven Pham

The idea in one line: personality is the set of preferences — stated and revealed — that steer how a person meets the world; we measure them so the person can understand themselves, never so anyone can judge them.

1What this measures — and what it refuses to

A person can be described in three separate lanes. This framework lives in exactly one.

PreferenceWHAT YOU TEND TOWARD
this framework — likes, leanings, tendencies
CapabilityWHAT YOU CAN DO
separate, established instruments
PathologyWHAT'S CLINICALLY WRONG
qualified clinicians only

If a reading here ever resembles a skill score or a symptom, the resemblance is not a claim. Nothing in this framework says what you're good at or whether anything is wrong with you.

2Six rules everything obeys

🔮No predictionWe never forecast what you'll do. Forecasts handed to young people become self-fulfilling.
📸Snapshot, not verdictEvery reading describes one day. People change; retakes build a moving portrait.
⚖️No good or bad polesEvery measure is two-sided and neutral. A shift under pressure is a change of instruments, not a breakdown.
🤫Clean or silentA gap or shift appears only if the data can stand behind it. No shaky numbers with caveats.
🚪Opens doors onlyResults may point you toward what fits. They may never rank, sort, screen, or exclude anyone.
🔒Yours aloneOffered only in our own program. Never shared with schools or employers. Minors' results expire.

Neither stream is the "truer you." A gap is expected and judgment-free: it's the distance between the self you describe and the self one day's evidence showed. Because people are plastic and trainable, that distance can move — if you decide it should.

4The four systems

Personality splits into four separable systems. Each is a lens, not a score.

System 1

Social Energy Economy

Gainwhere energy comes from — which company charges you, which drains
→
Spendwhich interactions you choose — few & deep vs many & light
→
Riskhow much standing you'll stake — raise the hard thing, or protect the peace

Read from a reconstructed real day and staked choices — not from "are you an introvert?"

System 2 · the centerpiece

The Thinking Machine

Where you reach when you think (your own compass vs outside facts), how you build the thought (feel vs structure) — read twice: on instinct, and given time. Then the stakes go up, and we watch whether you move.

One person's example: at low stakes they read the room by feel; as stakes rise they move toward their own principles, with structure. The path is the reading — and a shift is drawn only when it's big enough to trust. Moving is not worse than staying put; they're different styles.

7What we still owe (before anyone calls this validated)

  • Discrimination study — does a person's own profile fit them better than a stranger's profile does? If not, the readings are horoscope-generic and we say so.
  • Format-swap check — does a gap survive when the "said" and "did" sides are asked the same way? A gap that vanishes was measuring the questionnaire, not the person.
  • Stated-stream check — are the "wants" people pick stable across phrasings and a short interval, or one-off stories?
