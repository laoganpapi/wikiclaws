# Architecture and Working Spine

This is the working document for the book, not part of the manuscript. It records the
decisions that govern the theory, the template every chapter follows, and the register of
systems we have adopted or are still considering. Edit this first when a decision changes;
the chapters follow from it.

## Authors

A new theory of personality. With Steven Pham.

## Deliverable sequence

1. The book (current focus).
2. A website.
3. An application that administers the theory as an instrument.

Consequence for the writing: every system must be defined so that its measures are
operationalizable. A later instrument should be able to read a chapter and know what it is
asking a person and what it does with the answer. We do not have to build the instrument now,
but we may not define a measure we could not, in principle, administer.

## Core definitions

- **Personality** is a set of preferences, stated or revealed, that govern how an individual
  makes decisions and responds to any and every stimulus in their conscious existence.
- Personality is **not static**. Experience and environment change it. The theory measures a
  person at a time, not for life.
- The theory is **agnostic to nature versus nurture**. The origin of a preference does not
  change what the preference is or how it operates now.
- Personality is **distinct from intelligence**. Intelligence concerns capacity. This theory
  concerns preference. They are measured separately and should not be confused.

## Design axioms

1. **Preference, not ability.** Every measure records what a person prefers to do, not what
   they are capable of doing. This is the line that separates the theory from intelligence
   research, and it must hold in every system. Where ability and preference are hard to
   separate, the chapter must say how the measure isolates preference.
2. **De novo.** The manuscript presents the theory on its own terms. It does not name, map to,
   or argue against existing typologies. (See the novelty watch below, which is internal only.)
3. **Open and extensible.** The set of systems is not closed. We adopt new systems as we
   uncover them. The architecture must accommodate a system we have not yet written.
4. **Instrumentable.** See the deliverable sequence. No measure without a conceivable
   administration.
5. **Stated versus revealed.** Preferences come in two forms. Where they diverge, the theory
   privileges revealed preference for prediction and treats the gap between stated and revealed
   as itself informative.

## The system template

Every chapter that defines a system follows this structure. Uniformity is the point: it is what
makes four essays into one theory, and it is what the later instrument will read.

1. **The phenomenon.** What part of decision and response this system governs.
2. **The measures.** The axes along which people vary, each defined by what you would observe.
   State whether each measure records preference or, where unavoidable, capacity.
3. **The decision rule.** How the measures resolve into an actual decision when a person is
   faced with a stimulus. This is where stated and revealed preference are reconciled.
4. **The strategy layer.** The dynamic, interactional behavior the system produces over time,
   modeled as a game where that framing earns its keep. Not every system has a rich strategy
   layer; say so when it does not.
5. **Illustrations.** Two or three concrete profiles that are distinct on this system alone.
6. **Open questions.** What is unresolved, so the next pass knows where to dig.

| - | Regulation and order | Candidate | Disposition toward structure, follow-through, control |

Candidates are not yet written and not yet committed to. They are parking spots for dimensions
we have noticed but not developed. The "external cause" of Chapter 1 strongly implies a
motivation system; that is the most likely next adoption.

## Open structural questions

- Are the systems independent, or do some constrain others? The theory currently treats them as
  separate axes. We have not tested whether, for example, formal-thinking preference and
  observation fidelity are entangled.
- What is the timescale of a measurement? If personality is non-static, the instrument needs a
  stated horizon (this month, this year, this role) over which a reading is meant to hold.
- Does every system get a strategy layer, or only some? Currently only the social energy economy
  is modeled as a full game.

## Internal: novelty watch (NOT for the manuscript)

Kept only so we do not reinvent a known construct under a new name while building de novo. None
of this appears in the book.

- The social energy economy's "how one gains energy" input overlaps with a well-known
  energy-direction axis; our differentiator is the game and strategy layer, so keep that load
  bearing.
- "Preference for formal thinking" is close to a known need-for-cognition construct; our
  differentiator is that we measure preference to *formalize and communicate*, not appetite for
  thinking as such. Keep the communicability requirement explicit.
- "Internal and external observation" overlaps with a known perception axis; our differentiator
  is splitting scale from fidelity and separating both from the reliance preference.

## Style

- Declarative and precise. No em dashes. Semicolons, colons, commas, parentheses are fine.
- Define before you use. A term gets one definition and is used consistently after.
- Claims the authors have not settled go in Open Questions, not in the body as if settled.
