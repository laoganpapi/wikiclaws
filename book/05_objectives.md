# Chapter 5. Objectives

## The phenomenon

This system is the external cause itself. The other systems describe *how* a person operates;
Objectives describes *toward what*. It is the system that gives the theory the point we claimed for
it in Chapter 1: personality understood in the service of pursuing something outside oneself.
Without it, the rest of the framework would be a fine description of a mechanism with no account
of where the mechanism is pointed.

Because what motivates a person is not well captured by a slider, this system is measured
differently from the others. It begins with an open prompt: the person says, in their own words,
what they are working toward, what they want, what would count as a life well spent. The richness
of motivation is preserved in the response, and the structure is applied afterward.

## The measures

The open response is coded into a **motive profile**, a weighted set over the following motives.
The motives are not mutually exclusive; most people carry several, and the profile records their
relative weight rather than forcing a single label.

- **Order and peace.** The drive toward stability, harmony, and the absence of conflict.
- **Triumph.** The drive to win, to prevail in contest, to come out ahead.
- **Power and influence.** The drive to move other people and outcomes, to have one's will felt
  beyond oneself, and to hold things in one's own hands rather than depend on others. This motive
  absorbs what an earlier draft separated as control; the wish to command one's circumstances and
  the wish to move other people proved to be one drive pointed at different objects.
- **Truth-seeking.** The drive to know what is actually the case, to understand for its own sake.
- **Novelty.** The drive toward the new, the unexplored, the not-yet-tried.

Coding an open response into this profile is a task an AI can do at scale, and the coding must be
auditable: the system should be able to show which phrases in a person's own words drove each
weight. As with all such coding, it must be checked so that it reads motivation and not
eloquence.

## The decision rule

When a person faces a choice among ends, the motive profile describes which end they tend to reach for.
Where two motives conflict within a person, the heavier weight tends to govern, though context can
invert this, and the gap between what a person states they are motivated by and what their choices
reveal is, here as elsewhere, itself informative. A person who states truth-seeking but
consistently chooses the winning side over the correct one is read by the choices.

## The dynamic layer

Objectives is the system most likely to be stable over short horizons and to shift over long ones,
as a person's life stage changes. For the assessed population, high school through early career,
this matters: a motive profile taken at seventeen is a reading of seventeen, and the instrument
should say so. The profile also conditions the other systems. A strong Triumph or Power motive
will load the social energy economy's risk appetite and the thinking machine's stakes; a strong
Order motive will tend to damp both. The objective is the thing for which the rest of the machinery
is run.

## Illustrations

- Heavy on Truth-seeking and Novelty, light on Power. Oriented to understand and to explore, with
  little pull toward moving other people; will follow a question past where it is useful to anyone
  else.
- Heavy on Power and influence and Triumph. Oriented to prevail and to be felt; reads contests as
  the main game and invests accordingly.
- Heavy on Order and peace, light on Triumph and Power. Oriented to a stable, calm life; avoids the
  contests the previous profile seeks out.

## Open questions

- Is the list of five complete, and are the remaining motives independent? Control was folded into
  Power and influence, and a validation-and-excellence motive was dropped as orthogonal to
  motivation rather than a motive in its own right. We should still check that Triumph and Power do
  not themselves collapse together.
- Should the profile be a fixed weighting or a ranking, and how is conflict between top motives
  represented?
- How do we keep the coding honest across a population that varies widely in how fluently it can
  articulate what it wants?
