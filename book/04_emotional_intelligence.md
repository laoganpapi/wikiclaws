# Chapter 4. Emotional Intelligence and Social Competence

## The phenomenon

This system governs how a person attends to and acts on emotion, both their own and other
people's. Where the social energy economy is about the fuel and stakes of interaction, and the
thinking machine is about reasoning, this system is about the emotional channel: what a person
notices in the affective field, and how they move when they notice it. It is multidimensional, and
we measure it along two spectra.

A note on the preference line. The word competence implies ability, and some of what this system
describes is skill. As with the thinking machine, we read the system mainly through what a person
*habitually does* rather than the ceiling of what they could do under instruction, but the line is
softer here than in the social or motivational systems, and we flag it.

## The measures

**1. Agreeableness versus Confrontation.** The disposition, in a charged interaction, to
accommodate or to contest. The agreeable pole smooths, yields, and keeps the peace; the
confrontational pole challenges, presses, and is willing to make the interaction uncomfortable in
service of a point or a truth. This is a preference over how a person handles friction, not a
judgment of which pole is healthier; both have their uses, and the assessment reports position,
not virtue.

**2. Introspection versus Situational awareness.** Where a person's emotional attention points: at
their own internal emotional state, or at the emotional field of others and the situation. The
introspective pole reads the self well and may miss the room; the situationally aware pole reads
the room well and may lose track of the self. A person high on both attends fully to the emotional
channel; a person low on both moves through it largely unaware.

This second axis runs close to the Internal-External axis of the thinking machine, and the two
must be kept distinct. The thinking machine's axis is about the *source of reasoning*, theory
versus data, when a person is working out what to think or do. This axis is about the *target of
emotional attention*, self versus others, in a charged moment. A person can reason from external
data (thinking machine, external) while being blind to the feelings in the room (this system, low
situational awareness). Demonstrating that separation is one of the validity tasks we owe a
reviewer.

## The decision rule

In an emotionally charged interaction, behavior follows from where attention points meeting how
friction is handled. A person high in situational awareness and toward the confrontational pole
reads the room precisely and then chooses to press anyway; a person high in introspection and
toward the agreeable pole feels the friction acutely in themselves and moves to dissolve it. Where
stated and revealed preference diverge, the person who believes themselves a peacemaker but
consistently escalates is read by the escalation.

## The dynamic layer

The dynamic worth noting is interaction with stakes and with neuroticism. Emotional attention
tends to narrow under pressure, often collapsing toward the self (introspection) or seizing on a
single feature of the situation. How far and how fast attention narrows under load is partly a
neuroticism story, told in Chapter 6, and partly a competence story told here. The two systems
should be read together when a person is under pressure.

## Illustrations

- High situational awareness, confrontational. Reads the emotional field precisely and uses that
  reading to press hard and effectively. A formidable negotiator; not always a comfortable one.
- High introspection, agreeable. Acutely aware of their own discomfort with friction and quick to
  smooth it; may yield positions they did not need to yield.
- Low on both. Moves through charged situations without much registration of either self or room;
  not unkind, simply not attending to the emotional channel.

## Open questions

- Are two axes enough, or does the system need a third for the *regulation* of emotion once
  perceived, distinct from attention to it?
- How cleanly does Introspection versus Situational awareness separate from the thinking machine's
  Internal-External axis, empirically?
- How much of what looks like low competence is actually neuroticism narrowing attention under
  load, and how do we attribute correctly?
