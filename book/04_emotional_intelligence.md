# Chapter 4. Emotional Intelligence and Social Competence

## The phenomenon

This system governs how a person attends to and acts on emotion, both their own and other
people's, and how steady that emotional system is under load. Where the social energy economy is
about the fuel and stakes of interaction, and the thinking machine is about reasoning, this system
is the emotional channel: what a person notices in the affective field, how they move when they
notice it, and how much their emotional baseline is disturbed when the pressure comes on. It is
multidimensional, and we measure it along three things: how emotional attention is pointed, how
friction is handled, and how stable the system is.

Where the thinking machine measures **cognitive sourcing**, the raw material of reasoning, this
system measures **emotional and social attention**, where feeling and the reading of others is
pointed. The two are deliberately named apart because their internal-versus-external flavors run
close and must be kept distinct.

A note on the preference line. The word competence implies ability, and some of what this system
describes is skill. As with the thinking machine, we read the system mainly through what a person
*habitually does* rather than the ceiling of what they could do under instruction. The stability
measures below are read through revealed reactivity, not self-report, since people are unreliable
narrators of their own steadiness.

## The measures

**1. Agreeableness versus Confrontation.** The disposition, in a charged interaction, to
accommodate or to contest. The agreeable pole smooths, yields, and keeps the peace; the
confrontational pole challenges, presses, and is willing to make the interaction uncomfortable in
service of a point or a truth. This is a preference over how a person handles friction, not a
judgment of which pole is healthier; the assessment reports position, not virtue.

**2. Introspection versus Situational awareness.** Where a person's emotional and social attention
points: at their own internal emotional state, or at the emotional field of others and the
situation. The introspective pole reads the self well and may miss the room; the situationally
aware pole reads the room well and may lose track of the self. A person high on both attends fully
to the emotional channel; a person low on both moves through it largely unaware.

This axis runs close to the thinking machine's Internal-External cognitive sourcing, and the two
must be kept distinct. Cognitive sourcing is about the *source of reasoning*, theory versus data,
when a person works out what to think or do. This axis is about the *target of emotional
attention*, self versus others, in a charged moment. A person can reason from external data and
still be blind to the feelings in the room. Demonstrating that separation is one of the validity
tasks we owe a reviewer.

**3. Emotional stability: baseline and reactivity under load.** How steady a person's emotional
system is, and how that steadiness changes under pressure. We read it as two related quantities. The
first is baseline: even and steady at one pole, easily and strongly moved at the other, when
nothing in particular is wrong. The second, and the more consequential, is reactivity under load:
how far and how fast the baseline degrades as the stakes of a situation climb. Two people with the
same calm baseline can diverge sharply under stress; one holds, the other spikes.

We brought emotional stability inside this system rather than standing it up on its own because it
belongs to the same channel. Attention, handling, and stability are three faces of how a person
meets emotion. The reason stability earns a place beside the other two is its reach into the rest
of the framework, described next.

## The decision rule

In an emotionally charged interaction, behavior follows from where attention points meeting how
friction is handled, with stability setting how much the whole apparatus is disturbed while it
runs. A person high in situational awareness and toward the confrontational pole reads the room
precisely and presses anyway; a person high in introspection and toward the agreeable pole feels
the friction acutely and moves to dissolve it. A reactive person's attention and handling are
pulled off their usual settings as stakes rise; a stable person's are not. Where stated and revealed
preference diverge, the person who believes themselves a peacemaker but consistently escalates is
read by the escalation.

## The dynamic layer

The most important content here is a cross-system claim, and it is why stability lives in this
chapter rather than apart. **Reactivity under load is the moderator of the thinking machine's stake
response.** When a person's two thinking-machine graphs swing steeply as stakes rise, how steep
that swing is is partly a stability story told here. A reactive person's reliance migrates harder
and faster under pressure; a stable person's graphs stay flatter. The same holds within this
system: emotional attention tends to narrow under load, often collapsing toward the self, and the
rate of narrowing is a stability story. For this reason the assessment reads stability not only on
its own scenarios but also through the steepness of the stake responses observed elsewhere. This is
the system most entangled with the others, by design, and that entanglement is exactly what a
reviewer should examine when testing whether the systems are genuinely separable.

## Illustrations

- High situational awareness, confrontational, stable under load. Reads the emotional field
  precisely, presses hard, and is not pulled off that setting when the stakes climb. A formidable
  negotiator.
- High introspection, agreeable, reactive under load. Acutely aware of their own discomfort with
  friction, quick to smooth it, and increasingly so as pressure rises; may yield positions they did
  not need to yield, and yield them faster the more that is at stake.
- Low attention on both axes, stable baseline. Moves through charged situations without much
  registration of self or room, and without much disturbance either; not unkind, simply not
  attending to the emotional channel.

## Open questions

- Are three measures the right cut, or does attention need to split from regulation, the noticing
  of emotion from the managing of it once noticed?
- How cleanly does Introspection versus Situational awareness separate from the thinking machine's
  Internal-External cognitive sourcing, empirically?
- How much of stability should be measured directly, and how much inferred from the steepness of
  the other systems' stake responses?
- Given a young population, how do we distinguish trait reactivity from the ordinary volatility of a
  developmental stage, so that a reading is not mistaken for a verdict?
