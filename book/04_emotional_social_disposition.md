# Chapter 4. Emotional and Social Disposition

## The phenomenon

This system measures a person's stated or revealed preferences and tendencies in emotionally and
socially charged situations. It is deliberately not a measure of emotional intelligence or social
competence. We do not score how skilled a person is at reading a room, naming a feeling, or
managing emotion; skill is ability, and ability is not what this theory measures. We measure what a
person prefers and tends to do when emotion and other people are in play: where they point their
attention, how they handle friction, and how steady they stay under load.

This keeps the system on the same footing as the rest of the framework. The earlier framing, which
spoke of competence, quietly measured ability and broke the preference line; this framing restores
it. A person who reads emotion poorly but consistently moves to smooth conflict has a clear
disposition here, and that disposition, not their skill, is what we record.

Where the thinking machine measures **cognitive sourcing**, the raw material of reasoning, this
system measures **emotional and social attention and disposition**, what a person tends toward when
feeling and other people are present. The two are deliberately named apart because their
internal-versus-external flavors run close and must be kept distinct.

## The measures

Each measure records a preference or tendency, read where possible through what a person actually
does rather than what they say they do.

**1. Agreeableness versus Confrontation.** The tendency, in a charged interaction, to accommodate
or to contest. The agreeable pole smooths, yields, and keeps the peace; the confrontational pole
challenges, presses, and is willing to make the interaction uncomfortable in service of a point or
a truth. This is a preference over how a person handles friction, not a judgment of which pole is
healthier; the assessment reports position, not virtue.

**2. Introspection versus Situational awareness.** Which a person tends to orient by: their own
internal emotional state, or the emotional field of others and the situation. The introspective
tendency moves from the self outward, deciding by how one feels; the situationally aware tendency
moves from the room inward, deciding by what others seem to feel. This records where a person's
emotional and social attention habitually goes, not how accurately they read either target, since
accuracy would be a measure of skill.

This axis runs close to the thinking machine's Internal-External cognitive sourcing, and the two
must be kept distinct. Cognitive sourcing is about the source of reasoning, theory versus data,
when a person works out what to think or do. This axis is about the target of emotional and social
attention, one's own feeling versus the field of others, in a charged moment. A person can reason
from external data and still tend to decide social situations by their own internal feeling.
Demonstrating that separation is one of the validity tasks we owe a reviewer.

**3. Emotional stability: baseline and reactivity under load.** How steady a person's emotional
system tends to be, and how that steadiness changes under pressure. We read it as two related
quantities. The first is baseline: even and steady at one pole, easily and strongly moved at the
other, when nothing in particular is wrong. The second, and the more consequential, is reactivity
under load: how far and how fast the baseline shifts as the stakes of a situation climb. Two
people with the same calm baseline can diverge sharply under stress; one stays near their settings,
the other moves far from them. Neither response is the better one: a large shift is a change of
instruments, not a breakdown, and whether it serves the person is not this framework's question.
This is a tendency, read through revealed reactivity rather than self-report, since people are
unreliable narrators of their own steadiness.

## The decision rule

In an emotionally charged situation, behavior follows from where attention tends to point meeting
how the person tends to handle friction, with stability setting how much the whole apparatus is
disturbed while it runs. A person who tends toward situational awareness and the confrontational
pole reads by the room and presses anyway; a person who tends toward introspection and the
agreeable pole decides by their own discomfort and moves to dissolve it. A reactive person's
tendencies are pulled off their usual settings as stakes rise; a stable person's are not. Where
stated and revealed preference diverge, the person who believes themselves a peacemaker but
consistently escalates is read by the escalation.

## The dynamic layer

The most important content here is a cross-system claim, and it is why stability lives in this
chapter rather than apart. **Reactivity under load is the moderator of the thinking machine's stake
response.** When a person's two thinking-machine graphs swing steeply as stakes rise, how steep
that swing is is partly a stability story told here. A reactive person's reliance migrates harder
and faster under pressure; a stable person's graphs stay flatter. The same holds within this
system: the tendency to attend narrows under load, often collapsing toward the self, and the rate
of narrowing is a stability story. For this reason the assessment reads stability not only on its
own scenarios but also through the steepness of the stake responses observed elsewhere. This is the
system most entangled with the others, by design, and that entanglement is exactly what a reviewer
should examine when testing whether the systems are genuinely separable.

## Illustrations

- Tends toward situational awareness, confrontational, stable under load. Reads social situations
  by the room, presses hard, and is not pulled off that setting when the stakes climb.
- Tends toward introspection, agreeable, reactive under load. Decides charged situations by their
  own discomfort, moves quickly to smooth friction, and increasingly so as pressure rises; may
  yield positions they did not need to yield, and yield them faster the more is at stake.
- Low pull on both attention poles, stable baseline. Moves through charged situations without much
  tendency to orient by self or room, and without much disturbance either; not unkind, simply not
  disposed to attend much to the emotional channel.

## Open questions

- Are three measures the right cut, or does the disposition to attend need to split from the
  disposition to act once attending, the noticing from the responding?
- How cleanly does Introspection versus Situational awareness separate from the thinking machine's
  Internal-External cognitive sourcing, empirically?
- How much of stability should be measured directly, and how much inferred from the steepness of
  the other systems' stake responses?
- Given a young population, how do we distinguish a trait tendency from the ordinary volatility of a
  developmental stage, so that a reading is not mistaken for a verdict?
