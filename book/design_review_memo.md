# Review Memo: Instrument Design for the Four-System Assessment

To: Instrument design authors
Re: Adversarial review of instrument_design.md against 00_architecture.md, metrics_catalog.md, and 03_thinking_machine.md
Findings below were independently verified by skeptic reviewers before inclusion.

## Verdict

Conditional yes. No finding is fatal: the module structure, the two-graph output, the falsifiable core, and the coding governance stack all survive, and every defect below is fixable before the pilot with design amendments rather than architectural surgery. But the instrument as written cannot go to pilot, because the failures concentrate exactly where the framework claims novelty. The stakes axis is not currently a valid manipulation (no choice-contingent payoff, unmeasured per-person point valuation, fixed ascending order, tier-confounded accountability), the fast mode is not currently a validated mode (truncated deliberation, prose-only capture), and the ethics package is not currently adequate for deception research with minors. Fix the stakes cluster, the fast-mode cluster, and the ethics cluster as a precondition; treat the rest as pre-pilot amendments to the protocol and pre-registration.

## Fatal findings

None.

## Major findings

**1. The stake multipliers multiply nothing the decision affects.** Principle 6 forbids scoring for correctness, so no earning rule can make point outcomes ride on which option the respondent picks; Section 1 never specifies an earning rule, and the only choice-contingent consequence is audit exposure at the top tier. An honest economy makes this discoverable: by block two a respondent can verify their choices never move their balance and rationally stop attending to multipliers. Fix: denominate stakes in audit probability across all tiers (certain, 1-in-4, none), or add an incentive-compatible side bet on the respondent's own keep-or-revise behavior; both condition consequences on choices without scoring quality.

**2. Person-specific point valuation is unmeasured, so every slope is identified only up to an unobserved scaling factor.** The Section 2.4 model uses objective stake_tier as the regressor, but the experienced stake is tier times v_i, and points buy a heterogeneous optional bundle the design itself lets respondents opt out of. A flat graph is observationally equivalent to point indifference; the contamination propagates to mode divergence, total stake-sensitivity, S3 reactivity, and manufactures the S1-S2 correlation Section 9.3 tests. Fix: add an early calibration task measuring v_i directly (multiple price list trading points against a fixed non-point good), enter it as a person-level scaling factor, and gate slope reporting on demonstrated stake salience.

**3. Ascending block order confounds stake tier with time-on-task.** Section 1's rising multipliers mean tier is perfectly collinear with fatigue, practice, and rapport with the liaison, so every slope estimate carries an order effect. Fix: randomize or counterbalance tier order across respondents within decision type.

**4. Card prices do not scale with tier, so the sourcing slope is a price artifact.** Card cost is fixed while block stakes rise 1x/5x/25x, so the relative price of external data falls 25-fold along the stakes axis and any point-sensitive respondent rationally buys more cards at high tiers regardless of sourcing preference. This confounds the flagship revealed sourcing indicator with economic rationality and threatens the prespecified ICAR bound. Fix: peg card prices to the block multiplier (1, 5, 25) and verify in pilot that purchase rate is stake-invariant for respondents whose coded sourcing is flat.

**5. Cards have no instrumental value, so willingness to pay measures curiosity and point demand, not sourcing.** Since answers are never scored, an opened card cannot improve any payoff; its only value is wanting to know, which is nearly verbatim the S4 Truth motive, and hoarding for the terminal spend gives points competing option value. Fix: make cards free but scarce (a per-item, non-bankable card budget), or give opened cards instrumental value in the audit defense and control for measured v_i.

**6. One fungible currency across modules manufactures the cross-system correlations the validation plan is supposed to test.** Card purchases (S2), the audit election (S1), and the terminal spend (S4) all draw on one balance, so point demand loads on three systems, and forward-looking hoarding is rational play invisible to the latency-code detectors. Fix: partition into non-fungible sub-currencies with module-local sinks, or add point demand as an explicit latent in the joint model and demonstrate its identification before pilot.

**7. Audit exposure only at the top tier confounds accountability with stakes.** Anticipated defense reliably produces more elaborate, more shelf-citing justifications, so method and sourcing codes are biased toward Formal/External at 25x for impression-management reasons, and the bias differs between audit-electors and exempt respondents. Fix: detach the audit from the stake ladder; apply audit possibility uniformly at low probability across tiers, or run it as a separate post-Arena block.

**8. The audit exemption lets the trait being measured assign the stakes.** Paying to dodge the audit strips the top tier of its defining Exposure property, so 25x slope points are estimated under self-selected heterogeneous stakes, with selection driven by the S1 trait the same election reveals; the exemption price also reveals endogenous wealth, and tier 3 bundles points with social exposure so the stakes axis is not unidimensional. Fix: make tier-3 audit probability universal and undodgeable (disclosed in assent), and measure audit-avoidance willingness separately via a price list honored for one random respondent per cohort.

**9. Announced multipliers make a flat stake-slope scriptable.** Because tier is common knowledge, a respondent can hold shelf spend, tool use, register, and pacing deliberately constant across tiers, and a uniformly calm profile is exactly the legitimate signature of genuine stability, so no detector fires. This fakes the single most flattering output the instrument produces. Fix: randomize announced versus actual audit eligibility so high-stakes items are not identifiable, and compare Arena flatness against involuntary channels (keystroke dynamics, trap-item revision) that are hard to hold constant for 90 minutes.

**10. The 15-second F window elicits truncated deliberation, not fast-mode processing, and the latency check cannot tell.** Reading a multi-option scenario consumes most of the window, leaving deadline-pressured Type-2, and a hard deadline compresses latency whether or not the fast mode was engaged, so the Section 2.5 check verifies enforcement, not mode. Mode divergence then measures degradation under time scarcity, a capacity reading that violates the preference-not-ability axiom. Fix: decouple reading from responding (unlimited read time, 2-5 second response clock at choice onset), add a concurrent-load task in F, and validate the induction against independent Type-1 markers rather than latency compression.

**11. All four fast-mode parameters rest on one coded sentence, the exact Nisbett-Wilson failure case.** Phase F forbids shelf and tools, so fast sourcing and method are identified solely by AI codes of a sub-15-second retrospective one-liner, the canonical condition for confabulated causes, with no behavioral co-indicator; this contradicts principle 1 and principle 3 for half the centerpiece, and the Section 7.4 remedy (redesign toward behavioral capture) has no obvious move inside a phase defined by the absence of behavior. Fix: add a fast-compatible behavioral probe (a forced 5-second binary such as one free peek at a data card versus a principle card under the same deadline), and treat the F rationale as stated-side data until it earns better.

**12. F-phase one-liners are template-gameable and the existing checks reward the gaming.** Two rehearsed personas pasted into "what drove that?" satisfy the compressed-latency check (canned lines are faster than genuine ones) and the population-level F/S difference check (the personas differ by construction), so mode divergence becomes manufacturable. Fix: vary probe wording per item, require scenario-specific span evidence before coding, and flag near-duplicate F rationales via text similarity in the detection-analytics slot.

**13. F-then-S order converts mode divergence into a commitment measure.** Having publicly committed under the point economy, the S rationale becomes a defense of the F choice, and anchoring susceptibility plausibly correlates with deliberation default, so the contamination is differential; the single-phase subset can detect population-level anchoring but cannot correct within-person parameters. Fix: split the battery so half the scenarios are F-only and half S-only, matched within decision type and tier, estimating the two graphs from independent items; retain a smaller two-phase set as a revision-behavior module.

**14. The trap-pair leakage check is one item with no control condition.** Fast agreement with the hidden decisive card occurs at an unknown prior-driven base rate that varies by respondent, so with n=1 trap per battery the check can fire or fail for reasons unrelated to leakage, and it gates a design decision in the section the document calls the load-bearing risk. Fix: counterbalanced trap variants (identical scenario, decisive card pointing opposite directions across randomized halves) and several traps per battery; leakage predicts F answers tracking card direction across variants.

**15. The falsifiable core passes under pure noise.** Mode divergence is a raw distance between noisy per-person vectors estimated from 3 stake points, so expected distance strictly exceeds true distance and grows with error variance; a careless respondent is mechanically scored as two different thinkers, and the stake-tier coding (1/2/3 versus 1/5/25) is unspecified and changes every slope. Fix: replace the raw-distance check with an error-aware contrast (latent difference model, or a permutation null shuffling phase labels within person), disattenuate mode divergence before reporting, and prespecify the tier coding.

**16. Per-person slopes from 3 tiers and roughly 8 items each cannot reach usable reliability.** Eight parameters per respondent from 24 items across 3 x-values, built from noisy signed indicators with error bars that widen on coder disagreement, will produce confidence bands that swallow the trend lines, and low reliability wrecks the disattenuated ICAR comparison exactly where Section 9.2 needs it. Fix: either add stake tiers at the cost of decision types, or demote per-person slopes to a reliability-gated three-category report (rises, flat, falls) and make baselines the v1 headline; simulate expected slope reliability from pilot item variances before promising individual trend lines.

**17. Reactivity is estimated from the same data it moderates and is not separated from stake indifference.** Section 4.3 builds the moderation into the shared parameter rather than testing it, so the framework's headline cross-system claim is unfalsifiable within the instrument, and a leaderboard opt-out plus audit exemption lets one underlying indifference be scored three times (low S1 risk, flat S2 slopes, low S3 reactivity). Fix: estimate reactivity only from indicators outside the Arena stake manipulation (friction-probe recovery, standardized-stressor drift), test the moderation as a free cross-loading, and add a per-person stake-salience check that flags flat profiles as uninterpretable when salience is low.

**18. S2 sourcing and S3 attention target share a coded-prose method with no behavioral tiebreaker on the S3 side.** Both are self-versus-world judgments made by the identical dual-AI stack on the same person's prose, attention target has no behavioral channel at all, and the fluency audit controls writing quality, not referential style; the Section 9.3 discriminant test is therefore confounded in the direction of failure. Fix: add a behavioral attention-target indicator (purchasable probes split between check-your-feeling and read-the-field, mirroring the shelf mechanic) and fit an MTMM model with a coded-prose method factor in pilot.

**19. The retest design has no alternate form and no slope reliability floor.** Retesting the same 24 memorable dilemmas destroys the mode manipulation at T2 (a snap answer to a scenario deliberated on four weeks earlier is a cached slow conclusion), the audit surprise and trap pairs are one-shot, and "slopes allowed lower and reported honestly" sets no falsifiable criterion for the load-bearing metrics. Fix: author parallel Arena forms with new surface content counterbalanced across occasions, prespecify a minimum slope retest correlation with a decision rule (below r = .4, report slopes at cohort level only), and exclude one-shot mechanics from reliability computation.

**20. No consequential-use boundary exists for scores flowing to an evaluating company.** The architecture names this debt as Expected Objection 5 and the design never pays it: nothing restricts who receives scores, which metrics may inform selection, or how long a score stays valid, while the design generates the metrics most likely to be misused (reactivity, stated-revealed gap, motive profile) with criterion validity conceded as exploratory. Fix: add a binding use-policy section with a whitelist (developmental feedback only until criterion validity exists), a prohibition on selection use of reactivity, stability, and gap metrics, respondent-controlled release, an expiry date for minors' scores, and ethics-reader sign-off on the policy itself.

**21. The audit election taxes anxiety and no distress circuit-breaker exists.** For an anxious minor the exemption fee is not a preference measurement but an anxiety tax that depresses the point economy and gets written into S1 risk tolerance, while Sections 2.3 and 4.2 instrument distress signals purely as data with no threshold at which measurement stops and intervention starts. Fix: make exemption free, score the election only as one weak indicator triangulated with SJT and message drafts, and add predefined drift and error thresholds that pause probes, trigger repair, and mark affected items unscoreable rather than as reactivity data.

**22. The protocol runs scripted deception on minors without specific assent, debrief, guardian consent, or a withdrawal path.** The friction and dispute probes are deliberate deception, the blanket assent clause does not disclose staging, and no debrief, parental consent, or mid-assessment withdrawal mechanism appears anywhere, which fails baseline IRB standards for deception research with minors. Fix: guardian consent plus specific assent naming that the AI will sometimes disagree or be confusing on purpose, a mandatory end-of-session debrief naming both probes, a no-penalty withdrawal rule that overrides the point economy, and adult-only gating or removal of the sealed hardest-truths page.

**23. Visibility and competition elections score well-documented gender and cultural norms as social risk tolerance.** Competition entry, self-promotion, and deference to high-status figures show large replicated group differences independent of risk preference; these one-shot elections are not amenable to item-level DIF, and per-cohort stake calibration does nothing about within-cohort gaps, producing a double penalty via the inflated stated-revealed gap. Fix: never score elections alone; require convergence across at least three S1 risk indicators, run group-mean invariance testing on the elections specifically, and pre-register a rule dropping any election whose group gap exceeds a threshold after controlling for other indicators.

**24. Per-metric stated-revealed gaps are unreliable difference scores delivered as identity findings to adolescents.** A gap's reliability is far below either component and no reliability floor gates reporting, yet the report presents per-metric self-knowledge failure, framed by an axiom that the machine's read is the truth, to minors in active identity formation and potentially to an evaluating company. Fix: report gaps for minors only at system level, only above a pre-registered difference-score reliability threshold, framed symmetrically rather than privileging revealed as truth, and exclude gap magnitudes from any third-party report version.

**25. Free tool clicks buy a Formal code and the fluency audit certifies the purchase.** Tool opening is coded with no consistency gate, a vacuous weighting table plus bullet rationale makes the behavioral and text channels corroborate each other in the measurement model, and the fluency audit conditions on the faked behavioral signal, inverting the design's own claim that process tracing is the hard-to-game spine. Fix: score tool engagement rather than opening (does the chosen option win the respondent's own weighting?), require conditional independence between click and text channels before they corroborate, and flag tool-open-plus-incongruent-rationale; this checks consistency, not quality, so principle 6 survives.

**26. The disclosed, guaranteed-mild friction probe scores performed calm as stability.** Forewarned respondents can hold tone, pace, and recovery steady over the measured channels, and joint estimation with a scripted flat Arena profile turns two acts into one confident wrong reactivity estimate with a tight error bar. Fix: weight involuntary residue (error microstructure, keystroke timing variance) over performable channels, treat probe-window behavior as a lower bound on reactivity and say so in the report, and document that this metric is necessarily forewarned for minors.

**27. Module D's motive mapping is legible and consistent faking earns an "Aligned" gap.** The spend options self-label, the tradeoffs pit motives cleanly, and a persona held across 5.1, 5.2, and 5.3 produces a coherent audience-chosen profile whose stated-revealed gap of zero the framework reads as high confidence. Fix: bury motive contrasts in scenario texture with filler dimensions and rotated framings, include pairs whose surface-attractive and motive-coded options diverge, and use inconsistency across disguised framings as the Module D integrity signal.

**28. The 90-minute burden claim is contradicted by the design's own item counts.** Twenty-four two-phase Arena items alone plausibly consume the entire budget, the 12-minute two-day DRM implies a 4-6x speedup over a published method that takes 45-75 minutes for one day, and Modules C and D sit last where fatigue lands; realistic total burden is 2 to 3 hours. Fix: time-budget every module bottom-up by prototyping 3 Arena items and 1 DRM day with real teenagers, then either cut to ~15 Arena items with a reliability recalculation, drop the second DRM day, and move the stated battery to an asynchronous touchpoint, or restate the burden honestly and re-cost recruitment.

**29. n=300 cannot honor the pre-registered fairness and discriminant program.** Eleven correlated equivalence tests against ICAR, disattenuated by a reviewer using the slopes' low reliability, will not hold as written, and DIF by gender, language, and age band on every item leaves focal groups of roughly 20 to 100, below stable-DIF minimums, creating a paper trail of fairness commitments the study cannot keep. Fix: rescope the pre-registration to equivalence tests on baselines only with multiplicity correction and reliability-corrected bounds, declare pilot DIF screening-only, and name a phase-2 sample with per-group n targets now.

**30. Attrition across three sittings is endogenous to the constructs and no partial-data plan exists.** The most aversive events sit late, so the low-risk-appetite, high-reactivity respondents the audit is built to measure are the likeliest to quit before it, biasing surviving-sample metrics, and nothing specifies scoring for a respondent who completes two sittings. Fix: prespecify per-sitting completion targets and over-recruitment (~450 starts for 300 completers), define a degraded-report schema per partial-completion state, move one top-tier block into sitting 2, and pre-register an attrition-versus-early-behavior analysis.

**31. The coding governance stack is costed as a feature but is an operation, with a ~600-1,000 expert-hour calibration set.** The 20 percent expert dual-coded calibration on roughly 60 codable texts per respondent appears nowhere in the budget or build order, the fluency audit is a standing regression pipeline with a mandated redesign loop, and the physio-linguistic drift channel almost certainly cannot carry a signal from ~8 short rationales per tier. Fix: cut calibration to a stratified 10 percent with a power justification, budget expert coding as a line item now, and demote text-based drift from the v1 reactivity model (rest it on the friction-probe recovery curve and stated stability), logging telemetry for offline research.

**32. The honesty rule turns the spend menu into a product commitment and leaves stakes inert for solo respondents.** "Promised consequences always happen" obligates shipping the leaderboard, live audit flow, challenge round, sealed page, and an experimental sixth module built solely as a spend option, while a solo respondent who opts out of visibility faces multipliers that manipulate nothing, a failure correlated with the S1 construct. Fix: cut the v1 spend menu to options deliverable inside the report, defer the challenge round and experimental module, add the stake-salience manipulation check from finding 2, and do not ship the 25x framing until the ladder demonstrably moves behavior in pilot.

## Minor findings

**33. The uniform F window and the fairness battery miss two second-order language gaps.** The 15-second window has no per-person reading-speed calibration, so slower L2 readers get a systematically harsher fast condition, and the Section 9.6 battery never checks error-bar width by language group, so differential precision goes untested. Fix: calibrate the F window against a neutral reading-speed baseline collected at intake, and add report-precision-by-language-group to the fairness battery.

## Dismissed objections worth pre-empting for a real reviewer

- **"Shelf cards can be skim-cited to fake External sourcing for pennies."** Dismissed: the engineered shelf-versus-prior conflict and span-evidenced coding mean a consistent fake requires actually deciding with the data across 24 items, which is the measurement itself. Still add per-card dwell and scroll telemetry to the Section 2.3 capture table; it is cheap and closes the residual skim-cite blind spot.
- **"The catalog mislabels fast-mode metrics as Revealed."** The catalog's Observable-via tag explicitly disclaims method, but a reviewer will read the table before the disclaimer. Footnote the fast-mode rows honestly once finding 11's behavioral probe exists.
- **"There is no way to withdraw at all."** Overstated: repair flows and an ex-ante opt-out exist. But the reviewer will ask the question, so implement and document the no-penalty withdrawal rule from finding 22 rather than arguing the point.
- **"L2 processing cost is scored as emotional reactivity."** Mostly wrong: drift is a within-person contrast across tiers, so a constant processing cost cancels. State that cancellation explicitly in the fairness section so the reviewer does not have to derive it.
- **"The latency manipulation check is fully circular."** Partly wrong: respondents who deliberate to the deadline would show ceiling rather than near-floor latency, so the check has some teeth. Keep it, but present it as a constraint-enforcement check, never as evidence of mode engagement, which is finding 10's job.
