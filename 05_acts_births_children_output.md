# Actus Reus (d) and (e): Measures to Prevent Births and Forcible Transfer of Children

*Evidentiary review through May 25, 2026.*

This sub-record addresses the two Article II elements that, on a pre-existing review of the secondary literature, generally carry the thinnest documented record in the Gaza context: (d) imposing measures intended to prevent births within the group, and (e) forcibly transferring children of the group to another group. The record on (d) has strengthened over 2025, particularly after the UN Commission of Inquiry's March 2025 report on sexual, reproductive, and gender-based violence. The record on (e), as legally defined, remains thin in the Gaza setting. The discussion below maps facts to source framing and does not draw legal conclusions, which are reserved for Agent 9.

## 1. The legal category for (d)

The *Akayesu* Trial Chamber identified as "measures intended to prevent births" examples including sexual mutilation, sterilization, forced birth control, separation of the sexes, and prohibition of marriages (ICTR-96-4-T, Judgment, 2 September 1998, paragraphs 507 to 508). The chamber also recognized that rape can constitute a measure under (d) where it operates to prevent births (e.g. by causing trauma that makes the victim refuse procreation, or by impregnation by perpetrators of another group such that the child does not belong to the mother's group) (Akayesu, paragraphs 507 to 508). The Rome Statute Elements of Crimes for Article 6(d) require that the perpetrator "imposed certain measures upon one or more persons" with intent to prevent births within a protected group. Collateral effects on fertility from generalized war conditions are not (d) absent specific intent directed at preventing births within the group.

## 2. Reproductive healthcare collapse: evidentiary record

The reproductive-health record is among the better-documented sub-records in the Gaza setting. As of May 2026, UNFPA estimated approximately 55,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women at severe risk of death from malnutrition by mid-2026, three times the May 2025 figure of 17,000 (UNFPA, "As famine looms in Gaza," 2025; UNFPA, "Famine confirmed in Gaza," 25 August 2025). UNFPA reported one in three Gaza pregnancies as high-risk and one in five babies as born prematurely or underweight (UNFPA, "Catastrophic birth outcomes in Gaza," 2025).

Health agencies have reported a 300 percent increase in miscarriage rates since October 2023, cited by UNFPA and repeated by HRW in its January 2025 report on pregnant women's rights in Gaza (HRW, *Five Babies in One Incubator*, 28 January 2025). Through the first six months of 2025, UNFPA recorded approximately 17,000 births in Gaza, a 41 percent drop relative to the same period in 2022, with over 2,600 miscarriages and 220 pregnancy-related deaths before delivery; premature births exceeded 1,460 and low-birth-weight babies exceeded 1,600 (UNFPA Situation Report, May/June 2025).

UNFPA and UN Women have documented depletion of sexual and reproductive health supplies. As of mid-2025, around 700,000 women and girls of menstruating age in Gaza were affected by complete depletion of hygiene supplies following the March 2025 blockade tightening (UNFPA, "From natural process to nightmare," June 2025). Contraceptive supply was reported scarce enough that women shared pills, and IUDs could not be removed, producing infections and bleeding (UNFPA, June 2025).

The Independent International Commission of Inquiry (CoI), in its March 13, 2025 report, found that Israeli authorities "destroyed in part the reproductive capacity of the Palestinians in Gaza as a group, including by imposing measures intended to prevent births," citing destruction of maternity facilities, the December 2023 shelling of the Al Basma IVF clinic (with approximately 4,000 embryos reportedly destroyed), and the 41 percent decline in births during the first half of 2025 (CoI, A/HRC/58/CRP.6, 13 March 2025). PHR and the Global Human Rights Clinic, in a January 2026 report based on 78 interviews with international medical volunteers who worked in Gaza between January and October 2025, characterized the pattern as "reproductive violence," documenting damage to obstetric and neonatal infrastructure including the December 2024 demolitions at Kamal Adwan Hospital (PHR, *Destroying Hope for the Future*, January 2026).

The framing across these sources varies. UNFPA, WHO, and UNICEF have generally framed harms as conditions of life and serious harm to maternal and newborn health (joint statement by UNICEF, WHO, UNFPA, UNRWA, 2024). The CoI and PHR explicitly framed the same facts under the (d) "measures" rubric, with the CoI invoking the Genocide Convention and Rome Statute directly (CoI, A/HRC/58/CRP.6, 13 March 2025). The Israeli government disputes the CoI's characterization (covered by Agent 7).

## 3. Sexual violence: evidentiary record

The UN CoI report of March 13, 2025 found that forced public stripping, sexual harassment, threats of rape, and sexual assault formed part of "standard operating procedures" of Israeli security forces in interactions with Palestinian detainees and civilians (CoI, A/HRC/58/CRP.6). Commission chair Navi Pillay characterized the conduct as used "to terrorize" and "perpetuate a system of oppression" (CoI press briefing, 13 March 2025).

The UN Secretary-General's CRSV report (S/2025/389) verified 12 incidents of CRSV perpetrated by Israeli forces against seven Palestinian male detainees, including one rape, one attempted rape, three incidents of squeezing or pulling of genitals, and seven incidents of kicking or beating of genitals, alongside widespread forced public nudity and abusive strip searches. The Sde Teiman detention site has been a particular focus; multiple released detainees allege rape and sexual torture, and indictments brought against five soldiers accused of raping a detainee were subsequently dropped (HRW and PHR-Israel reporting through 2025). PHR-Israel has documented detainee abuse, including sexual violence, on a recurring basis (PHR-Israel, *Reports and Publications*, 2024 to 2025).

Whitelisted sources have not documented allegations of forced sterilization or systematic forced impregnation in Gaza as of May 2026. The (d)-relevant sexual-violence record consists of CRSV against detainees, alongside the reproductive-healthcare destruction in Section 2.

## 4. Element (e): forcible transfer of children

The legal category. The Rome Statute Elements of Crimes for Article 6(e) require that the perpetrator "forcibly transferred one or more persons" who were "under the age of 18 years," from one group to another, with the requisite genocidal intent. The drafting history of the Convention and *Akayesu* tie (e) to the destruction of group identity through the absorption of children into the perpetrator group.

The Gaza record. There is comparatively limited documented evidence in whitelisted sources of forcible transfer of Palestinian children "to another group" in the (e) sense (i.e. an Israeli-state-facilitated absorption pattern, such as adoption or fostering). No whitelisted source reviewed for this brief documents a pattern of Israeli adoption or fostering of Palestinian children from Gaza. Where the (e) wording is invoked by advocacy actors, it is generally not sustained by whitelisted source documentation as of May 2026.

What the whitelisted record does document is adjacent rather than directly within (e):

- UNICEF estimated at least 17,000 children unaccompanied or separated from their families in Gaza by June 2025, and reported 3,291 reunifications and case management for 17,051 children between January and September 2025 (UNICEF, *Stories of loss and grief*, 2024; UNICEF State of Palestine Humanitarian Situation Report No. 43, 30 September 2025).
- DCI-Palestine reported 351 Palestinian children in Israeli detention as of December 31, 2025, of whom 180 (51 percent) were in administrative detention without charge or trial, the highest proportion since DCI-P began tracking in 2008 (DCI-Palestine, *More than half of Palestinian child detainees have no charges*, 2026). DCI-Palestine ceased operations in April 2026 citing Israeli criminalization of Palestinian human rights organizations (DCI-Palestine closure statement, 2026).
- HRW reported approximately 3,544 Palestinians in administrative detention as of October 1, 2025, inclusive of children and adults (HRW, *World Report 2026: Israel and Palestine*).

Child detention is not, in itself, "forcible transfer to another group" within the meaning of (e). Family separation during displacement, similarly, is documented as a child-protection harm rather than as transfer with intent to destroy the group. Agent 9 will perform the legal application. This sub-record reports the source framing rather than asserting the (e) characterization.

## 5. Comparator: October 7 and child hostages

Hamas's October 7 conduct included the abduction of 251 hostages, of whom approximately 38 were children at time of capture (per Israeli government figures relayed via UN Special Representative). The youngest were Kfir Bibas (aged 8 to 9 months) and his brother Ariel (aged 4), both determined by the Israeli military to have been killed in captivity in November 2023; their remains were returned in February 2025 (Israeli government communications to the UN Security Council, S/2025/111; IDF announcements, February 2025). Child hostages, with the exception of the Bibas children and a small number who died in captivity, were largely released in the November 2023 truce.

The first phase of the January 2025 ceasefire produced 33 hostage releases (including women, children, men over 50, and injured civilians) (Britannica, "Israel-Hamas War: Ceasefire and hostage exchange (January–March 2025)"). The body of Ran Gvili, the last hostage remaining in Gaza, was recovered on January 26, 2026, after which no hostages remained held.

The Patten report of March 4, 2024 found "clear and convincing information" that sexual violence including rape, sexualized torture, and cruel and degrading treatment occurred against some women and children during captivity, including an allegation regarding two minors who reported being forced to perform sexual acts on each other (UN SRSG-SVC, *Mission report on official visit to Israel and the occupied West Bank*, 4 March 2024). The Patten team noted that its conclusions did not meet a "beyond reasonable doubt" criminal threshold and called for full investigation.

Some scholars have argued that the hostage-taking of children by Hamas raises (e) considerations as a separate sub-question. The whitelisted record does not document an Israeli-side absorption pattern that would mirror (e) in the Convention's drafting-history sense. The October 7 child-hostage record is documented, hedged where contested, and treated as a parallel evidentiary line.

## 6. Candor on evidentiary thinness

The (d) record has strengthened materially since training cutoff, primarily through the CoI's March 13, 2025 report and the PHR / GHRC January 2026 report, which together frame the destruction of reproductive infrastructure, the documented decline in births, and the obstruction of obstetric care under the "measures intended to prevent births" rubric. UNFPA, WHO, and UNICEF generally framed similar facts under maternal-health and conditions-of-life headings. South Africa's December 29, 2023 application before the ICJ included the destruction of essential health services for pregnant women and babies among the alleged genocidal acts and asked the court to address (d) under Article II (Application of South Africa, paragraphs concerning measures preventing births; ICJ provisional measures order of 26 January 2024 enumerating Article II(d) within its general directive). South Africa's memorial was filed in October 2024; Israel's counter-memorial followed in March 2026.

The (e) record, as legally defined under the Convention and Rome Statute Elements of Crimes, remains thin in the Gaza setting. Adjacent harms (i.e. child detention, family separation, unaccompanied minors, killings of children by attacks documented by Agent 3) are substantial. The specific (e) action element of "forcibly transferring children of the group to another group" is not, as of May 2026, supported by whitelisted source documentation of an Israeli adoption, fostering, or absorption pattern. The October 7 child-hostage record sits in a different category and is treated separately by the UN SRSG-SVC and by the CoI. *South Africa v. Israel* places less weight on (e) than on (a), (b), (c), and (d), consistent with the available record.
