# Determinations Matrix: Formal Positions on Whether Israel's Conduct in Gaza Constitutes Genocide

*Reference catalogue. Cutoff date: May 25, 2026. Direct quotes preserved exactly. No characterization is paraphrased across categories.*

## 1. Position taxonomy (five rows)

Positions are recorded under one of five rows. Collapsing rows would erase legally meaningful distinctions.

a. **"Genocide is occurring / has occurred."** A formal determination, with a named legal basis (the 1948 Convention; Article II acts plus dolus specialis). The actor states that the threshold is met.

b. **"Risk of / plausibly genocidal."** The actor characterizes a risk, a plausibility of rights, or a threshold likely to be crossed, without making a merits determination. The ICJ provisional-measures finding sits here. "Plausible" in the ICJ sense refers to the plausibility of the rights asserted, not a merits finding that genocide has occurred.

c. **"Acts of genocide / genocide-adjacent acts, without a full overall determination."** The actor identifies specific Article II act(s) as committed, but does not (in the same instrument) reach a final overall determination of genocide. Human Rights Watch's December 2024 water report is the cleanest example.

d. **"Serious IHL/IHRL violations short of genocide."** War crimes, crimes against humanity, ethnic cleansing, apartheid, or "atrocity crimes" framings, used without the genocide label. The ICC arrest warrants of November 21, 2024 sit here.

e. **"Rejects the genocide characterization."** Explicit denial that the threshold is met.

## 2. UN bodies

**UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry (Pillay, Sidoti, Kothari).** On September 16, 2025, the Commission released *Legal analysis of the conduct of Israel in Gaza pursuant to the Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide* (A/HRC/60/CRP.3). The press release states: "Israel has committed genocide against Palestinians in the Gaza Strip" (OHCHR press release, "Israel has committed genocide in the Gaza Strip, UN Commission finds," September 16, 2025, ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2025/09). Pillay's accompanying statement: "The Commission finds that Israel is responsible for the commission of genocide in Gaza. It is clear that there is an intent to destroy the Palestinians in Gaza through acts that meet the criteria set forth in the Genocide Convention." The Commission identified four genocidal acts (Article II(a), (b), (c), (d)) and named the President, Prime Minister, and former Defence Minister as having incited genocide. **Position: (a).**

**UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese.** Report *Anatomy of a Genocide* (A/HRC/55/73), March 25, 2024: there are "reasonable grounds to believe that the threshold indicating Israel's commission of genocide is met." Follow-up report *Gaza Genocide: a collective crime* (A/80/492), October 20, 2025, presented to the General Assembly Third Committee on October 28, 2025, frames third-state complicity in "the Gaza genocide" and identifies 63 states. **Position: (a).**

**UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk.** Türk has not himself used the genocide label. October 25, 2024: warning of "atrocity crimes, including potentially extending to crimes against humanity" in northern Gaza (OHCHR, "Türk says world must act," October 25, 2024). May 2025: "the methodical destruction of entire neighborhoods, and the denial of humanitarian assistance underline that there appears to be a push for a permanent demographic shift in Gaza that is in defiance of international law and is tantamount to ethnic cleansing." July 2025: "the need to prevent genocide, in line with the provisional measures of the International Court of Justice." February 2026 statement: "the absence of accountability is nothing short of shameful." In August 2025, more than 500 OHCHR staff signed a letter urging Türk to characterize the conflict as genocide; he declined, citing the role of courts. **Position: (d), with persistent reference to ICJ provisional measures.**

**UN Secretary-General António Guterres.** Position: it is "not within the attributions of the Secretary-General to make the legal determination of genocide," which belongs to judicial entities. He has called the situation in Gaza "one of the darkest chapters" of the conflict, the famine "a man-made disaster, a moral indictment, and a failure of humanity itself" (Statement on Gaza famine, August 22, 2025), and described the Commission of Inquiry's September 2025 report as "a serious report that should be carefully read, particularly by Israeli authorities." **Position: declines to characterize; defers to courts.**

**UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination (CERD).** Decision 2 (2024) under its Early Warning and Urgent Action Procedure; Ad Hoc Conciliation Commission report in *State of Palestine v. Israel* (CERD/C/113/3, August 2024). In December 2024, the Committee expressed "serious concerns about the commission of war crimes, crimes against humanity, and genocide" and recorded "blatant disregard" by Israel for binding international orders. **Position: between (b) and (d), with genocide named as a concern, not a determination.**

**UN General Assembly.** Multiple emergency-session resolutions demand ceasefire and aid (e.g. ES-10/22, June 2025: 149 in favor, 12 against, 19 abstaining). The Assembly has not adopted a resolution making a formal genocide determination. Resolutions reference the ICJ provisional-measures orders. **Position: (d) institutionally, with member-state language in debates varying widely.**

## 3. Courts

**International Court of Justice, *South Africa v. Israel* (Application of the Genocide Convention in the Gaza Strip).**

- *Order of January 26, 2024* (provisional measures): "In the Court's view, the facts and circumstances mentioned above are sufficient to conclude that at least some of the rights claimed by South Africa and for which it is seeking protection are plausible. This is the case with respect to the right of the Palestinians in Gaza to be protected from acts of genocide and related prohibited acts identified in Article III." The Court's plausibility test concerns the rights asserted, not a merits finding that genocide has been committed. The Court ordered Israel to take all measures within its power to prevent the commission of all acts within the scope of Article II.
- *Order of March 28, 2024* and *Order of May 24, 2024*: additional provisional measures, including the order to halt the military offensive in Rafah.
- South Africa's *Memorial* filed October 28, 2024 (over 750 pages plus 4,000 pages of annexes) alleged Israel's actions "are genocidal in character because they are intended to bring about the destruction of a substantial part of the Palestinian national, racial and ethnical group."
- Israel filed its *Counter-Memorial* on March 12, 2026 after two extensions. Oral hearings on the merits are not expected before late 2026 or 2027.

**Position of the ICJ in its orders: (b) — plausibility of rights, not a merits determination.** The Court has not ruled on the merits.

**Article 63 declarations of intervention filed at the ICJ as of May 2026:** Nicaragua (later withdrew April 3, 2025), Colombia, Libya, Mexico, Palestine, Spain, Turkey, Chile, Maldives, Ireland, Bolivia, Belgium (December 23, 2025), Cuba, Paraguay (March 3, 2026 — to support Israel), Netherlands and Iceland (March 11, 2026), Namibia, the United States, Hungary, and Fiji (March 12, 2026; the U.S. and Hungary intervened on the defending side). Intervention under Article 63 is on the interpretation of the Convention and is not equivalent to a state characterization of genocide; some interventions are to support Israel.

**International Criminal Court, Pre-Trial Chamber I, *Situation in the State of Palestine*, November 21, 2024.** Warrants of arrest were issued against Benjamin Netanyahu and Yoav Gallant for "the war crime of starvation as a method of warfare; and the crimes against humanity of murder, persecution, and other inhumane acts," covering "at least 8 October 2023 until at least 20 May 2024." The Chamber found "the lack of food, water, electricity and fuel, and specific medical supplies, created conditions of life calculated to bring about the destruction of part of the civilian population in Gaza." A separate warrant was issued against Mohammed Deif (Hamas). The warrants do **not** charge genocide. The phrasing "conditions of life calculated to bring about the destruction" tracks Article II(c) language; the Office of the Prosecutor and Chamber chose not to charge genocide at that stage. **Position: (d), with Article II(c)-resonant fact-finding.**

**U.S. Federal Court, *Defense for Children International–Palestine v. Biden*, N.D. California, January 31, 2024.** The court found that "the undisputed evidence before this Court comports with the finding of the ICJ" and "the current treatment of the Palestinians in the Gaza Strip" "plausibly constitute[s] a genocide." It dismissed for lack of jurisdiction (political question). **Position: (b), explicit reliance on ICJ plausibility framing.**

## 4. Human rights organizations

**Amnesty International.** *"You Feel Like You Are Subhuman": Israel's Genocide Against Palestinians in Gaza* (MDE 15/8668/2024), December 5, 2024. Conclusion: "Israel has committed and is committing genocide against Palestinians in the Gaza Strip." Amnesty applied the 1948 Convention's mens-rea standard and found three of the five Article II acts proven (a, b, c) and dolus specialis demonstrated through patterns of conduct and senior-official statements. **Position: (a).**

**Human Rights Watch.** *Extermination and Acts of Genocide: Israel Deliberately Depriving Palestinians in Gaza of Water* (179 pages), December 19, 2024. Conclusion: "Israeli authorities are responsible for the crime against humanity of extermination and for acts of genocide." HRW expressly framed this as a finding of "acts of genocide" (Article II(c)) without a finding of overall genocide in that report. Later: "Gaza: Latest Israeli Plan Inches Closer to Extermination" (May 15, 2025) and "Commission of Inquiry Report Highlights Growing Consensus on Israel's Genocidal Gaza Campaign" (September 30, 2025). **Position: (c) as of December 2024; HRW's language has subsequently moved toward (a) but has not been republished as a comprehensive overall determination report in the manner of Amnesty's.**

**B'Tselem.** *Our Genocide* (July 28, 2025): "An examination of Israel's policy in the Gaza Strip and its horrific outcomes, together with statements by senior Israeli politicians and military commanders about the goals of the attack, leads us to the unequivocal conclusion that Israel is taking coordinated action to intentionally destroy Palestinian society in the Gaza Strip." Sits within B'Tselem's existing apartheid framework (2021 report *A regime of Jewish supremacy*). **Position: (a).**

**Physicians for Human Rights–Israel (PHRI).** Companion report to B'Tselem, July 28, 2025: a medical-legal analysis finding the deliberate and systematic destruction of Gaza's healthcare system, concluding Israel is committing genocide in Gaza. **Position: (a).**

**Yesh Din.** Joined Israeli civil-society letters demanding the government comply with ICJ orders; the organization has not produced a stand-alone determination titled "genocide" as of May 2026; positions itself on apartheid and IHL violations. **Position: (d).**

**Adalah.** Has documented Article II acts in submissions to UN bodies; supports South Africa's case. No standalone formal determination report on the question of overall genocide as of May 2026; characterizes Israeli conduct using genocide language in advocacy. **Position: (a), via filings.**

**Diakonia International Humanitarian Law Centre.** Hosts legal notes on ICJ orders; does not itself issue a determination, presenting analyses of the third-state legal consequences of the January 26, 2024 order. **Position: institutional reporter on (b).**

**International Federation for Human Rights (FIDH).** International Board determination, October 2024, after deliberation with member organizations including Al-Haq, Al Mezan, and PCHR: Israel "was carrying out genocide against the Palestinian people in Gaza." **Position: (a).**

**International Commission of Jurists (ICJ-Geneva).** January 26, 2024 statement: "the world's highest judicial authority has acknowledged that there is a risk of genocide being committed in Gaza." Subsequent statements on third-state duties to prevent genocide. The ICJ-Geneva has tracked the case without itself issuing an organizational determination that the threshold is met. **Position: (b), with strong duty-to-prevent advocacy.**

**Center for Constitutional Rights (CCR).** Filed *DCIP v. Biden* (November 2023); litigation built on the premise of an "unfolding crime of genocide." CCR's institutional position: "Israel's unfolding crime of genocide of the Palestinian people." **Position: (a).**

**Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF).** Statement (2024): characterized the situation in Gaza as bearing the hallmarks of ethnic cleansing and used the phrase "signs of ethnic cleansing" before adopting genocide language. By 2025, MSF officials have used "genocide" in public communications. **Position: shift from (d) to (a) over 2024–2025.**

**Al-Haq, Al Mezan, Palestinian Centre for Human Rights (PCHR).** Joint and individual statements throughout 2023–2026 characterizing Israeli conduct as genocide and providing evidentiary submissions to South Africa's ICJ filing and the ICC. **Position: (a).**

## 5. Specialist scholarly bodies

**International Association of Genocide Scholars (IAGS).** Resolution adopted August 31, 2025 (final text dated August 2025): "Israel's policies and actions in Gaza fulfil the legal definition of genocide" under the 1948 Convention. Result: 86% of voting members in favor (of 28% who voted; 108 of approximately 500 members). The resolution states the conduct "constitute war crimes and crimes against humanity as defined in international humanitarian law and the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court," and further meets the genocide definition. **Position: (a).** Several hundred scholars subsequently signed an open letter calling for retraction (September 2025).

**Lemkin Institute for Genocide Prevention.** Statement of November–December 2023, updated through 2025: "Statement on Why We Call the Israeli Attack on Gaza Genocide." Issued an Active Genocide Alert and SOS Alerts (e.g., Rafah, March 2024); welcomed the September 2025 CoI report in "No More Excuses." **Position: (a).**

**Journal of Genocide Research.** Editor-in-chief A. Dirk Moses and a series of articles and symposia (including the December 2025 "Forensic and Counter-Forensic Approaches to International Law" symposium on Opinio Juris/JGR-affiliated platforms) have engaged the question. Moses has stated the field would be "in crisis" if scholars defended Israeli conduct against the genocide framing. **Position: (a) institutionally for many contributors; the journal as such does not issue a determination.**

## 6. Named scholars

**Omer Bartov (Brown University).** New York Times essay *I'm a Genocide Scholar. I Know It When I See It* (July 15, 2025): "My inescapable conclusion has become that Israel is committing genocide against the Palestinian people." Earlier New York Times essay (August 2024) called the campaign genocidal in pattern but stopped short of full determination; the July 2025 essay marks the explicit shift. **Position: (a).**

**Raz Segal (Stockton University).** Jewish Currents, October 13, 2023: "a textbook case of genocide unfolding in front of our eyes." **Position: (a).**

**Martin Shaw (Sussex/IBEI).** "Inescapably Genocidal" (2024) and *The New Age of Genocide* (Agenda/Columbia, 2025): Israel's bombardment "represented a strategic choice" with genocidal consequences. **Position: (a).**

**Amos Goldberg (Hebrew University).** Multiple essays (Jacobin, Local Call, *Tel Aviv Review*, 2024–2025) including "Yes, it is genocide": Israel is "undoubtedly" committing genocide in Gaza. **Position: (a).**

**Lee Mordechai (Hebrew University).** *Bearing Witness to the Israel-Gaza War*, 124-page documentation report first published March 15, 2024, continuously updated through 2025–2026 at witnessing-the-gaza-war.com. Mordechai characterizes the conduct as genocide and frames the project as a contemporaneous record of dissent. **Position: (a).**

**Melanie O'Brien (IAGS President).** "Applying the legal definitions of genocide as found in the Genocide Convention and the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court, I would determine that what is happening in Gaza constitutes genocide." **Position: (a).**

**A. Dirk Moses (CCNY).** *Journal of Genocide Research* editorials; argues the artificial separation between genocide and "military targets" needs to be combated; describes the conduct using genocide framing while writing on the conceptual history of the term. **Position: (a).**

**John Cox.** IAGS-affiliated scholar; signatory and contributor to the IAGS resolution process; supports the genocide framing. **Position: (a).**

**William Schabas (Middlesex; former UN Commission of Inquiry chair on Gaza 2014).** 2025 interview with Middle East Eye and analysis for the Zoryan Institute: "clearly such a case of genocide," and the South Africa case is "arguably the strongest case of genocide that has ever come before the Court." On complicity: third states "know what Israel is doing" and "close their eyes." **Position: (a).**

**Philippe Sands (UCL).** Counsel for Palestine in ICJ advisory proceedings. On the genocide concept, Sands has stated in the *Reading Lemkin Today* lecture (Harvard HRJ, 2025) that the prevailing judicial definition of genocidal intent "sets the bar far too high," indicating discomfort with the Convention's narrow construction. He has not in public commentary issued a personal formal determination that Israel is committing genocide using the Convention threshold; rather, he has questioned the threshold itself. **Position: ambiguous; closer to (b) given his ICJ-counsel posture, with reservation about the Convention bar.**

**Kevin Jon Heller (Copenhagen; Doughty Street Chambers).** Repeated analyses on Opinio Juris through 2024–2025; on the ICC arrest warrants, on Israel's jurisdictional challenges, and on the question of belligerent intent. Heller has expressed analytic skepticism about whether dolus specialis is demonstrable on the available record while accepting the strength of the IHL and crimes-against-humanity findings. **Position: between (b) and (d); analytic, with reservations on intent proof.**

**Payam Akhavan (Toronto/Massey College).** Counsel in *The Gambia v. Myanmar*; has commented widely on the Convention. As of May 2026, no public statement of personal formal determination on Gaza identified in the sources reviewed. **Position: no formal determination identified as of May 25, 2026.**

**Geoffrey Nice KC.** Lead prosecutor of Milošević. Public commentary has been measured, framing Israeli conduct as raising serious genocide concerns and noting that Israeli official statements may rise to incitement; has criticized "permanent buffer zone" plans as "unjustified, by any view, under international law." **Position: (b), with concern for (a).**

**John Quigley (Ohio State).** Author of *The Genocide Convention: An International Law Analysis* (2006). In commentary on South Africa's case has supported the strength of the genocide claim and the applicability of Article II in Gaza. **Position: (a).**

**Eyal Weizman (Forensic Architecture, Goldsmiths).** *Ungrounding: The Architecture of Genocide* (Fern Press/Penguin, May 2026). Forensic Architecture has produced evidence submitted in the South Africa case, including counter-analysis of Israel's claimed "humanitarian evacuations." **Position: (a).**

**Itamar Mann (Haifa).** Verfassungsblog series including "Almost Genocide: Accountability and the Emergence of Intent Over Time" and "Counter-Genocidal Governance." Argues genocidal intent emerges gradually; describes the regime of ICJ oversight as "counter-genocidal governance." **Position: (b)→(a) over time; analytic, with emphasis on intent's emergence.**

**Aslı Bâli (Yale).** Yale "Future of War Crimes Prosecution" panel and *The Nation* podcast (January 2024): "We have seen a real time genocide unfold." **Position: (a).**

**Noura Erakat (Rutgers).** Democracy Now! interview (October 13, 2023) and Boston Review essay: Israel's military response is a "genocidal campaign," framed by apartheid and settler colonialism. **Position: (a).**

**Neve Gordon (Queen Mary) and Nicola Perugini (Edinburgh).** Multiple co-authored pieces: "A Legal Justification for Genocide," "Medicide in Gaza and International Law" (Institute for Palestine Studies). Gordon's "Shields and the Genocide in Gaza" (2024). Israel uses "the law itself as a tool legitimizing genocide." **Position: (a).**

**Shmuel Lederman, Daniel Blatman, Yair Auron (Israeli genocide-studies scholars).** Multiple Israeli scholars have publicly called the conduct genocide over 2024–2025 (Auron earliest among them). **Position: (a).**

**Counter-positions among scholars.** A number of scholars have publicly disputed the genocide characterization, including those who signed letters calling for the IAGS resolution to be retracted (September 2025) and writers in *The Atlantic*, *Times of Israel*, and AJC analytic pieces (e.g., the AJC "Israel Is Not Committing Genocide" position paper). Named scholars in this category include Norman J. W. Goda and Jeffrey Herf (writing against Segal in late 2023), and Sara Brown (criticizing IAGS process and outcome). **Position: (e).**

## 7. States — formal positions

**Position (a) — formal genocide characterization or ICJ applicant.**

- **South Africa.** Applicant in *South Africa v. Israel*, December 29, 2023; alleged Israel's acts are "genocidal in character … to destroy Palestinians in Gaza as a part of the broader Palestinian national, racial and ethnical group" (Application, paras. 1–2; UN GA statement, December 12, 2023).
- **Nicaragua.** Initially intervened in support; *Nicaragua v. Germany* before the ICJ (March 2024) alleged Germany's arms transfers facilitate "plausible genocide." Withdrew Article 63 declaration April 3, 2025.
- **Colombia, Libya, Mexico, Spain, Turkey, Chile, Maldives, Ireland, Bolivia, Belgium, Cuba.** Filed Article 63 declarations of intervention supporting the applicant side at various points 2024–2025. Several have made parallel ministerial statements characterizing conduct as genocide:
  - **Bolivia:** characterized Gaza as "genocide" upon filing intervention October 2024.
  - **Turkey:** President Erdoğan has repeatedly stated Israel is committing genocide; Turkey severed trade ties May 2024.
  - **Malaysia, Iran, Venezuela, Cuba, Pakistan, Indonesia, Bangladesh, Maldives, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Guyana, Belize.** Heads of state or foreign ministries have used "genocide" formally in 2024–2025 statements.
- **Ireland.** President Higgins (October 2024): "the exclusion [from the United Nations] of those who are practising genocide, and those who are supporting genocide with armaments." Ireland filed an Article 63 declaration of intervention January 6, 2025.
- **Spain.** Filed an Article 63 declaration of intervention; the Sánchez government has publicly used "genocide" through 2025; arms-exports suspension since October 2023.
- **Brazil.** President Lula da Silva, February 2024: "What's happening in the Gaza Strip with the Palestinian people hasn't existed at any other moment in history. Actually, it has: when Hitler decided to kill the Jews." (Statement at AU Summit, Addis Ababa.) Joined the ICJ case as intervenor in 2024.
- **Colombia.** President Petro: declared genocide; severed diplomatic ties May 2024.

**Position (d) — serious violations / ethnic cleansing / atrocity crimes, short of formal genocide determination.**

- **Norway.** Has used "war crimes" and called for ICJ compliance; recognized Palestine May 28, 2024; foreign-minister statements characterize the conduct as risking genocide without formal determination.
- **Slovenia.** Recognized Palestine June 2024; imposed full arms embargo on Israel (first EU state to do so, 2025); foreign-ministry statements have approached but not formally adopted the genocide label.
- **Belgium.** Filed Article 63 declaration December 23, 2025 supporting South Africa, which constitutes intervention rather than a standalone formal determination by the executive; ministers had characterized the conduct as genocide-adjacent.

**Position (e) — rejects genocide characterization.**

- **United States.** Biden administration (Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin, March 2024): "no evidence" of genocide; State Department spokesperson on the September 2025 CoI report: "Israel went to war in self-defense and has consistently taken steps to minimize civilian casualties. That is not a genocide." Trump administration (2025–2026): formal rejection continues; the U.S. filed an Article 63 declaration of intervention on March 12, 2026 in support of Israel.
- **United Kingdom.** Successive governments have declined to characterize Israeli conduct as genocide; in May 2025 the UK, France, and Canada jointly declared the continued aid blockade a "violation of international humanitarian law" and threatened sanctions but stopped short of "genocide."
- **Germany.** January 2024 government statement called the South Africa application "baseless" and a "political instrumentalization" of the Convention; spokesperson Hebestreit: Israel is "defending itself" after "inhuman" attacks. Germany later withdrew its planned Article 63 intervention supporting Israel after *Nicaragua v. Germany* was filed. As of March 2026, Germany has not characterized the conduct as genocide.
- **France.** No formal genocide determination; joined UK/Canada May 2025 IHL-violation statement.
- **Italy.** No formal genocide determination; partial restrictions on arms exports; opposed UN GA ceasefire resolutions at various points.
- **Canada.** No formal genocide determination; joined the May 2025 UK/France joint statement.
- **Australia.** No formal genocide determination; sanctioned ministers Ben-Gvir and Smotrich in June 2025.
- **Netherlands.** Filed Article 63 declaration of intervention on March 11, 2026 in support of Israel.
- **Hungary, Paraguay, Fiji, Argentina.** Filed Article 63 declarations of intervention in March 2026 on the defending side, or have voted against UN GA ceasefire resolutions.
- **Japan.** Has not characterized the conduct as genocide; continues to call for IHL compliance.

**Regional bloc positions.**

- **Arab League.** August 2025 statement: characterized Israel's Gaza occupation plan as a "genocidal threat to entire Arab world." Joint OIC/Arab League/AU statement, December 16, 2025: welcomed the September 16, 2025 CoI report which "affirmed that Israel committed genocide against the Palestinian people."
- **Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC).** 51st Council of Foreign Ministers (June 2025) and joint statement (December 2025): formal genocide characterization.
- **African Union.** Joint statement with AL and OIC (December 2025) endorsing the CoI finding. The AU has issued statements characterizing the conduct as genocide since 2024.
- **European Union (institutional).** No formal EU genocide determination; the EU has issued resolutions on famine, hostages, two-state solution, IHL violations; European Parliament motions through 2025 (e.g. B10-0372/2025) called for action without adopting a genocide label institutionally.
- **ASEAN.** No formal regional determination.
