## Your job

Test every legal claim against controlling jurisprudence and authoritative scholarship. Flag misstatements, oversimplifications, missing controlling precedent, conflated standards, and erroneous applications.

Doctrinal checkpoints (not exhaustive):

1. **Convention Article II text.** Quoted accurately, including the dolus specialis clause "with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group, as such"?
2. **Akayesu standard for Article II(b).** Serious bodily or mental harm need not be permanent or irremediable, but must "go beyond minor or temporary impairment" (Akayesu ¶¶502–504, ¶731). Does the report apply this correctly?
3. **Krstić for "in part" / "substantial part."** ICTY Appeals Chamber held that destruction of a substantial part (geographic, qualitative, emblematic) satisfies the threshold (*Krstić*, Appeals Chamber, April 2004, ¶¶8–14). Does the report apply this correctly?
4. **Bosnia ¶373 "only reasonable inference."** "The dolus specialis... has to be supported by facts that... could only point to the existence of such intent" (ICJ, *Bosnia v. Serbia*, 26 Feb 2007, ¶373). Is this quoted correctly? Applied correctly?
5. **Croatia ¶¶148, 417, 437–441.** The "alternative reasonable inferences" doctrine. The alternative must be reasonable, not merely conceivable. Does the report apply this correctly?
6. **Standard of proof at merits.** "Fully conclusive" (*Bosnia* ¶209). Is this characterised correctly? Specifically, is it characterised as higher than beyond-reasonable-doubt or as equivalent?
7. **Plausibility-vs-merits distinction.** The plausibility standard at provisional measures is procedural (*Gambia v. Myanmar* provisional measures order, *Bosnia v. Serbia* provisional measures order). The Court has been explicit. Does the report ever blur this?
8. **Purpose vs. knowledge based intent.** The Greenawalt knowledge-based reading vs. the ICJ purpose-based formulation. The ICJ has not adopted the knowledge-based reading (Schabas commentary; Ambos commentary). Does the report represent this debate accurately?
9. **Protected group.** "National, ethnical, racial or religious" — the ICJ in *South Africa v. Israel* accepted Palestinians as a protected group (Order, 26 Jan 2024, ¶45). Does the report characterise this finding correctly?
10. **Attribution.** State responsibility under the ILC Articles (Arts. 4–11). Is the standard for attributing organ conduct correctly stated?
11. **ICC vs. ICJ standards.** Individual criminal responsibility (Article 6 Rome Statute) vs. state responsibility (Article IX Convention). Different standards of proof, different elements, different procedural postures. Does the report conflate at any point?
12. **ICC Elements of Crimes "manifest pattern."** Article 6(a)–(e) of the Elements of Crimes requires conduct to take place "in the context of a manifest pattern of similar conduct directed against that group or was conduct that could itself effect such destruction." Whether this imports a substantive contextual requirement is disputed (Schabas, Ambos). Does the report register this?
13. **Subjective vs. objective protected-group identification.** Akayesu objective vs. Krstić/Rutaganda subjective trend. Does the report acknowledge?
14. **"Calculated to bring about" in Article II(c).** What does "calculated" mean doctrinally? Is the report's interpretation consistent with Krstić and Karadzić jurisprudence?
15. **"Forcibly transferring children" in Article II(e).** Drafting history and *Akayesu* tie this to absorption-into-perpetrator-group with intent to destroy the source group's identity. Does the report apply this correctly?
16. **State responsibility for failure to prevent.** Article I of the Convention (duty to prevent). Distinct doctrinal track. Does the report register that South Africa's application includes failure-to-prevent claims?
17. **Complicity.** Article III(e) — complicity in genocide. The Albanese October 2025 report names 63 complicit states. Does the synthesis correctly distinguish state-as-perpetrator from state-as-complicit?
18. **Order of magnitude reasoning.** Bosnia distinguishes the legal threshold from the empirical scale; *Krstić* established that 7,000–8,000 victims at Srebrenica can satisfy "in part." Does the report avoid the lay-reader misimpression that scale alone determines the legal question?
