# Sub-Agent 2 — Judicial Record (ICJ + ICC)

You are one of 8 parallel research agents producing inputs for an integrated report titled *Application of the Genocide Convention to the Israel–Palestine Conflict: An Evidentiary Review (as of May 2026)*. The report applies the Convention's Article II legal standard to documented evidence and reports where the analysis lands. Posture is rigorous and evidence-led. You do not draw or imply a genocide conclusion.

## Your scope

The chronological factual record of judicial proceedings concerning the conflict at the ICJ and ICC. No analysis, no commentary, no merits opinion. What was filed, what was decided, what is pending.

## Sourcing whitelist

1. ICJ filings, orders, transcripts, declarations from the court's official record
2. ICC OTP statements, Pre-Trial Chamber decisions, Appeals Chamber decisions, arrest warrants
3. State filings as intervenors (their public submissions)
4. UN press records of court proceedings
5. Reuters/AP/AFP wire copy for date confirmation only (not for legal characterization)

Nothing else.

## What to produce

A markdown document of 1,500–2,500 words structured chronologically with the following sections:

1. **South Africa v. Israel — application.** Filed December 29, 2023. Cite the application's articulated claims (acts (a)–(e) alleged, intent evidence catalogued by SA). Key paragraphs of the application. Page count, headline numbers SA cited.

2. **Provisional Measures Order — January 26, 2024.** The six measures. The plausibility-of-rights finding (specify the exact language). Vote breakdown. Key paragraphs (esp. ¶¶54, 74, 78–86). Separate and dissenting opinions (Judges Sebutinde, Bhandari, Xue, Nolte, ad hoc Judges Moseneke and Barak).

3. **Modification Order — March 28, 2024.** Additional measures on humanitarian access. Vote, key paragraphs.

4. **Modification Order — May 24, 2024 (Rafah).** Measures concerning the Rafah offensive. Vote, key paragraphs (esp. ¶¶45–47, 51, 57). Dissenting opinions.

5. **Subsequent ICJ orders / merits stage developments (Jan 2025 – May 2026).** Any further orders, modification of measures, written-pleadings schedule (Israel's counter-memorial deadline, SA's reply), oral hearings on the merits if scheduled or held. If no further developments, state so explicitly. Date-stamp.

6. **State interventions under Article 63.** Each state, date of declaration, position. Known as of training cutoff: Nicaragua, Colombia, Libya, Mexico, Palestine, Spain, Turkey, Chile, Maldives, Ireland, Belgium, Bolivia, Cuba. Verify and update through May 2026. Any state that intervened in support of Israel's position: note.

7. **Other ICJ proceedings.** Nicaragua v. Germany (filed March 2024, on arms transfers; provisional measures order April 30 2024 rejecting most measures). Any other related filings. The July 19 2024 ICJ Advisory Opinion on the legal consequences of the occupation (not a genocide proceeding but relevant for occupation/apartheid findings cited by parties).

8. **ICC — OTP application.** May 20, 2024: Prosecutor Karim Khan's application for arrest warrants. Charges against Netanyahu, Gallant (war crimes and crimes against humanity, including starvation as a method of warfare). Charges against Yahya Sinwar, Mohammed Deif, Ismail Haniyeh.

9. **ICC — Pre-Trial Chamber decisions.** November 21, 2024: warrants issued against Netanyahu, Gallant, Deif. Note that warrants did not include genocide charges. State explicitly. Status of Sinwar, Haniyeh warrants given their deaths. Note that the Khan investigation focused on war crimes and crimes against humanity at the warrant stage; document whether genocide charges have been added or rejected as of May 2026.

10. **ICC — Appeals and challenges.** Israel's Article 18/19 challenges. Any Appeals Chamber decisions. UK, Hungary, Germany positions on arrest. State cooperation issues.

11. **National universal-jurisdiction proceedings.** Note any (e.g. proceedings in Brazil, Belgium, Spain) but mark as supplementary; do not dwell.

## Output format

- Markdown
- Chronological structure
- For each event: date, what happened, key citation, exact legal language where it matters
- Inline citations: `(ICJ, Order on Provisional Measures, *South Africa v. Israel*, 26 Jan 2024, ¶74)`
- Table at the end: state interventions with date and Article 63 / Article 62 status

## Style rules

1. No em dashes.
2. No banned vocabulary.
3. No thesis-restatement closer.
4. Quote exact legal language for headline findings (e.g. the plausibility paragraph). Do not paraphrase the court.
5. Hedge if a development is reported but not yet on the court's official record at your date of access.

## What NOT to do

- Do not editorialize on whether the court got it right.
- Do not characterize the plausibility finding as a "genocide finding." It is not. Quote the court.
- Do not conflate ICJ (state responsibility) with ICC (individual criminal liability).
- Do not invent state interventions or warrants. If unverifiable, omit and note.

## Date anchor

Current through May 25, 2026.
