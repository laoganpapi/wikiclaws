# Judicial Record: ICJ and ICC Proceedings Concerning the Israel–Palestine Conflict (December 2023 to May 2026)

*Chronological factual record. No analysis. No merits opinion.*

---

## 1. South Africa v. Israel: Application (29 December 2023)

On 29 December 2023 the Republic of South Africa filed an Application instituting proceedings against the State of Israel before the International Court of Justice, together with a request for provisional measures, under Article IX of the Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide (ICJ Press Release, *South Africa institutes proceedings against Israel*, 29 Dec 2023). The Application runs to 84 paragraphs.

South Africa pleaded that Israel's acts and omissions in relation to Palestinians in Gaza are "genocidal in character" and committed with "the requisite specific intent" to bring about the destruction of the Palestinian group in Gaza, "that being the part of the Palestinian group in the Gaza Strip" (Application, 29 Dec 2023, ¶1, ¶15). The Application catalogued allegations corresponding to Article II acts (a) through (e): killing of Palestinians (II(a)); serious bodily and mental harm (II(b)); inflicting conditions of life calculated to bring about physical destruction in whole or in part, including through siege and denial of food, water, medicine, fuel and shelter (II(c)); measures intended to prevent births (II(d)); and forcible transfer of children (II(e)) (Application, ¶¶111–127).

For intent evidence the Application catalogued statements by senior Israeli officials, including the Prime Minister, the President, the Minister of Defence, the Minister of Energy, the Minister of National Security, and military officers (Application, ¶¶101–107). South Africa requested nine provisional measures (Application, ¶144).

## 2. Provisional Measures Order (26 January 2024)

After public hearings on 11 and 12 January 2024, the Court delivered its Order on 26 January 2024.

On prima facie jurisdiction the Court found a dispute existed between the parties (ICJ, Order on Provisional Measures, *South Africa v. Israel*, 26 Jan 2024, ¶¶27–31). On standing the Court accepted South Africa's invocation of erga omnes partes obligations (¶33).

On the plausibility of rights, the Court held at paragraph 54:

> "the Court considers that at least some of the rights claimed by South Africa and for which it is seeking protection are plausible. This is the case with respect to the right of the Palestinians in Gaza to be protected from acts of genocide and related prohibited acts identified in Article III, and the right of South Africa to seek Israel's compliance with the latter's obligations under the Convention."
(ICJ, Order, 26 Jan 2024, ¶54)

This is a finding on the plausibility of the *rights asserted*, not a finding of genocide. On risk of irreparable prejudice the Court found at paragraph 74 that there is "a real and imminent risk that irreparable prejudice will be caused to the rights found by the Court to be plausible, before it gives its final decision" (ICJ, Order, 26 Jan 2024, ¶74).

The Court indicated six provisional measures in the dispositif (¶86). Israel was ordered to: (i) take all measures within its power to prevent the commission of any acts within the scope of Article II (15 to 2; Sebutinde and ad hoc Judge Barak against); (ii) ensure with immediate effect that its military does not commit such acts (15 to 2); (iii) take all measures within its power to prevent and punish direct and public incitement to commit genocide (16 to 1; Sebutinde against); (iv) take immediate and effective measures to enable the provision of urgently needed basic services and humanitarian assistance (16 to 1; Sebutinde against); (v) take effective measures to prevent the destruction and ensure the preservation of evidence (15 to 2); (vi) submit a report to the Court within one month (15 to 2) (ICJ, Order, 26 Jan 2024, ¶86).

Separate or declaratory opinions were appended by Judges Xue, Bhandari, Nolte, and ad hoc Judge Moseneke. Vice-President Sebutinde and Judge ad hoc Barak appended dissenting opinions. Judge Barak voted in favour of measures (iii) and (iv) and against the remainder.

## 3. Modification Order (28 March 2024)

Following a request by South Africa of 6 March 2024, the Court issued a new Order on 28 March 2024 modifying the January measures. The Court observed that "Palestinians in Gaza are no longer facing only a risk of famine ... but that famine is setting in" (ICJ, Order, *South Africa v. Israel*, 28 Mar 2024, ¶21).

The Court reaffirmed the measures of 26 January 2024 and added new measures, including that Israel "take all necessary and effective measures to ensure, without delay, in full co-operation with the United Nations, the unhindered provision at scale by all concerned of urgently needed basic services and humanitarian assistance, including food, water, electricity, fuel, shelter, clothing, hygiene and sanitation requirements, as well as medical supplies and medical care" (ICJ, Order, 28 Mar 2024, ¶51). New measures passed by votes of 15 to 1 with Judge Sebutinde dissenting.

## 4. Modification Order (24 May 2024, Rafah)

On a further request by South Africa filed 10 May 2024, the Court issued a third Order on 24 May 2024 directed to the Rafah offensive. The Court considered the "developments in the military offensive in Rafah" and the "humanitarian situation" to be "exceptionally grave" (ICJ, Order, *South Africa v. Israel*, 24 May 2024, ¶¶45–47).

The dispositif at paragraph 57 indicated, in relevant part, that the State of Israel shall:

> "(a) Immediately halt its military offensive, and any other action in the Rafah Governorate, which may inflict on the Palestinian group in Gaza conditions of life that could bring about its physical destruction in whole or in part;
> (b) Maintain open the Rafah crossing for unhindered provision at scale of urgently needed basic services and humanitarian assistance;
> (c) Take effective measures to ensure the unimpeded access to the Gaza Strip of any commission of inquiry, fact-finding mission or other investigative body mandated by competent organs of the United Nations to investigate allegations of genocide."
(ICJ, Order, 24 May 2024, ¶57)

Measures (a), (b) and (c) passed 13 to 2 with Vice-President Sebutinde and Judge ad hoc Barak dissenting. Dissenting opinions were appended by Sebutinde and Barak. Separate or declaratory opinions were appended by Judges Nolte, Iwasawa, Tladi, Cleveland, and Aurescu.

## 5. Merits Stage Developments (October 2024 to May 2026)

By Order of 5 April 2024 the Court fixed deadlines for the Memorial of South Africa and the Counter-Memorial of Israel. On 28 October 2024 South Africa filed its Memorial; reporting indicates over 750 pages of text with roughly 4,000 pages of annexes (DIRCO statement, 28 Oct 2024).

Israel's Counter-Memorial was originally due 28 July 2025. The Court granted an extension to 12 January 2026, then a further extension by Order of 20 October 2025 to 12 March 2026. South Africa stated publicly that Israel filed its Counter-Memorial on or about 12 March 2026 (DIRCO statement, March 2026).

As of 25 May 2026, the schedule for South Africa's Reply and Israel's Rejoinder has not been publicly announced, and oral hearings on the merits have not been scheduled. The ICJ Report for the year ending 31 July 2025 lists the case among pending proceedings (A/80/4, Aug 2025). No further provisional measures order has issued since 24 May 2024.

## 6. State Interventions Under Article 63

Article 63 of the Statute permits any State party to a convention whose construction is in question to intervene. See the Annex Table for filing dates (each citation: ICJ Press Release for the corresponding state). The United States and Hungary filed Article 63 declarations on 12 March 2026; public reporting indicates these declarations contest aspects of South Africa's articulation of Convention obligations. No state has been admitted as intervening in support of Israel as co-respondent under Article 62. Admissibility rulings on the Article 63 declarations are pending or partial as of 25 May 2026.

## 7. Other ICJ Proceedings Relevant to the Conflict

**Nicaragua v. Germany** (case No. 193). Nicaragua filed its application on 1 March 2024 alleging breaches by Germany of obligations under the Genocide Convention, the Geneva Conventions, and customary international law in connection with arms transfers to Israel and the suspension of UNRWA funding (ICJ Press Release, 1 Mar 2024). Hearings on provisional measures were held on 8 and 9 April 2024. By Order of 30 April 2024 the Court found, by 15 votes to 1, that "the circumstances, as they now present themselves to the Court, are not such as to require the exercise of its power under Article 41 of the Statute to indicate provisional measures" (ICJ, Order, *Nicaragua v. Germany*, 30 Apr 2024, dispositif). The Court did not dismiss the case. In November 2025 Germany filed preliminary objections. The case remains pending as of May 2026.

**Advisory Opinion of 19 July 2024.** Following a 2022 UN General Assembly request, the Court delivered its Advisory Opinion on *Legal Consequences arising from the Policies and Practices of Israel in the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including East Jerusalem* (ICJ, Advisory Opinion, 19 Jul 2024). The Court found, by 11 votes to 4, that the continued presence of Israel in the Occupied Palestinian Territory is unlawful and must end as rapidly as possible (Advisory Opinion, 19 Jul 2024, dispositif ¶285(3)). It found violations of Article 3 of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (¶229) and obligations on third states not to render aid or assistance maintaining the unlawful situation (¶279). The Opinion is not a genocide proceeding; parties to *South Africa v. Israel* have cited it on the underlying findings on occupation and discriminatory practices.

## 8. ICC: Office of the Prosecutor Application (20 May 2024)

On 20 May 2024 ICC Prosecutor Karim A.A. Khan KC announced applications to Pre-Trial Chamber I for warrants of arrest in the Situation in the State of Palestine. The Prosecutor sought warrants against Benjamin Netanyahu (Prime Minister of Israel) and Yoav Gallant (then Minister of Defence), on charges of war crimes and crimes against humanity, including the war crime of starvation of civilians as a method of warfare under Article 8(2)(b)(xxv) of the Rome Statute, and the crimes against humanity of murder, persecution, extermination and other inhumane acts (OTP Statement, 20 May 2024).

The Prosecutor also sought warrants against Yahya Sinwar (Head of Hamas in Gaza), Mohammed Diab Ibrahim Al-Masri ("Mohammed Deif," al-Qassam Brigades Commander-in-Chief), and Ismail Haniyeh (Head of the Hamas Political Bureau), on charges including extermination, murder, hostage-taking, and rape and other acts of sexual violence as crimes against humanity and war crimes (OTP Statement, 20 May 2024). The applications did not include charges of genocide.

## 9. ICC: Pre-Trial Chamber Decisions

On 21 November 2024 Pre-Trial Chamber I issued warrants of arrest against Benjamin Netanyahu, Yoav Gallant, and Mohammed Deif (ICC Press Release, 21 Nov 2024). The Chamber found "reasonable grounds to believe that Mr Netanyahu ... and Mr Gallant ... bear criminal responsibility for the war crime of starvation as a method of warfare; and the crimes against humanity of murder, persecution, and other inhumane acts" (ICC Press Release, 21 Nov 2024). The Chamber further found "reasonable grounds to believe that Mr Netanyahu and Mr Gallant bear criminal responsibility as civilian superiors for the war crime of intentionally directing an attack against the civilian population" (ICC Press Release, 21 Nov 2024). The temporal scope was "from at least 8 October 2023 until at least 20 May 2024."

The warrants do not include charges of genocide. The Chamber's findings rest on Articles 7 and 8 of the Rome Statute; Article 6 (genocide) was not invoked at the warrant stage. The Prosecutor's investigation, on the public record as of 25 May 2026, has not added charges under Article 6.

The applications against Sinwar, Haniyeh and Deif were affected by their deaths. Haniyeh was killed on 31 July 2024 and Sinwar on 16 October 2024; the OTP withdrew the warrant applications against them prior to the November 2024 decision. The warrant issued against Deif was terminated on 27 February 2025 after the OTP confirmed his death (ICC Press Release, 27 Feb 2025).

## 10. ICC: Appeals and Challenges

Israel filed challenges under Articles 18(1) and 19(2) of the Rome Statute. On 21 November 2024 Pre-Trial Chamber I rejected the Article 19(2) jurisdictional challenge as premature and rejected Israel's request for a fresh Article 18(1) notification.

By Judgment of 24 April 2025 the Appeals Chamber dismissed by majority Israel's Article 18(1) appeal as inadmissible (ICC Appeals Chamber, Judgment ICC-01/18-422 OA, 24 Apr 2025), and found error of law in the Pre-Trial Chamber's handling of the Article 19(2)(c) challenge, reversing and remanding. The Appeals Chamber confirmed that the warrants remained in force.

On 9 May 2025 Pre-Trial Chamber I issued procedural directions on remand. On 21 May 2025 the Chamber confirmed there was "no basis to withdraw or vacate the warrants" issued on 21 November 2024 (ICC, Pre-Trial Chamber I, Decision, ICC-01/18, 21 May 2025). On 17 October 2025 the Chamber issued a further decision on the remanded jurisdictional challenge, finding that Israel's submissions largely repeated arguments addressed in the prior analysis (ICC, Pre-Trial Chamber I, Decision, ICC-01/18, 17 Oct 2025). The warrants remain in force as of 25 May 2026.

State cooperation. In February 2025 the second Trump administration issued Executive Order 14203 sanctioning ICC personnel involved in the Palestine situation. Hungary announced non-cooperation and hosted Prime Minister Netanyahu in April 2025; the United Kingdom and Germany have indicated they would meet treaty obligations without explicit enforcement commitments; the Netherlands, Ireland, Belgium, Spain and South Africa have publicly affirmed the obligation to execute the warrants (national statements 2024–2025).

## 11. National Universal-Jurisdiction Proceedings (Supplementary)

National proceedings have been initiated in several jurisdictions. Brazilian federal authorities have been petitioned to open inquiries against Israeli soldiers transiting Brazilian territory. Belgian federal prosecutors have received complaints under Belgium's universal jurisdiction framework. Spanish complaints have been lodged before the Audiencia Nacional. None has produced an arrest or substantive judicial determination on genocide as of 25 May 2026.
