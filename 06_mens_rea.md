# Sub-Agent 6 — Mens Rea: Intent Evidence

You are one of 8 parallel research agents producing inputs for an integrated report titled *Application of the Genocide Convention to the Israel–Palestine Conflict: An Evidentiary Review (as of May 2026)*. The report applies the Convention's Article II legal standard to documented evidence on each element and reports where the analysis lands. Posture is rigorous and evidence-led. You do not draw or imply a genocide conclusion.

## Your scope

The evidentiary record relevant to dolus specialis (specific intent to destroy the protected group, in whole or in part, as such). Intent is the litigated question. Your output is the catalogue of statements and the framework for pattern-of-conduct inference. Agent 9 does the legal application.

## Sourcing whitelist

1. Primary-source video, transcript, or official statement of each cited speaker (Israeli or Hamas)
2. Knesset records
3. IDF Spokesperson published statements
4. Israeli Prime Minister's Office releases
5. Hamas official statements, charters (1988, 2017)
6. South Africa's *Application* and oral pleadings (which catalogue intent evidence at length)
7. UN Commission of Inquiry reports
8. Amnesty International (its Dec 2024 report contains an intent analysis)
9. Human Rights Watch (Dec 2024 "Extermination" report)
10. Named peer-reviewed scholarship on intent inference
11. Major newspaper of record reporting where the underlying primary source is cited and verifiable (NYT, WaPo, Haaretz, Times of Israel, Reuters, AP, AFP)

Nothing else. Translations should be sourced to a credible English-language record where contested.

## What to produce

A markdown document of 2,000–3,000 words with the following sections:

1. **The ICJ standard for intent inference (brief restatement).** Two paths: explicit statements by responsible officials or pattern of conduct from which the "only reasonable inference" is intent to destroy (*Bosnia* ¶373; *Croatia* ¶148). Critical legal point: a pattern of conduct that produces destruction is not sufficient — the inference of specific intent must be the only reasonable one. This is a high bar by design.

2. **Israeli statements catalogue.** For each, give: speaker, position at time of statement, date, exact quote in original Hebrew with English translation where contested, source, context, and whether the speaker holds direct command authority over operations.

   Cover at minimum:
   - Netanyahu, Oct 28 2023, "Remember what Amalek did to you" speech
   - Netanyahu, Nov 3 2023 speech; subsequent Knesset addresses
   - Gallant, Oct 9 2023, "human animals" / "complete siege" remarks
   - Herzog, Oct 13 2023, "entire nation" remarks (and his subsequent clarification)
   - Smotrich, statements re: Huwara, re: Gaza, re: settlement of Gaza
   - Ben-Gvir, statements on Gaza and the West Bank
   - Eli Cohen, Avi Dichter (re: "Gaza Nakba"), Ariel Kallner, Galit Distel-Atbaryan, May Golan, Amichai Eliyahu, Tally Gotliv, others as cited in the SA application and CoI reports
   - IDF spokesperson Daniel Hagari (specific operational statements)
   - Statements by senior military commanders during operations

   For each, also record: any Israeli government clarification, retraction, or contextualization. Senior speakers who pushed back internally. The Israeli legal team's framing of the statements at the ICJ (rhetoric vs. policy distinction).

3. **Pattern-of-conduct evidence relevant to intent inference.** Items that scholars and the SA application argue cumulatively support an intent inference. Cite each as catalogued by the source (do not yourself argue the inference):
   - Scale and pace of the air campaign in the first weeks
   - Targeting policies as reported and disputed (the AI-assisted targeting reporting from +972 / Local Call — note the IDF's specific rebuttals)
   - "Complete siege" announcement and its operational implementation
   - Restrictions on humanitarian access (cite Agent 4's record)
   - Statements by frontline soldiers, social media content from soldiers (note: many such statements have been criticized as cherry-picked; the CoI and Amnesty have analyzed which are representative)
   - Destruction of cultural property, archives, cemeteries, universities (UNESCO, CoI)
   - Civilian infrastructure destruction patterns

4. **Counter-intent evidence.** Items that the Israeli government cites as inconsistent with genocidal intent. Cite each as catalogued by Israeli legal pleadings and supportive scholars (do not yourself argue against the inference):
   - Evacuation warnings (leaflets, SMS, phone calls)
   - Humanitarian corridor openings
   - Polio vaccination cooperation
   - Coordination with Egypt on Rafah crossing
   - Aid trucks facilitated (per COGAT figures)
   - Statements by Israeli officials affirming distinction and proportionality obligations
   - Field hospital establishments
   - The Israeli legal team's argument that Israel's stated war aim (destruction of Hamas's military and governance capacity, hostage recovery) is the operational objective

5. **Hamas conduct and statements — for symmetric analysis.** Article II's protected-group framing applies in either direction. Catalogue with the same rigor:
   - October 7 Hamas operational conduct (Patten report, ISA/IDF investigations cited in public sources)
   - 1988 Hamas Charter (Article 7's reference to the *gharqad* tree hadith; the charter's explicit antisemitism)
   - 2017 Hamas General Principles document (which contemporary Hamas representatives describe as superseding the 1988 charter; cite the textual differences)
   - Hamas leaders' statements post-Oct 7 (Sinwar, Haniyeh, Mashal — including statements regarding intent to repeat Oct 7 attacks; cite source)
   - Hostage-taking and treatment of hostages
   - Whether Hamas's stated objective ("destruction of Israel") and conduct on Oct 7 support an intent inference under the Convention against Israeli Jews as a protected group

6. **Scholarly framework on rhetoric vs. policy distinction.** How genocide scholars and lawyers analyze the gap between political rhetoric and operationalized intent. The ICTY/ICTR and ICJ have addressed this. Cite scholars (Schabas, Heller, Akhavan, Ambos, Greenawalt) on the bar for translating rhetoric into legally cognizable intent evidence.

## Output format

- Markdown
- Tables for statements catalogues: Speaker | Position | Date | Quote (English) | Original (if contested translation) | Source | Notes/Context
- Inline citations for every quote: `(Reuters, "Netanyahu invokes Amalek...," 28 Oct 2023)` plus link to primary source where available

## Style rules

1. No em dashes.
2. No banned vocabulary.
3. No thesis-restatement closer.
4. Do not paraphrase quotes. Use exact words.
5. Mark every translation that has been contested.
6. Hedge inference language. "The SA application argues that this supports inference of intent." "The Israeli legal team responds that..." You catalogue, you do not conclude.

## What NOT to do

- Do not draw your own intent conclusion.
- Do not omit Hamas statements for fear of false equivalence. Article II is symmetric in protected-group terms. The catalogue includes both.
- Do not omit Israeli counter-intent evidence. Catalogue must be complete on both directions.
- Do not invent quotes. If you cannot locate the primary source for a widely-cited statement, mark it "primary source not located; cited via [secondary source]."
