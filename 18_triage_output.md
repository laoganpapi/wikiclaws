## 2. Conflicts Requiring Human Adjudication

### C1. The "two reasonable inferences" framing in §5(d)

- **Red Team 11 (pro-Israel) position.** The synthesis's §5(d) "two competing readings" architecture is structurally correct under *Croatia* ¶148. The cumulative actus reus density should not migrate into the intent inference. RT11 H3 recommends foregrounding the doctrinal separation more starkly.
- **Red Team 12 (pro-Palestine) position.** The "two competing readings" framing reverses the *Croatia* standard. RT12 HIGH-2 argues that *Croatia* ¶148 requires the genocidal inference be the only *reasonable* inference, not merely that any conceivable alternative defeats it; on the documented record (siege, IPC Phase 5, 81% structure damage, IVF clinic, 22 of 38 universities), the counterterrorism inference is conceivable but not reasonable.
- **Stake.** If the synthesis adopts RT11's framing, the report tilts toward the high-bar reading (the doctrinal architecture is preserved; alternative inferences need only be reasonable, and serious bodies hold them so). If it adopts RT12's framing, the report tilts toward concluding that the alternative inference is unreasonable on the documented record and that the bodies finding genocide are correctly applying the standard.
- **Triage recommendation.** *Croatia* ¶148 requires the inference of *dolus specialis* be "the only inference that could reasonably be drawn from the acts in question." *Croatia* ¶¶437–441 found that forced displacement was a "reasonable" alternative inference on the Croatia record; the test is reasonableness, not bare conceivability. RT12's doctrinal point is correct: a merely conceivable alternative does not defeat the inference. RT11's structural point is also correct: the actus reus density does not, by its weight alone, satisfy *dolus specialis*. Both are doctrinally true. The synthesis as currently written meets RT11's standard but does not meet RT12's because §5(d) closes by characterising the disagreement as "real, well-articulated on both sides, and turns on a legal question that the ICJ has explicitly reserved for the merits," which silently treats reservation-for-merits as if it adjudicated reasonableness. Recommended framing: retain the two-readings architecture but add an explicit paragraph stating that the *Croatia* test is whether the alternative inference is reasonable, that the bodies finding genocide have concluded the counterterrorism inference is not reasonable on the cumulative record, that the bodies rejecting the characterisation have concluded it remains reasonable, and that this is the substantive disagreement, not a procedural one. The reservation-for-merits clause is true but does not bear on which view is doctrinally correct; it bears only on who has authority to adjudicate.
- **Awaiting human adjudication: NO.** Doctrinally clean. Synthesis to revise per the recommendation above. Tracked as H5.

### H3. Article I duty to prevent and Article III(e) complicity collapsed into commission question

- **Raised by:** RT14 (H1, H2). Adjacent to RT12 (MEDIUM-5, MEDIUM-6).
- **Severity rationale:** The synthesis collapses three analytically distinct State-responsibility tracks under the Convention (commission, duty to prevent, complicity) into an undifferentiated "is it genocide" question. *Bosnia v. Serbia* ¶¶425–438 (duty to prevent) and ¶¶419–424 (complicity) are controlling on those tracks and are not cited. The duty to prevent is a "distinct obligation, separate from the duty to punish" (*Bosnia* ¶427) triggered by "serious risk" (¶431) under a "due diligence" standard (¶430). Serbia was found in breach of the duty to prevent in *Bosnia* notwithstanding acquittal on commission (¶438, dispositif (5)). The Albanese "63 complicit states" framing under Article III(e) requires the *Bosnia* ¶¶419–424 knowledge standard. Failure to register these tracks is doctrinally consequential.
- **Routing:** synthesis (§§2, 7) and brief (§2).
- **Location:** 09 §2 (legal standard), §7(b) (what is contested); 10 §2.
- **Current text (synthesis §2):** Recites Article II actus reus and *dolus specialis*; protected group; "in part" / substantial part; standard of proof. Does not register Article I as separately actionable.
- **Proposed change:** Insert a new paragraph in §2 after the standard-of-proof paragraph: "The Convention engages three analytically distinct State-responsibility tracks. The first is the obligation not to commit genocide under Article I read with Article II, attributed via the customary rules in the ILC Articles (Arts. 4 to 11). The second is the duty to prevent under Article I, which the Court has held to be 'a distinct obligation, separate from the duty to punish' (*Bosnia v. Serbia*, 26 February 2007, ¶427), arising 'at the instant that the State learns of, or should normally have learned of, the existence of a serious risk that genocide will be committed' (¶431), with breach assessed on a 'due diligence' standard (¶430). Serbia was found in breach of the duty to prevent in respect of Srebrenica notwithstanding acquittal on commission (¶438). The third is the obligation not to be complicit under Article III(e), which the Court applied under a knowledge standard rather than a specific-intent standard (¶¶419 to 424); 'at the least, the organs of the accomplice State knew' of the principal's genocidal intent (¶422). The three tracks are distinct in their elements; breach of any one supports State responsibility under Article IX." Similar paragraph in brief §2.
- **Rationale:** Brings the synthesis into alignment with controlling ICJ doctrine on State responsibility under the Convention. Permits the Albanese "63 complicit states" framing to be doctrinally assessed (H6). Permits the South Africa Application's duty-to-prevent claims to be registered.
- **Citation/support:** *Bosnia v. Serbia*, Judgment of 26 February 2007, ¶¶419–424 (complicity), ¶¶425–438 (duty to prevent). Schabas, *Genocide in International Law*, 2nd ed. 2009, ch. 9.

The convergence pattern is informative.

- **Purpose-vs-knowledge intent debate** (M10) is raised by RT14 and RT17. Agent 01 engaged it; the synthesis dropped it.

**Divergence (opposite-direction critiques requiring doctrinal adjudication):**
